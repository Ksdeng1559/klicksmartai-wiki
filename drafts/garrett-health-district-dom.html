Section 01Investment Thesis

Garrett Health District Residences is a purpose-built rental housing opportunity anchored by proximity to the largest hospital redevelopment in Fraser Health — and positioned at the intersection of three converging policy tailwinds: BC's Transit-Oriented Development legislation, the Townhouse and Infill Housing Accelerator Programs, and the national Affordable Housing Fund.

The site sits 550 metres from Sapperton SkyTrain Station and 400 metres from the Royal Columbian Hospital's new Jim Pattison Acute Care Tower, which opened May 31, 2026. Phase 2 and 3 of the RCH redevelopment are estimated at $2 billion — representing the most significant healthcare infrastructure investment in the Fraser Valley.

This is not a generic apartment proposal. It is a workforce housing project for the 2,000+ nurses, physicians, allied health workers, and medical technicians who will staff the expanded hospital. That framing — not available to most infill sites — aligns the project simultaneously with provincial housing policy, municipal housing objectives, and community livability goals.

Key Finding: The Path to Viability

Pure market rental at current construction costs and cap rates produces tight residual economics — a common finding across Metro Vancouver purpose-built rental sites in 2025–2026. The project reaches viability through:

MechanismImpact
CMHC MLI Select financingReduces construction financing rate by ~100–150 bps; increases feasibility
BC Housing co-investmentReduces effective capital requirement; may improve cap rate
DCL partial or full waiverSaves $500,000–$800,000+ in municipal DCLs
5% affordability set-aside premiumQualifies for expedited processing; Council support
Healthcare employer partnershipRCH or Fraser Health partnership anchors demand; reduces vacancy risk

Healthcare Worker Housing Demand

Royal Columbian Hospital is the largest hospital in Fraser Health and one of the busiest in BC:

  • Catchment population: 3.8 million
  • Total staff (current): ~2,000+ FTE
  • RCH Phase 2–3 expansion: ~$2 billion additional investment
  • New tower opened: May 31, 2026 (Jim Pattison Acute Care Tower)
  • Staff housing challenge: Healthcare workers consistently rank commute time and housing affordability as top retention factors
Implication: A purpose-built rental project targeting this cohort has near-zero vacancy risk if rents are set at or near market rates. A waitlist is realistic at opening.

Section 07Capital Structure Options

Two structures are presented — landowner preference will determine the final arrangement.

Option A — Assemble and Sell

Landowner Preferred if Simplicity Desired

  • Landowners assemble 3 parcels
  • Land sold to developer at $4,000,000–$5,500,000 (assembled value)
  • Developer assumes all rezoning, financing, and construction risk
  • Landowner proceeds: Clean exit; no ongoing involvement; capture assembly premium over individual lot values
  • Timeline to proceeds: 6–12 months from signed agreement to closing

Option B — Landowner Partnership Landowner Recommended

Landowner Recommended if Upside Desired

  • Landowners contribute assembled land at appraised value (~$5,000,000)
  • Developer contributes all construction capital (~$25,000,000)
  • Profit split: 70% Developer / 30% Landowner Partnership
  • Landowner return: ~$6,500,000–$7,500,000 total (land value + profit share)
  • vs. Option A: Incremental $1,500,000–$2,000,000 in upside
  • Structuring vehicle: Development LP or Development Agreement with completion guarantees
  • Dennis's role: Landowner Representative — negotiates and monitors on behalf of landowners
