01 — Investment Thesis

Not an Apartment Building.
A Healthcare Workforce Community.

Garrett Health District Residences sits at the intersection of three converging policy tailwinds: BC's Transit-Oriented Development legislation, the Townhouse and Infill Housing Accelerator Programs, and the national Affordable Housing Fund — anchored by proximity to the largest hospital redevelopment in Fraser Health.

The site is 400 metres from the Royal Columbian Hospital's new Jim Pattison Acute Care Tower (opened May 31, 2026) and 550 metres from Sapperton SkyTrain Station. Phase 2–3 of the RCH expansion is valued at $2 billion. This creates a structural, non-cyclical demand driver for workforce housing that no generic infill site can replicate.

🏥

RCH Adjacency

2,000+ hospital workers needing housing within walking distance. Nurses, residents, physicians, allied health, and support staff form a captive tenant base with guaranteed employment and transit dependency.

🚇

TOD Tier 3

Confirmed within 800m of Sapperton SkyTrain Station. BC's Mandatory Density Framework entitles the site to 3.0 FSR / 8 storeys — Council must consider this density on rezoning.

📋

Policy Alignment

Directly advances New Westminster's OCP housing targets, BC's healthcare workforce housing strategy, and federal CMHC MLI Select eligibility — making approval politically defensible.

05 — Capital Structure

Two Paths to Liquidity

Option A — Simplicity

Assemble & Sell

Landowners assemble all three parcels and sell the assembled site to a developer at market value. Developer assumes all rezoning, financing, and construction risk.

Combined sale price$4.0M–$5.5M
Timeline to proceeds6–12 months
Ongoing involvementNone
Upside captureAssembly premium only
Best for: landowners who want a clean exit with no development exposure.
Option B — Maximum Return

Landowner Partnership

Landowners contribute assembled land at appraised value. Developer contributes all construction capital. Profit split: 70% Developer / 30% Landowner Partnership.

Land value (at inception)~$5,000,000
Profit share (30%)~$1,500,000–$2,000,000
Total landowner return~$6.5M–$7.5M
vs. Option A upside+$1.5M–$2.0M
Best for: sophisticated landowners with a trusted representative who want to retain upside.
Dennis's role in Option B: Landowner Representative — negotiates LP or Development Agreement terms, reviews developer pro-forma for optimistic assumptions, monitors performance, and protects landowner interests throughout the project lifecycle.
06 — Next Steps

Getting to a Deal

01

Expression of Interest

Share this DOM with 2–3 active New Westminster / Fraser Valley developers. Target firms with CMHC MLI Select experience and purpose-built rental track record.

02

Data Room Access

Provide full data package: title searches, surveys, independent appraisal, planning consultant brief, and RLV model to qualified developers.

03

Pre-Application Meeting

Engage a planning consultant and request a pre-application meeting with New Westminster Planning Dept. Brief the healthcare housing narrative before any public process.

04

LOI / Option Agreement

Negotiate and execute a Letter of Intent or Option Agreement. Defines price, profit share (if Option B), conditions precedent, and exclusivity period.

05

Rezoning Application

Developer submits rezoning application. No public hearing required (Bill 44 — ≥50% residential). Estimated 18–24 months to permit issuance.

06

Land Closing

Land closes on receipt of Council approval (third reading or final adoption, per agreement). Landowners receive land value + any profit participation per agreement terms.
