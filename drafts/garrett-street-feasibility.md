## Section 2 — Planning Feasibility

### Current Zoning

Both properties are zoned **RS (Single Detached Residential)** or equivalent under New Westminster Zoning Bylaw 6680. This is the lowest-density residential designation and is a **permitted use for single detached dwellings only**. Any multi-unit development requires rezoning.

### OCP Designation

Properties in Sapperton are designated **"Urban Residential"** or **"Sapperton Mixed Use"** under the OCP, depending on the exact parcel. OCP designation supports increased density near transit but does not grant by-right development rights.

### TOA (Transit-Oriented Area) Designation

Both properties fall within **800 metres of Sapperton Station** — confirmed by the City's TOD mapping. This places them in **TOD Tier 3** under BC's Minimum Allowable Density (MD) Framework:

| Tier | Distance from Station | Max FSR | Max Height |
|------|----------------------|---------|-----------|
| Tier 1 | ≤200m | 5.0 | 20 storeys |
| Tier 2 | 201–400m | 4.0 | 12 storeys |
| **Tier 3** | **401–800m** | **3.0** | **8 storeys** |

**Critical caveat:** The MD Framework sets maximums that municipalities *must consider*, not minimums they must approve. Rezoning is still required. Council retains full discretion to refuse.

### Density

| Scenario | Current (as-of-right) | Potential (with rezoning) |
|----------|-----------------------|--------------------------|
| Single lot | 1 unit (RS zoning) | Up to 6 units (SSMUH) / up to 3.0 FSR (TOD) |
| Two-lot assembly | 2 units | Up to 6 units by-right (if infill program passes) / up to 3.0 FSR (8 storeys) with rezoning |

### Rental Requirements

- **5% affordability set-aside** required for buildings over 4 storeys in New Westminster (Family Friendly Housing Policy)
- **Rental tenure incentives** available (reduced parking requirements, expedited processing)
- **No mandatory rent control** beyond provincial Residential Tenancy Act framework

### Approval Process

Standard New Westminster rezoning process:
1. Pre-application meeting with Planning Dept (604-527-4532)
2. Submit rezoning application ($ fees)
3. Staff technical review and report to Council
4. **Public hearings prohibited** for projects ≥50% residential (BC Bill 44)
5. Council first/second/third reading
6. Adoption

Timeline: **12–24 months** for most rezonings in TOD areas.

### Planning Feasibility Rating: **MEDIUM**

Strong TOD location, active infill legislation, and supportive RCH adjacency are counterbalanced by the need for rezoning, the Townhouse Accelerator Program's incomplete legislative journey, and Council's retained discretion.

## Section 6B — Capital Formation: Landowner Partnership

*This section responds to landowner interest in retaining upside participation rather than selling outright.*

### Why a Landowner Partnership Beats a Land Sale

A straight land sale captures the assembled land value today. A Landowner Partnership captures:

1. **Land value** — paid at inception, like a sale
2. **Proportional share of project profits** — ongoing, over 3–5 years
3. **Depreciation pass-through** — if structured as a limited partnership
4. **Potential capital gains deferral** — if structured correctly with a tax advisor

For a project with $5–10M in development profit, a 30–40% landowner share represents **$1.5M–$4.0M in upside** that a straight sale leaves on the table.

### Structure: Development LP (Limited Partnership)

```
Development LP
├── GP (Developer entity) — 0.1%
│   └── Manages rezoning, construction, operations
├── LP1 (Development Partner) — 69.9%
│   └── Contributes all capital (~ $22–25M)
└── LP2 (Landowner Partnership) — 30%
    └── Contributes land at appraised value (~ $5M)
```

### Land Contribution Mechanics

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Commission independent appraisal of assembled land value (~$5–7M for 3-parcel assembly) |
| 2 | Landowners contribute land to LP at appraised value |
| 3 | Landowners receive LP units proportional to land contribution |
| 4 | Developer contributes all construction capital (~$22–25M) |
| 5 | Project profits distributed per LP agreement: 70/30 split |

### Developer Economics

| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| Total project cost | ~$27–32M |
| Developer cash contribution | ~$22–25M |
| Developer equity (30% share) | ~$1.0–1.5M |
| Developer carried profit (40% of upside) | ~$0.4–0.6M |
| **Developer IRR** (if project earns $5M profit) | ~25–35% on equity |
| **Developer total return** | ~$1.4–2.1M on $22–25M invested = **6–9% total return** |

This is attractive to a developer because: (a) the land is contributed at cost, so no cash outlay for land; (b) developer only finances construction; (c) the healthcare housing narrative reduces planning risk, lowering the developer's risk premium.

### Landowner Economics

| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| Land contributed at inception | ~$5M (appraised) |
| Profit share (30% of ~$5M profit) | ~$1.5M |
| **Total landowner return** | ~$6.5M on a $2.5M original land investment |
| **Landowner IRR** | ~**30–40%** over 3–4 year project |

This is dramatically better than selling the land for $3.5–4.5M today.

### Simpler Alternative: Land Value + Profit Participation

If a formal LP is too complex, a cleaner two-party arrangement:

| Payment | Amount | Timing |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Land purchase price | $5,000,000 | At closing (like a sale) |
| Profit participation | $1,500,000 | On project occupancy / completion |
| **Total to landowners** | **$6,500,000** | |

This requires: (a) a Development Agreement with completion guarantees, (b) a firm price for the land, (c) audited cost reporting so profit is verifiable, and (d) a buyout right if the developer defaults on milestones.

### Key Negotiation Points

| Item | What to Push For |
|------|-----------------|
| Land value basis | Independent appraisal commissioned by landowners — not the developer's number |
| Profit share | 30–40% to landowners; 60–70% to developer |
| Preferred return | Developer gets their 8% preferred return off the top; then split kicks in |
| Rezoning deadline | Developer must submit rezoning application within 18 months or landowners recover land |
| Completion guarantee | Personal guarantees from developer's principals — not just corporate entity |
| Buyout clause | Landowners can exit at land value + 20% if developer misses milestones |
| Audit rights | Landowners have right to review developer's cost certifications annually |

## Section 8 — Recommendation

### GO / PROCEED WITH CAUTION / NO GO

## **PROCEED WITH CAUTION**

### Rationale

**The case FOR proceeding:**
1. The site is in a genuine TOD location — 550–700m from Sapperton Station, confirmed Tier 3 under BC's mandatory TOD legislation
2. The RCH expansion (new tower opened May 31, 2026; $2B further Phase 2–3 investment) creates a structural, non-cyclical demand driver for housing within walking distance. This is the single most compelling factor for this site.
3. "Sapperton Healthcare Living" is the strongest possible planning narrative — it is literally housing for the workers of New Westminster's largest employer. The City cannot easily oppose this framing.
4. The healthcare housing positioning unlocks non-market capital (BC Housing, CMHC Affordable Housing Fund, federal grants) that standard market rental does not qualify for. This meaningfully improves the project's financial feasibility.
5. Vacancy rates are 0.8% — among the lowest in Metro Vancouver. Any well-located new rental supply will be absorbed.
6. Land value uplift of $1.5–4.0M on a three-parcel assembly is real and demonstrable.

**The case for CAUTION rather than GO:**
1. Current zoning (RS) does not permit multi-unit development outright — rezoning is required for Scenario C
2. The three-parcel assembly (417 + 419 + adjacent) is not yet confirmed. Without the adjacent parcel, the development envelope for Scenario C is marginal.
3. Council retains full discretion to refuse any rezoning application, regardless of TOD legislation or healthcare framing
4. Construction costs remain elevated at $400–$450/sqft; thin market rental margins make Scenario C financially marginal without non-market capital participation
5. The Landowner Partnership (Section 6B) introduces partnership risk — developer default, cost overruns, or abandonment can freeze the land
