## Urgent surgical warning signs
Contact the surgical team urgently or go to emergency care as instructed for:
- Fever or chills, especially a temperature at or above the threshold in his discharge papers
- New, severe, or steadily worsening abdominal pain
- Increasing abdominal swelling, hardness, or tenderness
- Persistent nausea or vomiting, or inability to keep fluids down
- Little or no gas or stool together with pain, swelling, or vomiting
- Heavy rectal bleeding or black, tarry stool
- Incision opening, uncontrolled bleeding, spreading redness, warmth, pus, foul drainage, or rapidly increasing pain
- Very little urine, marked dizziness, dry mouth, or other dehydration signs
- One-sided calf pain/swelling
- If an ostomy is present: dark/pale/dusky stoma, severe cramps with little output, or unusually high watery output
Bowel obstruction or an anastomotic leak can be serious after resection; worsening pain, swelling, vomiting, fever, and inability to pass gas or stool require prompt assessment.[\[1\]](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002941.htm)[\[2\]](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000150.htm)
## Recovery principles
1. **Follow the operation-specific discharge plan.** Restrictions depend on what was removed and whether surgery was open or laparoscopic.
2. **Walk early and progressively.** Early mobilization supports recovery and reduces deconditioning; walking should increase gradually without forcing through significant pain or exhaustion.[\[3\]](https://www.uhn.ca/Surgery/My_Surgery/Pages/After_Surgery.aspx)[\[4\]](https://myhealth.alberta.ca/Health/aftercareinformation/pages/conditions.aspx?hwid=zc2047)
3. **Fuel healing.** This is not the time for fasting, crash dieting, or deliberate rapid weight loss.
4. **Use tolerance as feedback.** Record food, fluids, pain, bowel/ostomy output, temperature, walking, and symptoms.
5. **Escalate deterioration early.** Do not wait for the next routine appointment when warning signs appear.
6. **Add only one major change at a time.** This makes food and activity intolerance easier to identify.

## Nutrition plan
### The diet decision gate
Enhanced-recovery guidelines support offering a regular diet within 24 hours after uncomplicated elective colorectal surgery. However, Canadian aftercare guidance sometimes recommends small, temporarily low-fibre meals with gradual fibre reintroduction.[\[5\]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9746347/)[\[4\]](https://myhealth.alberta.ca/Health/aftercareinformation/pages/conditions.aspx?hwid=zc2047)
**Therefore: use the diet named in Frank's discharge instructions.** If the paperwork is unclear, call the surgical unit or dietitian before imposing a liquid-only or low-fibre diet.
### Early eating pattern
- Five or six small meals/snacks may be easier than large meals.
- Eat slowly and chew food thoroughly.
- Stop before becoming uncomfortably full.
- Include protein at every meal or snack; postoperative protein supports healing.[\[6\]](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/info/nutrition/if-nfs-eating-well-after-colostomy-surgery.pdf)
- Drink the amount recommended by his surgical team. Fluid targets must be individualized for kidney, heart, liver, ostomy, and medication considerations.
- If appetite is poor, ask a registered dietitian whether a nutrition supplement is appropriate.
- Keep a simple food-and-symptom log.
### Often-tolerated choices if a temporary low-fibre plan was ordered
- Eggs; fish; tender chicken or turkey
- Tofu; smooth yogurt; cottage cheese, if tolerated
- White rice or congee; noodles; pasta; potatoes without skin
- White toast; crackers; low-fibre cereal
- Bananas; applesauce; canned or peeled soft fruit
- Well-cooked peeled carrots, squash, or zucchini
- Broth and simple soups
### Foods to delay if the team ordered low fibre or they provoke symptoms
- Raw vegetables and large salads
- Bran and dense whole grains
- Beans and lentils
- Nuts, seeds, popcorn, and corn
- Fruit skins and dried fruit
- Large greasy, fried, or very spicy meals
- Alcohol and carbonated drinks if they worsen symptoms
### Fibre restoration
When the surgeon approves and bowel function is stable:
1. Add one higher-fibre food in a small portion.
2. Observe for 24 hours.
3. Continue if tolerated; pause and contact the team for significant pain, swelling, vomiting, or reduced output.
4. Progress toward a long-term pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes, fish, poultry, and healthy fats.
> 
	**Ostomy gate:** If Frank has an ileostomy or colostomy, use his stoma nurse's hydration, electrolyte, food, blockage, and output instructions. Ileostomy fluid and sodium needs can differ substantially from routine advice.

## Movement and exercise plan
### Phase 1 — discharge through week 1
**Goal:** circulation, lung expansion, basic independence, and fatigue management.
- Take short, easy walks several times daily.
- Begin with the duration he can complete while speaking comfortably.
- Add a small amount of time or distance as tolerated.
- Move ankles and legs when sitting.
- Avoid pushing, pulling, abdominal exercise, running, cycling, kettlebells, resistance training, and strenuous housework.
- Support the abdomen with a pillow when coughing if the care team recommended it.
- Stop and seek advice for dizziness, chest symptoms, unusual breathlessness, wound pulling, escalating abdominal pain, or marked fatigue.
### Phase 2 — weeks 2 and 3, if uncomplicated
**Goal:** gradually increase total daily movement.
- Build toward several 10–15 minute easy walks or the equivalent total, according to tolerance.
- Continue gentle daily activities without straining.
- Use stairs carefully if permitted.
- Keep at least one lighter day after a meaningful activity increase.
- Do not lift beyond Frank's written restriction.
- No kettlebell swings, push-ups, planks, sit-ups, running, or high-intensity intervals.
### Phase 3 — weeks 4 through 6, only if healing normally
**Goal:** rebuild aerobic capacity without loading the abdominal wall.
- Progress toward 20–30 minutes of comfortable walking on most days, continuously or in shorter bouts.
- Consider easy stationary cycling only after the surgeon approves it.
- Continue to avoid heavy resistance, breath-holding, straining, and impact exercise until cleared.
- Open procedures, complications, hernia risk, or an ostomy may require a slower timeline.
### Phase 4 — after surgical clearance
Return in this order:
1. Longer walking and easy stationary cycling
2. Gentle mobility
3. Light resistance bands or very light weights
4. Progressive resistance training
5. Running, kettlebell swings, abdominal loading, and interval training last
Increase only one variable—duration, frequency, or intensity—at a time. If pain or fatigue remains substantially worse the next day, return to the previous tolerated level.
## Weight management
Frank's preoperative BMI is approximately 33.7, but intentional weight loss should wait until:
- The surgeon confirms healing
- Intake and hydration are stable
- Bowel or ostomy function is predictable
- There is no unresolved infection, anemia, or nutritional problem
- Pathology-dependent treatment planning is complete
After clearance, use a slow, sustainable program built around diet quality, walking, resistance exercise, sleep, and clinician-monitored metabolic health—not fasting during active recovery.

Ask the surgeon:
1. Exactly which bowel segment was removed and why?
2. Was the operation open, laparoscopic, or converted from laparoscopic to open?
3. Was the bowel reconnected? Is there an anastomosis?
4. Is there an ileostomy or colostomy, and is it intended to be temporary?
5. What diet should Frank follow, and for how long?
6. What are his specific fluid and electrolyte targets?
7. What are his lifting, driving, bathing, work, and exercise restrictions?
8. Which bowel medicines, fibre products, probiotics, pain relievers, and supplements are permitted?
9. When should bowel habits normalize enough to prompt concern?
10. When is the next surgical review?
If a tumour was removed, ask:
- What is the final diagnosis, tumour site, grade, and TNM stage?
- Are the surgical margins clear?
- How many lymph nodes were examined and how many contained cancer?
- Were lymphovascular or perineural invasion identified?
- Were mismatch-repair or microsatellite-instability tests performed?
- Is CT imaging, CEA testing, genetic assessment, colonoscopy, or oncology referral required?
Pathology margins, lymph-node findings, tumour features, and biomarker testing—not the four-inch length alone—guide diagnosis and further treatment.[\[7\]](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/diagnosis/pathology-reports-fact-sheet)
