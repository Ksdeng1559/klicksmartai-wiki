# 02 — Variable 1: Dream Outcome
## How Hormozi frames it
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	*"Let's say you have something you can sell for weight loss, like a \$50,000 liposuction surgery. And on the other hand, you've got a \$5 ebook that promises a skinny tummy. If they make the same promise — same dream outcome — how can these two things be so different in price? It's the other three variables."*
</callout>
\>
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	— Alex Hormozi
</callout>
The dream outcome is variable #1 because nothing else in the equation matters if this piece is missing. It's the result the prospect actually wants — not what you do, not how you do it, but what they're trying to achieve.
One note from Hormozi that matters for cold email: business-oriented dream outcomes (making money, investment returns, business growth) tend to command higher prices than consumer dream outcomes (like losing weight). If you're writing B2B cold email, you're already operating in the higher-leverage category. Your dream outcome line has real weight.
---
## The common mistake
Most cold emails spend the majority of their word count describing **the service**, not the result the prospect would actually get.
A common example looks like this:
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	*"We're a B2B YouTube agency that helps B2B companies grow their online presence."*
</callout>
That sentence tells the reader what you do. It tells them nothing about what they would actually get from working with you.
The people you're emailing don't know they need you yet. Every sentence they spend trying to parse your internal structure is a sentence they're not spending imagining the outcome. That's how reply rates quietly die.
---
## The 2026 way
Don't **state** the dream outcome directly. **Demonstrate** a brand that's already at it, then offer to share how they got there.
Same dream outcome. Different delivery mechanism:
- **Old way:** *"If we could help you drive \$80M in revenue using our ad system, guaranteed or you don't pay, would that be worth a chat?"*
- **New way:** Offer a video breakdown of how a comparable brand doing \$80M is running their full-funnel right now.
The new version never asks the prospect to believe you can take them to \$80M. It offers them a free look at what a brand at that level is actually doing behind the scenes. It's casual, value-dense, and passive — the dream outcome is still \$80M, but you're not claiming you'll deliver it. You're showing evidence that the level exists and offering access to how it's being executed.
A PS line can then float a related low-commitment offer (a complimentary audit of their own account, for example) that quietly implies you could help them move toward the same outcome — without ever promising you will.

## Real script: the \$80M e-commerce audit
**Subject:** \{\$80M e-com teardown, relevant to \{company\}\}
**Body:**
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	Hey \{first_name\},
</callout>
\>
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	Just finished a full-funnel account audit for a \{niche\} e-commerce brand doing over \$80M in revenue.
</callout>
\>
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	I recorded a quick breakdown of what we found — the ad structure, the funnel gaps, and the three changes they're testing next quarter.
</callout>
\>
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	Worth me sending the video over?
</callout>
\>
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	\{name\}
</callout>
\>
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	**P.S.** We're doing a small batch of complimentary audits this month for \{niche\} brands in a similar revenue range. Happy to run the same teardown on your account if the video ends up being useful.
</callout>

## What this script is *not* doing
It's not saying *"we helped Brand X go from \$1M to \$10M in three years."* It's not guaranteeing a result. It's not even explicitly positioning you as the reason the \$80M brand got there. The dream outcome is present in every line, but nowhere does the email pitch it.
That's the entire move. The old way projects the dream outcome onto the prospect. The new way places it in front of them and lets them reach for it.
---
## Checklist before sending
- [ ] Does the email reference a specific brand or result that represents the dream outcome?
- [ ] Is the dream outcome framed as evidence, not a claim?
- [ ] Have you cut every sentence that explains *how you do it* in favor of sentences showing *what they'd get*?
- [ ] Is the CTA lower-friction than the dream outcome implies (a video, an audit, a resource)?
