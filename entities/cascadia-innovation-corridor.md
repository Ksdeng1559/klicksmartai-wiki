# Cascadia Innovation Corridor

Cross-border region spanning **British Columbia (Canada), Washington State, and Oregon State (USA)**, built around the I-5 corridor connecting Vancouver, Seattle, Portland, and surrounding metro areas.

## 1. Economic Profile

### Growth Leadership
- **2012–2017:** Washington posted the strongest real GDP growth of any U.S. state; BC posted the strongest of any Canadian province.
- Top 3 fastest-growing North American jurisdictions (of 60): **Washington → California → British Columbia** (BCBC, 2018)
- Washington's 2017 real GDP growth: **4.4%** (repeat of 2016's 3.9%)
- BC's 2017 real GDP growth: **3.9%** — led all provinces for 3 consecutive years

### Trade Architecture
- Washington's export-to-GDP ratio: **~16%** — among the highest of any U.S. state (most states average ~6.7%)
- Washington's top export markets: **China (#1), Canada (#2), Japan (#3)**
- BC's top export markets: **USA (#1), China (#2), Japan (#3)**
- If BC were an independent country, it would be **Washington's 3rd largest export market**
- **Washington exports more to BC than to all other Canadian provinces combined**
- **BC exports more to Washington than to any U.S. state**
- 90% of northbound truck crossings at the border are destined for the Lower Mainland (BC). Nearly 60% of southbound trucks are destined for Whatcom County or Puget Sound.
- The four border crossings in the corridor handle **>80% of all B.C.-Washington vehicle crossings**

### Shared Sector Profile
Both jurisdictions share similar economic structure:
- Strong technology sectors
- Substantial forest products
- Expanding agricultural sectors
- Large port gateways connecting to overseas markets
- Growing biotech sectors
- Abundant hydroelectric resources
- Dynamic film and television industries

## 2. The Housing Crisis

### Scale
- Home prices and rents across Cascadia have risen **2–4x faster than incomes** since 2019
- Share of housing stock affordable to households **<120% AMI** in Washington/Oregon: **80–85% (2019) → 45% (2024)** — catastrophic collapse
- Current deficit: **at least 250,000 housing units** across the region
- By 2050, an additional **3–4 million people** will call Cascadia home
- 20-year demand: **~3 million units**; current trajectory misses by ~1 million

### Jurisdiction Shortfalls (next 20 years)
| Jurisdiction | Shortfall |
|---|---|
| Washington | ~400,000 units |
| Oregon | ~200,000 units |
| British Columbia | ~300,000 units |

## 3. Grand Boulevard Strategy

### Concept
Higher-density mixed-use development along **underutilized strip commercial arterials** (4–6 lane roads) across the 285-mile corridor (Portland → Vancouver BC).

### Land Inventory (UrbanFootprint analysis)
- **>42,000 acres** of qualifying non-residential commercial land fronting 4–6 lane arterials
- Spans ~**2,000 miles of existing arterials** in the 285-mile study area
- Long-term capacity at 3–6 stories mixed-use/mixed-income: **4.6 million units**
- High-probability near-term redevelopment parcels (building-to-land value ratio < 1): **~13,000 acres** (20–30% of qualifying land)

### Potential Unit Production by Metro
| Metro | Near-term Units |
|---|---|
| Seattle Region | ~670,000 |
| Portland Region | ~400,000 |
| Metro Vancouver (BC) | ~370,000 |
| **Cascadia Total** | **~1.4 million** |

### Cost Savings from Reform
| Reform | Savings |
|---|---|
| As-of-right entitlement streamlining | up to $48K/unit |
| Eliminated/changed parking minimums | up to $50K/parking stall |
| **Combined average savings** | **~$90,000/unit** |

### Policy Ingredients
1. **As-of-Right Zoning** — mixed-income, mixed-use housing by-right
2. **Form-Based Codes** — massing/height; no over-regulation of housing diversity
3. **Parking Reform** — eliminate mandatory minimums; market-driven ratios
4. **Fast-Track Permitting** — streamline entitlements for new housing
5. **Workforce Housing Focus** — 80–120% AMI, with inclusionary affordable housing below 80% AMI
6. **Targeted Subsidies** — tax incentives, finance insurance, impact fee reductions where needed

### Co-Benefits
- Arterials naturally on high ground → avoids flood/fire climate hazard zones
- Higher density enhances transit viability (240 miles of wide 6-lane arterials can accommodate BRT)
- Bikeway/landscaping/sidewalk improvements possible along 1,700 miles of 4-lane facilities
- Reduced auto dependency and GHG emissions by locating housing near jobs/services/transit

## 4. Whatcom County Master-Planned Community

> Developer pitch document — distinct from the BCBC/Cascadia policy reports.

- **Investment:** $50B over 25–30 years
- **Residents:** 20,000+ in single integrated community
- **Location:** Whatcom County, WA (north of Bellingham, near Blaine/Canadian border)

### Key Components
1. **Residential** — single-family, multifamily, affordable housing
2. **2,000-acre Industrial Park** — technology, logistics, advanced industries
3. **Cascadia Rail Integration** — Seattle ↔ Bellingham ↔ Blaine ↔ Surrey
4. **IT & Financial Hubs** — dedicated zones for multinational corporations
5. **Commercial/Retail** — malls, office towers, entertainment districts
6. **Tourism & Hospitality** — world-class resort + iconic convention center
7. **Recreation & Education** — parks, schools, community center, green spaces
8. **Healthcare** — hospital, daycares, senior housing, essential services

### Alignment with Cascadia Reports
- Cascadia Rail connectivity as major economic catalyst (BCBC 2018 + housing reports both cite this)
- Whatcom County as strategic cross-border node (BCBC: 60% of southbound trucks destined for Whatcom or Puget Sound)
- Industrial park and housing development aligning with regional workforce housing needs
- Directly relevant to [[whatcom-county-housing-developers]] CDFI pipeline ( Kulshan CLT, Opportunity Council, Mercy Housing, Habitat for Humanity all active in Whatcom County)

## 5. Cross-Cutting Themes

- **Public-Private Partnership** — essential to financing, entitlement reform, and implementation across both U.S. and Canadian jurisdictions
- **Asia Orientation** — both BC and Washington are more Asia-facing than their national counterparts; China is top export market for Washington, #2 for BC
- **Climate Resilience** — new development along arterials avoids hazard zones; green infrastructure in Whatcom MPC plans
- **Workforce Housing Gap** — crisis is specifically acute for 80–120% AMI households; affordable stock collapsed 80–85% → 45% in 5 years
- **Regional Governance** — Cascadia Innovation Corridor framework: co-chaired by Challenge Seattle (Gov. Chris Gregoire) and Business Council of BC (Laura Jones) as of 2024
- **Cascadia Rail** — recurring infrastructure theme connecting the corridor; referenced in all four source documents
