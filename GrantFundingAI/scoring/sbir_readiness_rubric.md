# SBIR Readiness Scoring Rubric

## Scoring Overview
GrantFundingAI uses a weighted score to estimate whether a company appears ready for SBIR/STTR or related funding pathways.

This score is not a legal eligibility determination. It is an evidence-based readiness score based on website content, interview answers, and supporting documents.

## Score Categories

| Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Eligibility | 20 |
| Problem significance | 15 |
| Innovation | 15 |
| Commercialization | 15 |

## Decision Thresholds

| Score | Decision |
|---:|---|
| 85–100 | High Priority |

| 40–69 | Needs Development |
| 0–39 | Not SBIR Ready |

## Evidence Logic
Use three-state logic:

- YES: evidence found
- NO: evidence contradicted or absent after review
- UNKNOWN: evidence not visible from website

UNKNOWN should trigger a follow-up interview question.

## SBIF Reviewer Scorecard Alignment

This section maps each rubric criterion to what a typical SBIF review panel evaluates during official review. Use this alignment to understand how GrantFundingAI readiness signals correspond to real-world evaluation criteria.

### Eligibility → 20 pts
*Corresponds to: SBA Eligibility Checklist + Agency Scientific Merit Review*

SBIF reviewers confirm eligibility before scoring merit. A panel checks:
- **U.S. incorporation** and business status (for-profit, ≤500 employees)
- **R&D focus** — the project must be predominantly R&D, not sales or marketing
- **Innovation locus** — the small business must itself perform the R&D

GrantFundingAI eligibility signals (U.S. entity, for-profit, small business, R&D activity, commercial intent) map directly to what reviewers verify during the eligibility pre-screen.

### Problem Significance → 15 pts
*Corresponds to: "Significance of the Problem" Scorecard Item (typically 20–25 pts in agency rubrics)*

Reviewers ask: *Does this problem matter at scale?* They look for:
- A clearly articulated, specific problem—not a vague pain point
- Evidence the problem is widespread or high-impact (supported by data or citations)
- Why this problem is urgent or growing (market failures, regulatory changes, technological shifts)

GrantFundingAI signals (clear problem statement, large market/community, measurability, public-sector relevance, urgency) mirror the reviewer question: "So what?"

### Innovation → 15 pts
*Corresponds to: "Technical Innovation" or "Novelty" Scorecard Item (typically 20 pts)*

Reviewers evaluate: *Is this genuinely new or improved, not incremental?* They assess:
- Proprietary advantage or differentiated approach vs. existing solutions
- Technical feasibility supported by evidence (data, preliminary results, lit review)
- Patent filings, published IP, or clear defensibility path

The rubric's innovation signals map closely: proprietary tech, novel process, prototype evidence, and patent/IP signals each address what reviewers look for in this dimension.

### Commercialization → 15 pts
*Corresponds to: "Commercialization Potential" or "Market Readiness" Item (typically 15–20 pts)*

Reviewers ask: *Can this turn into a sustainable product or service with revenue?* They evaluate:
- Defined customer segments and real market pull
- Credible revenue model or funding pathway beyond the grant
- Competitive landscape addressed, not ignored
- Traction indicators (letters of intent, pilot data, contracts, revenue)

GrantFundingAI's signals (customer segment, revenue model, market opportunity, case studies/testimonials, scaling path) each address a common reviewer concern: "Will this outlive the grant?"

### Government Fit → 15 pts
*Corresponds to: "Relevance to Agency Mission" or "Program Fit" Item (typically 15–20 pts)*

This is often a **go/no-go** factor. Reviewers check:
- Explicit connection to the agency's named problem areas, topics, or solicitations
- Benefit to a public sector mission (energy, health, environment, defense, etc.)
- Pathway to deployment or use by government (procurement, partnership, licensing)

The rubric's agency signal, public infrastructure relevance, municipal/county use case, and mission alignment map directly. The procurement pathway signal (2 pts) reflects the reviewer concern for sustainability beyond public funding.

### Community Fit → 10 pts
*Corresponds to: "Broader Impacts" or "Societal Benefits" Item (typically 10 pts)*

Reviewers—particularly at NSF, DOE, and USDA—assess:
- Benefits to specific underserved or disadvantaged communities
- Environmental improvements or sustainability contributions
- Workforce development or job creation in target communities
- Non-deployment risks (does the tech create new problems?)

GrantFundingAI's community signals (rural relevance, tribal relevance, environmental improvement, workforce/job creation, housing/infrastructure benefit) reflect the broader-impacts lens used by agencies with social-mission mandates.

### Partnerships → 10 pts
*Corresponds to: "Team / Collaborators" or "Resources" Item (typically 10 pts)*

Reviewers examine the team's ability to execute. Strong applications show:
- Relevant institutional credentials (university affiliation, research history)
- Industry or customer partners who validate market pull
- Government or nonprofit partners who improve deployment likelihood
- Technical advisors or a credible management team

The rubric's partnership signals (university, industry, government, pilot customer, strategic advisor) each address a dimension reviewers weigh when asking: "Who else believes in this, and why?"

### Using This Alignment

When evaluating a company against the rubric:
1. Score each category using the evidence signals
2. Cross-reference with the SBIF reviewer lens above
3. Identify gaps where a "UNKNOWN" score corresponds to a high-weight reviewer concern
4. Use the alignment to craft follow-up interview questions or coaching priorities

A company scoring 70+ on GrantFundingAI with strong Government Fit and Innovation scores is well-positioned for a competitive SBIF application. Scores below 70 in Government Fit or Innovation are the most common reviewer rejection factors.
