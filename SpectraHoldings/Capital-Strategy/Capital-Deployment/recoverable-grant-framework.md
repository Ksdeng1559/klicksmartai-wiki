## Executive Purpose

This framework defines how Spectra may structure recoverable grants and impact capital as restricted-purpose capital that can be deployed, tracked, recovered where permitted, and redeployed into future community development projects.

The framework supports Spectra's four-pillar model:

1. Health
2. Housing
3. Economics
4. Education

The purpose is to convert nonprofit, foundation, donor, faith-based, CDFI, CRA, municipal, and public-purpose capital into a controlled capital deployment system.

---

## Strategic Thesis

Spectra's core thesis is that many underserved communities do not lack demand; they lack retained wealth. Capital often enters distressed communities but exits immediately through outside developers, outside contractors, outside retailers, and outside operators.

Spectra's model is designed to retain and recirculate wealth through:

- local housing delivery;
- local workforce development;
- local procurement;
- vertically integrated manufacturing and distribution;
- homebuyer readiness;
- community-serving commercial infrastructure;
- disciplined capital recycling.

```text
Restricted Mission Capital
        ↓
Controlled Deployment System
        ↓
Housing + Jobs + Local Services
        ↓
Community Wealth Retention
        ↓
Recovery Trigger, if applicable
        ↓
Redeployment into Next Project
```

---

## Why Recoverable Grants

Recoverable grants allow mission-aligned funders to deploy philanthropic-style capital with:

- restricted use controls;
- project-level reporting;
- stewardship documentation;
- public-benefit measurement;
- recoverability triggers;
- capital recycling.

Unlike traditional grants, recoverable grants may return some or all principal if defined project outcomes or revenue events occur. Returned capital can then be redeployed into additional eligible projects.

## Documentation Stack

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **CDPA — Community Development Partnership Agreement** | Master framework establishing purpose, governance, partner roles, reporting standards, conflict management, and order of precedence. |
| **RGA — Recoverable Grant Agreement** | Program-level agreement defining restricted use, disbursement, recovery, repayment triggers, audit rights, reporting, remedies, and legal terms. |
| **UFA — Use of Funds Agreement** | Project- or tranche-specific agreement defining eligible uses, prohibited uses, restricted accounts, draw controls, and project-level reporting. |
| **Restricted Account Agreement** | Defines segregated bank accounts and controls for restricted funds. |
| **Escrow / Trustee Instructions** | Defines milestone-based release mechanics. |
| **Project Joinder** | Adds a project, hub, tract, or tranche into the program. |
| **Reporting Template** | Standardizes financial, impact, and compliance reporting. |
| **Reinvestment Policy** | Defines how recovered capital may be reused. |

## Restricted Use of Funds

All recoverable grant proceeds should be restricted to approved program purposes and tracked through project-level accounting.

Potential eligible uses may include:

- predevelopment;
- site control;
- entitlement support;
- engineering;
- infrastructure-enabling costs;
- construction-enabling capital;
- affordability gap support;
- developer credits for eligible homebuyers;
- workforce training;
- homebuyer readiness;
- health, education, and economic pillar launch activities;
- compliance and reporting systems.

Prohibited uses should include:

- personal benefit;
- political activity;
- impermissible lobbying;
- personal guarantees;
- unrestricted corporate operating expenses;
- any use inconsistent with the approved scope.

---

## Stewardship Controls

Minimum controls:

- segregated restricted accounts;
- project-level accounting;
- approved budget;
- eligible-use certification;
- milestone-based disbursement;
- compliance officer review;
- monthly reconciliation;
- record retention;
- audit rights;
- remediation procedures for noncompliant expenditures.

## Sample Mission Capital Deployment

For a nonprofit or foundation capital commitment, funds should not be deposited into general corporate operating accounts.

Preferred structure:

```text
Mission Capital Commitment
        ↓
Restricted Account
        ↓
Approved Budget + UFA
        ↓
Milestone Draws
        ↓
Project SPV / MCF Deployment
        ↓
Eligible Project Costs
        ↓
Recovery or Forgiveness Treatment
```

Illustrative allocation for a $3M restricted capital pool:

| Category | Amount | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Land control / site readiness | $300,000 | Options, diligence, title, surveys |
| Predevelopment | $450,000 | Engineering, permits, design, legal |
| Construction-enabling reserve | $1,800,000 | Eligible vertical or project-enabling costs |
| Contingency | $300,000 | Overrun protection |
| Compliance / reporting | $150,000 | Audit, reporting, administration |
| **Total** | **$3,000,000** |  |

All amounts are illustrative and subject to project-specific underwriting.

## Relationship to the Master Credit Facility

The Master Credit Facility acts as the deployment engine for repeatable housing production.

Recoverable grant capital may serve as:

- soft subordinate mission capital;
- affordability gap support;
- predevelopment capital;
- first-loss reserve;
- developer credit support;
- project-enabling capital;
- leverage support for CRA, CDFI, CMF, municipal, or tax-credit capital.

The goal is not to spend the same funds once. The goal is to create a controlled revolving deployment model where recoverable capital can support multiple project cycles where permitted.

## Nonprofit Capital Stack Role

Recoverable grant capital may sit beside or underneath other layers of the stack:

| Layer | Role |
|---|---|
| Donor / foundation / DAF capital | Mission capital, grant, recoverable grant, or PRI |
| HUD Section 4 | Capacity building, not construction capital |
| CMF | Affordable housing finance support and leverage layer |
| CRA bank capital | Senior debt, participation, or qualified investment |
| CDFI capital | Acquisition, bridge, infrastructure, or subordinate capital |
| NMTC / LIHTC / OZ | Tax-credit or place-based equity support |
| MCF | Construction deployment and capital recycling engine |
| Developer / sponsor equity | Alignment and risk capital |

## Institutional Alignment

### CRA

Eligible tranches may be structured to help participating banks document community development purpose, LMI benefit, restricted use, use of proceeds, and measurable outcomes.

### HUD Section 4

Section 4 should be treated as capacity-building support for qualified nonprofit capacity, reporting systems, pipeline development, technical assistance, and organizational readiness. It should not be commingled with recoverable grant proceeds.

### NMTC

NMTC may support job-creating and community-serving facilities such as manufacturing, distribution, health, education, and commercial assets in eligible low-income communities.

### Opportunity Zones

Opportunity Zone capital may support long-horizon place-based equity where the site and structure qualify.

### CDBG / Section 108

Municipal partners may use CDBG or Section 108 where eligible for infrastructure, public improvements, or project-enabling costs. The municipality remains the grantee or borrower; Spectra may act as implementation partner or master developer.

## Decision Framework

Each recoverable grant deployment must answer:

1. What is the capital source?
2. Is it grant, recoverable grant, PRI, debt, equity, or hybrid?
3. What entity receives the funds?
4. What account holds the funds?
5. What uses are eligible?
6. What uses are prohibited?
7. What documentation controls the funds?
8. What milestone releases the funds?
9. What event triggers recovery?
10. Where does recovered capital go?
11. What reporting is required?
12. What happens if funds are misused or the project changes?

## Validation Requirements Before Implementation

Before external use or implementation:

- tax counsel should review nonprofit, donor, and foundation treatment;
- securities counsel should review any return-bearing capital, investor participation, units, debt, or equity;
- public-program counsel should validate HUD, CRA, CMF, CDFI, NMTC, OZ, CDBG, and Section 108 references;
- accounting advisors should validate restricted fund treatment;
- all performance claims should be backed by source documentation;
- all financial examples should remain labeled illustrative unless supported by executed agreements or verified operating history.

## Executive Decision Brief

**Decision:** GO as a draft internal framework.

**Strategic value:** This framework converts recoverable grants from a fundraising idea into a governed capital deployment system.

**What improves the strategy:**

- restricted accounts;
- CDPA / RGA / UFA standardization;
- milestone draws;
- project-level accounting;
- recovery triggers;
- reinvestment policy;
- capital stack integration;
- CRA / CDFI / CMF / HUD alignment.

**What breaks the strategy:**

- commingling funds;
- unrestricted operating use;
- unclear recovery terms;
- unsupported public-benefit claims;
- no reporting cadence;
- no reinvestment policy;
- no counsel review.
