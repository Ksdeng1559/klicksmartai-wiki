# Master Credit Facility Alignment

## Purpose

This document explains how the Spectra Master Credit Facility (MCF) aligns with CDFI capital, mission-driven finance, public-private partnership frameworks, and scalable housing deployment.

---

## Strategic Thesis

The MCF is not a traditional one-off construction loan.

It is a revolving housing production system designed to:

- recycle capital,
- accelerate deployment,
- reduce financing friction,
- and support repeatable housing delivery.

Within the Spectra ecosystem, the MCF functions as the operational funding engine for residential construction.

---

## Core MCF Characteristics

- Revolving construction facility.
- Residential housing focus.
- Capital deployed into project-level SPEs.
- Controlled draw schedules.
- Escrow governance.
- First-lien collateral where applicable.
- 110% funding logic.
- 60% presale threshold before vertical construction.
- Standardized reporting.
- Repeatable deployment model.

---

## Why CDFIs May Align with the MCF

CDFI-aligned capital seeks:

- measurable community impact,
- affordable housing outcomes,
- underserved market deployment,
- and scalable community development.

The MCF aligns because it creates:

- repeatable housing production,
- structured use-of-funds controls,
- project traceability,
- and a standardized deployment framework.

---

## Strategic Benefits

### 1. Capital Recycling

Unlike static project financing, the MCF recycles capital across multiple construction phases.

### 2. Controlled Deployment

The MCF uses phased construction deployment rather than uncontrolled scaling.

### 3. Improved Transparency

Escrow governance and cost-to-build reporting improve lender and investor visibility.

### 4. Municipal Alignment

The system supports:

- workforce housing,
- resilient housing,
- disaster recovery,
- and economic development.

### 5. Reduced Execution Friction

Vertical integration lowers:

- supply chain risk,
- markup stacking,
- delays,
- and contractor dependency.

---

## Strategic Capital Stack Role

Within a blended capital stack, the MCF can function as:

- construction capital,
- revolving project financing,
- deployment infrastructure,
- or warehouse-style project funding.

Potential adjacent capital layers include:

- CDFI subordinate debt,
- municipal bonds,
- grants,
- Opportunity Zone capital,
- NMTC,
- philanthropic capital,
- donor-advised funds,
- and institutional private capital.

---

## Institutional Narrative

The MCF should be positioned as:

**A standardized housing finance operating system capable of deploying capital repeatedly across underserved markets while maintaining controls, reporting, and community impact alignment.**
