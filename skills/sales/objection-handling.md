# Objection Handling — B2B Sales Framework

## Core Philosophy

"An objection is a request for more information — not a rejection."

The first stated objection is rarely the real objection. Prevention through discovery beats reactive handling every time.

## The ACRC Framework

1. **Acknowledge** — Validate the concern without agreeing with it
2. **Clarify** — Ask a question to find the real underlying issue
3. **Respond** — Address with evidence, reframe, or story
4. **Confirm** — Check that the response landed: "Does that make sense?"

## Common Objections + ACRC Responses

### Price / Budget
"That's fair to raise. Help me understand — is it that the budget doesn't exist, or that you're not sure the value justifies the cost?" Then: cost-of-inaction math, ROI framing, "compared to what?"

### Timing / Not now
"I hear that. What would need to be true for this to be the right time?" Surface the real urgency blocker or confirm it's a genuine delay.

### Competition / We're looking at others
"Totally reasonable. What's driving the evaluation — are you looking to solve a specific problem or comparing capabilities broadly?" Don't trash competitors. Win on fit.

### Status quo / We're fine
"What made you take the call today, then?" Or: "What would need to be true for the status quo to no longer work?" Find the latent pain.

### Authority / Need to talk to someone else
"Of course. To help you make the case internally, what are the top 2–3 things [decision-maker] will want to know?" Help the champion sell.

### Technical / Integration concerns
Take them seriously. "Let me connect you with our technical team to walk through this specifically." Don't minimize.

## Channel-Specific: Email Objections

Email structure (under 150 words):
1. Acknowledge their concern
2. Respond to the most likely interpretation
3. Ask one clarifying question
4. Suggest an easy next step (low-friction)

Don't defend. Don't over-explain. Create a path back to conversation.

## Ghosting Protocol

**Touch 1**: Value-add resource (no ask)  
**Touch 2**: Direct check-in: "Is this still a priority?"  
**Touch 3**: Breakup email: "I don't want to keep reaching out if the timing isn't right..."

Breakup emails recover ~30% of dormant prospects through loss aversion.

## Systemic vs. Individual

If 60%+ of prospects raise the same objection → pricing, positioning, or product problem. Track objections across 30 days to diagnose root causes.
