# Spectra Holdings Group × Whatcom County — Strategic Alignment Brief

**Date:** 2026-04-27
**Purpose:** Connect Spectra Holdings Group's $300M Master Credit Facility and vertically integrated construction model to Whatcom County's active affordable housing pipeline.

---

## Executive Summary

[[Whatcom County]] has **five active affordable housing developers** building 200+ units with a combined pipeline exceeding 300 units. All face the same funding gap: construction financing, gap equity, and down-payment assistance. [[Spectra Holdings Group]] operates a **$300M revolving credit facility** (MCF Fund) designed for exactly this use case — affordable residential construction with CDFI-aligned capital, buyer-side credit enhancements, and a "Pay as We Sell" repayment structure.

This brief maps each Whatcom developer to Spectra's capital stack and identifies the highest-probability engagement path.

---

## The Whatcom County Pipeline (At a Glance)

| Developer | Units in Pipeline | Status | Funding Need |
|-----------|-------------------|--------|--------------|
| [[Kulshan Community Land Trust|Kulshan CLT]] | 133 (18+60+55) | 1 project under construction, 2 in pre-development | Construction financing, permanent financing, grant match |
| [[Habitat for Humanity Whatcom|Habitat Whatcom]] | 30+ townhomes + scattered single-family | Active construction | Phased construction draws, materials financing |
| [[Opportunity Council]] | 129 (65+64) | Phase One under construction, Phase Two pending | Phase Two construction financing, senior housing capital |
| [[Future Homebuyers Organization|FHO]] | 7 (pilot) + scale plans | Pre-development, $1M state grant secured | Gap equity, down-payment assistance capital, scale financing |
| [[Mercy Housing Northwest|Mercy NW]] | 70 senior units | Late 2026/early 2027 construction start | Major construction financing, LIHTC equity, CDFI partnership |

**Total addressable pipeline:** 350+ affordable housing units.

## Spectra's Value Proposition for Whatcom Developers

### 1. Master Credit Facility (MCF) — Construction Financing

| Spectra Offer | Whatcom Fit |
|---------------|-------------|
| **Up to 110% of per-home cost** prior to vertical construction | Covers 100% of construction for Kulshan, Habitat, FHO projects |
| **12% flat interest, Pay as We Sell** | Repayment aligned with unit sales/closings — no accrued interest drag |
| **60% pre-sales gate** | De-risks construction; aligns with CLT and Habitat pre-sale models |
| **First lien + SPE isolation** | Each project in separate legal entity — no cross-contamination |
| **$5M minimum per tranche** | Fits Mercy NW (70 units) and Opportunity Council Phase Two (64 units) |

### 2. Buyer-Side Credit Enhancements

| Spectra Program | Whatcom Application |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| **No Down Payment Program** | Developer subsidy eliminates down-payment barrier for AMI-qualified buyers — critical for Whatcom's $80K–$120K income gap |
| **Buyback Protection** | SCNC guarantees loan performance; lender never faces foreclosure — reduces CDFI/capital partner risk |
| **Financial Literacy Training** | Required buyer qualification — aligns with CLT and Opportunity Council mission |

### 3. Vertically Integrated Construction Speed

| Traditional | Spectra |
|-------------|---------|
| 12–36 months planning + 12–24 months construction | 28–45 days post-permit per home |
| Fragmented stakeholders, cost overruns | Single-source: manufacturing → supply → construction → capital |
| 3–6 year total delivery | 1–2 year total delivery with pre-engineered plans |

**Whatcom implication:** Kulshan's Thornton Rd (55 homes) and Habitat's Mateo Meadows (30 townhomes) could compress delivery timeline by 18–36 months.

### 4. Disaster-Resistant Product

| Feature | Whatcom Relevance |
|---------|-------------------|
| ICF construction (storm/fire/earthquake/mold resistant) | Whatcom County seismic risk + moisture exposure |
| Revvo APU (93% electricity reduction) | Cuts resident utility burden — critical for AMI 30–80% households |
| 30+ year durability | Reduces long-term maintenance reserve requirements for CLT and nonprofit stewards |

## Developer-by-Developer Engagement Strategy

### Priority 1: Kulshan Community Land Trust

**Why first:** Smallest organization, most mission-aligned, Rose Lathrop (ED) is a visible advocate with direct grant-management experience. Kulshan already relies on layered public financing — they understand capital stacking.

**Spectra angle:** Offer MCF tranche for Thornton Rd (55 homes) or Telegraph Townhomes (60 units). Position as "construction financing + buyer down-payment assistance in one facility." Kulshan's permanently affordable model fits Spectra's "Pay as We Sell" — buyers are pre-qualified through CLT stewardship.

**Decision-maker:** Rose Lathrop, ED + Shell Philhower, Finance/Operations Director.

**Entry script:** "Kulshan is one of the most active affordable housing stewards in Whatcom. We're working with a CDFI-aligned construction finance facility that provides up to 110% of per-home cost, eliminates down-payment barriers for buyers, and repays only as units close. Would you have 15 minutes to walk through how this could layer with your current grant stack for Thornton Rd or Telegraph?"

---

### Priority 2: Mercy Housing Northwest

**Why second:** Largest single project (70 senior units), national organization with CDFI relationships already. Joe Thompson (President, NW Region) is a capital-raising executive.

**Spectra angle:** Mercy NW regularly deploys LIHTC + CDFI + conventional debt. Spectra's MCF can serve as the construction/bridge layer between LIHTC equity and permanent financing. The 70-unit scale justifies a dedicated $5M+ MCF tranche. Senior housing = stable buyer pool = lower pre-sales risk.

**Decision-maker:** Joe Thompson, President NW + Mercy NW capital team.

**Entry script:** "Mercy NW is one of the most sophisticated affordable housing capital structurers in the region. We're representing a $300M CDFI-construction facility that provides senior-secured, per-home construction financing with a 'Pay as We Sell' repayment structure. Given your 900 W. Holly St timeline, would it make sense to review a capital-stack comparison?"

---

### Priority 3: Opportunity Council

**Why third:** Large organization but leadership transition (Greg Winter retiring Aug 2026) creates timing uncertainty. Phase Two (64 senior units) is the right scale for MCF.

**Spectra angle:** Opportunity Council's Bellis Fair Phase Two needs construction financing. Spectra's MCF can provide the full construction envelope, with CMF (Capital Magnet Fund) or CDFI bond guarantee program layered beneath as subordinated capital. The "No Down Payment" program aligns with Opportunity Council's mission to serve low-income seniors.

**Decision-maker:** Incoming ED (TBD by Aug 2026) + current development team.

**Entry script:** "Opportunity Council has delivered more affordable units in Whatcom than almost any other nonprofit. With Bellis Fair Phase Two on the horizon, we're offering a construction finance facility designed for mission-aligned developers — no accrued interest, buyer down-payment support built in, and repayment tied to closings. Worth a conversation once your new leadership is in place?"

---

### Priority 4: Future Homebuyers Organization (FHO)

**Why fourth:** Brand-new organization, small pilot (7 units), but explicitly seeking capital to scale. Paul Schissler is a founder with energy but limited track record.

**Spectra angle:** FHO's Madrona partnership is a proof-of-concept. Spectra can offer a micro-tranche or joint-venture structure for the next phase — scaling from 7 to 30+ units. The "3X Cost to Build" program (unregistered, project-specific equity) is a fit for FHO's early-stage capital needs.

**Decision-maker:** Paul Schissler, Founder + Kerri Burnside, Board President.

**Entry script:** "FHO was built to solve the exact problem Spectra's model addresses — the gap between local wages and home prices. We've funded projects like FHO's Madrona pilot with a combination of construction credit and buyer-side down-payment assistance. Would you be open to a 20-minute call on how we could help you scale beyond the pilot?"

---

### Priority 5: Habitat for Humanity Whatcom

**Why fifth:** Habitat affiliates have national capital relationships (Habitat's Tithe Fund, local bank lines). However, Mateo Meadows (30 townhomes) is a discrete project that could accept MCF layering.

**Spectra angle:** Habitat's sweat-equity model reduces construction cost. Spectra's MCF can finance materials + subcontractor labor, with Habitat's volunteer labor reducing the per-home cost basis — making the MCF tranche even more efficient. Buyback Protection reduces Habitat's mortgage-recycling risk.

**Decision-maker:** Marcelo Pratesi, Development Director.

**Entry script:** "Habitat Whatcom's Mateo Meadows project is exactly the type of community-building development our facility funds — permanently affordable, buyer-qualified, construction-phase capital. We've partnered with Habitat affiliates in other states to provide materials-and-labor financing with a 'Pay as We Sell' structure. Is there room in your capital stack for a conversation?"

## Risk Factors & Mitigants

| Risk | Mitigant |
|------|----------|
| Whatcom developers unfamiliar with Spectra | Start with Kulshan (smallest, most mission-aligned); use as reference |
| Washington State regulatory environment | Spectra's ICF construction meets/exceeds WA building codes; Revvo APU qualifies for energy-efficiency incentives |
| Spectra has no Pacific Northwest presence | First project = beachhead; Kulshan or Mercy NW as anchor partner opens NW pipeline |
| CDFI certification required for CMF layering | Spectra partners with certified CDFIs (SCNC structure); no need for Spectra itself to be certified |
| Pre-sales gating (60% minimum) | CLT and Habitat models already pre-qualify buyers; senior housing (Mercy, Opportunity Phase Two) has waiting lists |
