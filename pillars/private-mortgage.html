🔑 Equity Is Your Qualification — Not Your Credit Score

What is a Private Mortgage?

A private mortgage is a loan secured against real estate, funded by private individuals, Mortgage Investment Corporations (MICs), or private capital pools — not banks or traditional financial institutions. Private lenders operate outside the regulated banking system, which gives them the freedom to approve loans based on equity and property value rather than income documentation and credit scores.

Private mortgages are typically short-term (6–24 months), interest-only or partially amortized, and carry higher rates (8–14%) than bank mortgages. They're designed as bridge solutions — getting you through a temporary situation until you can qualify for conventional financing.

When Does a Private Mortgage Make Sense?

🏦

Bank Declined

Your bank or credit union said no — due to credit, income, or property type. Private lenders look past the decline and focus on equity.

🌉

Bridge Financing

You bought a new home before selling your current one. A private bridge loan covers the gap until your sale closes.

🏗️

Construction

Building or renovating and need fast capital. Private construction draws fund in days, not weeks.

📋

CRA Arrears

Owe back taxes? CRA can freeze accounts and register liens. A private mortgage pays CRA and stops enforcement action.

⚠️

Power of Sale

Facing foreclosure or power of sale? A private mortgage can stop the process and buy time to sell or refinance properly.

🌍

Foreign Income

You earn income outside Canada that banks won't recognize. Private lenders accept foreign income and assets.

🔑 The Exit Strategy — Why It Matters

Every private mortgage needs an exit strategy — a clear plan for how you'll pay off the private loan and transition to conventional financing. Without one, you risk getting stuck in high-interest debt.

Common exit strategies:

  1. Credit rebuild → B-lender → A-lender: Use the private mortgage period (6–12 months) to improve credit. Then refinance to a B-lender, and eventually back to prime rates.
  2. Property sale: The private loan is repaid when you sell. Common for bridge loans and pre-sale situations.
  3. Income documentation: Use the time to build 2 years of declared income (for self-employed) or establish Canadian credit history (for newcomers).
  4. Refinance after value-add: Complete renovations, get a new appraisal showing higher value, then refinance with an A- or B-lender at a lower rate.
