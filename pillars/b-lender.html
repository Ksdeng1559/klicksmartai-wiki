What is a B Lender? Canada's Complete Guide

Short Answer: A B-lender is a mortgage lender that sits between prime banks (A-lenders) and private lenders. They approve borrowers who don't meet strict bank criteria — self-employed with stated income, bruised credit, or non-traditional income sources. Rates are typically 1–3% higher than bank rates, and terms are usually 1–2 years, designed as a bridge back to A-lending.

B-Lender vs A-Lender vs Private Lender

A-Lender (Prime Banks)
  • Credit Score: 680+
  • Income: Full doc, T4, NOA
  • Rates: 4.5% – 6.5%
  • LTV Max: 80% (uninsured)
  • Terms: 1–5 year fixed/variable
  • Fees: Standard (appraisal, legal)
  • Approval: 5–10 business days
  • Best For: Strong credit, traditional income
B-Lender (Alternative)
  • Credit Score: 550–680
  • Income: Stated, bank statements, NOA
  • Rates: 6.5% – 9.5%
  • LTV Max: 75–80%
  • Terms: 1–2 year fixed
  • Fees: 1% lender fee + broker fee
  • Approval: 3–7 business days
  • Best For: Self-employed, credit rebuilding
Private Lender
  • Credit Score: Any (equity-based)
  • Income: Minimal verification
  • Rates: 8% – 15%+
  • LTV Max: 65–75%
  • Terms: 6–12 months, open
  • Fees: 2–5% lender + broker fees
  • Approval: 1–3 business days
  • Best For: Urgent, short-term bridge

Who Qualifies for a B-Lender Mortgage?

  • Self-employed borrowers who can't show traditional income (stated income or bank statement programs)
  • Bruised credit — scores as low as 550 accepted with sufficient equity
  • Recent consumer proposal or bankruptcy — discharged proposals accepted by most B-lenders
  • Debt consolidation — high-ratio debt consolidation refinances up to 80% LTV
  • New to Canada — limited credit history but strong down payment
  • Non-traditional property types — mixed-use, rural, or unique properties banks decline
  • Commission or seasonal income — variable income that doesn't fit bank formulas

🔑 The B-Lender Exit Strategy

B-lender mortgages are not permanent. They're designed as a 1–2 year bridge. Here's the playbook:

  1. Year 1: Make every payment on time. Rebuild credit. Keep all financial statements organized.
  2. Year 2: Re-apply to A-lenders. With 12+ months of clean payment history and improved credit, most borrowers qualify for prime rates.
  3. Result: You save 2–3% on interest and eliminate lender fees — often $5,000–$15,000/year in savings.

Dennis tracks your exit timeline from day one. Every B-lender file has a built-in transition plan back to A-lending.
