The Self-Employed Mortgage Problem

If you're self-employed, you already know: banks don't understand your income. You might gross $200,000 but show $40,000 on your Notice of Assessment after legitimate business deductions. The bank sees $40,000. You know you can afford a $700,000 mortgage. The bank disagrees.

This is the self-employed income gap — and it's the #1 reason business owners get declined by A-lenders. The solution isn't to pay more tax. It's to work with a broker who knows which lenders use alternative income verification.

3 Qualification Paths for Self-Employed Borrowers

📄

Traditional (Full Doc)

Best for: Self-employed with high declared income

  • 2 years NOA + T1 Generals
  • 2 years business financials
  • Line 150 income used
  • A-lender rates (4.5%–6.5%)
  • Up to 80% LTV

Works if you declare enough income. Most self-employed don't.

✍️

Stated Income

Best for: Established business, low declared income

  • Business license + GST number
  • 6–12 months bank statements
  • Stated income (reasonable to industry)
  • B-lender rates (6.5%–8.5%)
  • Up to 75% LTV

Most popular path. Dennis's specialty.

🏦

Bank Statement Program

Best for: High gross deposits, minimal deductions

  • 12 months business bank statements
  • Gross deposits averaged
  • 50% expense ratio applied
  • B-lender rates (7%–9%)
  • Up to 75% LTV

Ideal for contractors, trades, cash businesses.

📊 Good News for Incorporated Owners

If you operate through a corporation, you have options that sole proprietors don't. Some A-lenders will consider retained earnings in your corporation as available income — even if you didn't pay it out to yourself. Others will look at corporate financial statements rather than personal NOAs. Dennis specializes in structuring mortgage applications for incorporated professionals (doctors, dentists, lawyers, consultants) and business owners who maximize tax efficiency through their corporation.

A-lenders: Typically 2 years of self-employment history with filed tax returns. Some will accept 1 year if you were previously in the same industry as an employee.

B-lenders: As little as 6 months of self-employment with a demonstrated business track record. Stated income programs are more flexible on history length — they care more about current cash flow than historical filings.

Private lenders: No minimum — they lend based on equity, not income history.
