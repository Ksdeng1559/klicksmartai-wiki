What is a Debt Consolidation Mortgage?

A debt consolidation mortgage is a refinance of your existing mortgage that pays out high-interest debts — credit cards, car loans, personal loans, lines of credit — and rolls them into a single, lower-interest mortgage payment. Instead of juggling 4–5 payments at 10–25% interest, you make one payment at mortgage rates.

This isn't a separate product. It's a strategic use of your home equity through one of three mechanisms: a mortgage refinance, a Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC), or a second mortgage. The right choice depends on your current mortgage terms, penalty exposure, and how much equity you've built.

How Much Equity Do You Need?

To consolidate debt through a mortgage refinance, you generally need at least 20% equity in your home after the refinance. Here's how it works:

  • 20% minimum equity — You must retain at least 20% equity post-refinance to avoid CMHC insurance on the new loan.
  • Up to 80% LTV — Most A-lenders cap refinances at 80% loan-to-value. B-lenders may go to 75–80%.
  • Example: Home worth $800,000 with a $400,000 mortgage = 50% equity. You could refinance up to $640,000 (80% LTV), freeing $240,000 to consolidate debt.
  • Less than 20% equity? A HELOC or second mortgage may still be viable. Dennis can assess your specific numbers in a free consultation.

3 Ways to Consolidate Debt Using Your Home

🏠

Mortgage Refinance

Best for: Large debt loads, existing mortgage near renewal

  • Replace current mortgage with larger one
  • Payout all debts at closing
  • One payment, lowest rate
  • Timeline: 2–4 weeks
  • Potential prepayment penalty on existing mortgage

Highest savings. Best if your mortgage term is ending soon.

💳

HELOC (Home Equity Line of Credit)

Best for: Moderate debt, want to keep current mortgage

  • Second-position line of credit
  • Pay interest only on what you use
  • Rates: Prime + 0.5% – 2%
  • Timeline: 2–3 weeks
  • Reusable — pay down and re-access

Flexible. Good if you want ongoing access to equity.

📋

Second Mortgage

Best for: Locked into low-rate first mortgage, urgent need

  • Fixed-term loan behind first mortgage
  • Rates: 8% – 14% (private/B-lender)
  • Timeline: 1–2 weeks
  • No disruption to first mortgage
  • Shorter term (6–24 months)

Fastest option. Higher rate but preserves your low first mortgage rate.
