## 1. Economic Profile

### Growth Leadership
- Over the 2012–2017 period, **Washington posted the strongest real GDP growth** of any U.S. state; **BC posted the strongest growth** of any Canadian province.
- Among all 60 North American jurisdictions (50 states + 10 provinces), the top three fastest-growing were: **Washington, California, and British Columbia** (BCBC, 2018).
- Washington's 2017 real GDP growth: **4.4%** — strongest of any U.S. state (repeat of 2016's 3.9%).
- BC's 2017 real GDP growth: **3.9%** — led all provinces for the third consecutive year.

### Trade Orientation
- Washington's international export-to-GDP ratio: **~16%** — among the highest of any U.S. state. Most states average ~6.7%.
- Washington's largest export market: **China**, followed by Canada (#2), Japan (#3).
- BC's largest export market: **USA**, with China as #2 and Japan #3.
- **If BC were an independent country**, it would be Washington's **3rd largest export market** (after China and Japan).
- **Washington exports more to BC than to all other Canadian provinces combined.**
- **BC exports more to Washington than to any U.S. state.**
- 90% of northbound truck crossings at the border are destined for the Lower Mainland (BC). Nearly 60% of southbound trucks are destined for Whatcom County or the Puget Sound region.
- The four border crossings in the corridor handle **>80% of all B.C.-Washington vehicle crossings**.

### Sector Alignment
Both jurisdictions share similar economic structure:
- Strong technology sectors
- Substantial forest products
- Expanding agricultural sectors
- Large port gateways connecting to overseas markets
- Growing biotech sectors
- Abundant hydroelectric resources
- Dynamic film and television industries

## 2. The Housing Crisis

### Scale of the Problem
- Home prices and rents across Cascadia have risen **2–4x faster than incomes** since 2019.
- The share of housing stock affordable to households earning **<120% AMI** in Washington and Oregon fell from **80–85% (2019) → 45% (2024)** — a dramatic collapse.
- Cascadia currently faces a **deficit of at least 250,000 housing units**.
- By 2050, an additional **3–4 million people** will call Cascadia home.
- To meet 20-year demand, Cascadia needs to build approximately **3 million new housing units**; current trajectory misses this target by ~1 million units.

### Jurisdiction-Specific Shortfalls (next 20 years)
| Jurisdiction | Housing Unit Shortfall |
|---|---|
| Washington | ~400,000 |
| Oregon | ~200,000 |
| British Columbia | ~300,000 |
| **Cascadia Total** | **~900,000+ gap** |

## 3. Grand Boulevard Strategy

### Concept
Focus higher-density mixed-use development along **underutilized strip commercial arterials** (4–6 lane roads) across the 285-mile Cascadia corridor (Portland → Vancouver BC).

### Land Inventory
- Using UrbanFootprint mapping: **>42,000 acres** of qualifying non-residential commercial land fronting 4–6 lane arterials identified.
- These lands span approximately **2,000 miles of existing arterials** across the study area.
- If built out under Form-Based Code (3–6 stories mixed-use/mixed-income): potential capacity of **4.6 million units** over the long term.
- Using a "building-to-land value ratio < 1" filter to identify likely near-term redevelopment parcels: approximately **20–30% of qualifying parcels**, or **~13,000 acres**, are high-probability redevelopment candidates.

### Potential Unit Production by Metro
| Metro Area | New Units (near-term potential) |
|---|---|
| Seattle Region | ~670,000 |
| Portland Region | ~400,000 |
| Metro Vancouver (BC) | ~370,000 |
| **Cascadia Total** | **~1.4 million** |

### Cost Savings from Reform
- Streamlined entitlement (as-of-right zoning) can save up to **$48K/unit**.
- Eliminated/changed parking requirements can save up to **$50K per structured parking stall**.
- Combined savings (entitlement + parking): **~$90,000 per unit** on average.

### Key Policy Ingredients
1. **As-of-Right Zoning** — allows range of mixed-income, mixed-use housing by-right
2. **Form-Based Codes** — establish massing/height; don't over-regulate housing diversity
3. **Parking Reform** — eliminate mandatory minimums, allow market-driven ratios
4. **Fast-Track Permitting** — streamline entitlements for new housing
5. **Workforce Housing Focus** — 80–120% AMI, with inclusionary affordable housing below 80% AMI
6. **Targeted Subsidies** — tax incentives, finance insurance, impact fee reductions where needed

### Benefits Beyond Housing
- Arterials naturally sit on high ground — avoids flood/fire climate hazard zones
- Higher density along corridors enhances transit viability (240 miles of wide 6-lane arterials could accommodate BRT)
- Bikeway, landscaping, and sidewalk improvements possible along 1,700 miles of 4-lane facilities
- Reduced auto dependency and GHG emissions by locating housing near jobs/services/transit

## 4. Whatcom County Master-Planned Community

> Developer pitch document — distinct from the BCBC/Cascadia policy reports above. Presents a specific investment opportunity.

- **Project scope:** $50B investment over 25–30 years
- **Residents:** 20,000+ (single integrated community)
- **Location:** Whatcom County, WA (north of Bellingham, near Blaine/Canadian border)

### Key Components
1. **Residential** — single-family, multifamily, affordable housing
2. **2,000-acre Industrial Park** — technology, logistics, advanced industries
3. **Cascadia Rail Integration** — connecting Seattle, Bellingham, Blaine, Surrey
4. **IT & Financial Hubs** — dedicated zones for multinational corporations
5. **Commercial/Retail** — shopping malls, office towers, entertainment districts
6. **Tourism & Hospitality** — world-class resort + iconic convention center
7. **Recreation & Education** — parks, schools, community center, green spaces
8. **Healthcare** — hospital, daycares, senior housing, essential services

### Alignment with Other Reports
- Cascadia Rail connectivity as a major economic catalyst (referenced in BCBC 2018 and housing reports)
- Whatcom County as a strategic cross-border node (BCBC: 60% of southbound trucks destined for Whatcom County or Puget Sound)
- Industrial park and housing development aligning with regional workforce housing needs

## 5. Cross-Cutting Themes

- **Public-Private Partnership** — essential to financing, entitlement reform, and implementation across both U.S. and Canadian jurisdictions
- **Asia Orientation** — both BC and Washington are more Asia-facing than their national counterparts; China is top export market for Washington, #2 for BC
- **Climate Resilience** — new development along arterials avoids hazard zones; green infrastructure emphasized in Whatcom MPC plans
- **Workforce Housing Gap** — the crisis is specifically acute for 80–120% AMI households; existing affordable stock has collapsed from 80–85% → 45% in 5 years
- **Regional Governance** — the Cascadia Innovation Corridor provides a bilateral (BC-WA-OR) framework for coordinated action; co-chaired by Challenge Seattle (Gregoire) and BCBC (Jones) as of 2024
