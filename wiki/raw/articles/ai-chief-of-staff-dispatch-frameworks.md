# 28 Dispatch Delegation Frameworks That Replace a $150K Executive Hire

By Jonathan Peslar | Founder, LeadPanther
Word Count: 4,600+ | Reading Time: ~18 minutes

## Core Insight

Telling an LLM to 'act as my assistant' is lazy and doesn't work. The AI averages every assistant on the internet — you get the exact middle of a Fortune 500 Executive Assistant and a college intern. Average creates more manual work.

**The real gap:** Chatbot users tinker and build conversations. Operators build systems and get ROI.

## The 4-Element Delegation Formula

1. **Context** — Define your exact business reality. Don't assume the AI knows your pipeline or target market.
2. **Constraint** — Most critical. Real experts work with limits. Give it a strict budget, timeline, scope. Without constraints, AI hallucinates success.
3. **Format** — Never ask for 'an analysis' or 'some ideas.' Demand a specific structured output: a 2-page executive memo, a .csv file, an exact email draft.
4. **Verification** — Do not trust the AI blindly. Force it to confirm its own accuracy before delivering the final result.

### Copy-Paste Initialization Template

```
CONTEXT:
You are my Chief of Staff. We are a [Niche] company selling to [Target Audience].
Here is the background on the task: [Insert Detail]

CONSTRAINT:
You have a strict limit of [Time/Budget/Scope].
Do not use external web sources. Only use the local files provided.

FORMAT:
Output the final result as a [Spreadsheet/Memo/Draft].
Structure it exactly like this:
- [Heading 1]
- [Heading 2]

VERIFICATION:
Before finalizing, audit your work against the constraints.
If you missed anything, revise before delivering the file.
```

## Claude Dispatch Overview

Not a chat window — a single persistent conversation thread that syncs between phone and desktop. Think of it as a remote employee who lives on your desktop but texts you updates.

**Key capabilities:**
- Phone-to-desktop delegation: text from mobile, work happens on your computer
- Computer use: opens apps, clicks buttons, updates spreadsheets, scrapes competitor pricing
- Persistent memory: no more re-explaining your business model every morning
- Scheduled tasks: daily CRM hygiene, weekly audits, monthly reconcilations — runs while you sleep
- Direct file access: organizes messy folders, extracts data, builds archives

**Requirements:**
- Claude Desktop app + Claude mobile app
- Pro or Max plan
- Computer must be powered on and awake

**Setup:** 4 minutes — toggle Local File Access + Computer Use to ON.

## 28 Frameworks by Category

### Part 3: Revenue & Sales Frameworks (5)

**Framework 1: The Revenue Pulse**
- Daily/weekly revenue check
- Pull MRR, churn rate, new customer count, compare to previous period
- Flag anomalies. Deliver one-page executive MRR snapshot.
- Schedule at 7 AM for daily visibility.

**Framework 2: The Pipeline Surgeon**
- Weekly pipeline review
- Audit all active deals, find stalled opportunities (zero activity in 7 days)
- Draft personalized re-engagement emails for each stalled deal.
- Pair with Follow-Up Machine for automated sequences.

**Framework 3: The Proposal Builder**
- New client opportunity
- Search email + Slack for prior communication with [Client Name]
- Pull meeting notes. Draft structured proposal and SOW.
- Save best proposals as templates for Claude's memory.

**Framework 4: The Pricing Auditor**
- Quarterly pricing review or before major pitch
- Research [Competitor 1], [Competitor 2], [Competitor 3] pricing pages
- Build comparison matrix, recommend specific adjustments.
- Run before every enterprise pitch.

**Framework 5: The Churn Predictor**
- Monthly churn prevention
- Scan support tickets, product usage patterns, payment history
- Flag at-risk accounts with recommended interventions.
- Set as weekly scheduled task.

### Part 4: Content & Marketing Frameworks (5)

**Framework 6: The Content Autopilot**
- Weekly content planning
- Analyze top 10 performing posts from last 30 days
- Identify patterns. Generate next week's content calendar.
- Draft 5-7 post concepts with strong hooks.
- Schedule Friday at 3 PM.

**Framework 7: The Repurposing Engine**
- After strong post engagement
- Repurpose into: Twitter thread, newsletter section, LinkedIn carousel outline, 60-second video script.
- Run within 24 hours of viral post — momentum matters.

**Framework 8: The Engagement Radar**
- Daily morning routine
- Scan comments on last 5 posts. Identify high-value commenters (founders, decision-makers).
- Draft personalized replies + DM openers.
- Focus on commenters who asked questions — warmest leads.

**Framework 9: The Competitor Content Tracker**
- Weekly competitive intel
- Visit LinkedIn profiles of [Competitor 1], [Competitor 2], [Competitor 3]
- Pull latest posts, analyze topics/hooks/engagement.
- Identify content gaps to exploit.
- Track same 5 competitors for 4 weeks to see their playbook.

**Framework 10: The Lead Magnet Drafter**
- Creating new lead magnet
- Read 3 source articles on [Topic] for [Target Audience]
- Produce detailed outline. Then write full first draft section by section.
- Always provide source material — AI writing without sources is just guessing.

### Part 5: Inbox & Communication Frameworks (5)

**Framework 11: The Inbox Zero Protocol**
- Daily morning routine
- Scan inbox, categorize all unread: Urgent / Respond Later / FYI / Archive
- Draft short direct replies to urgent ones based on previous communication style.
- Schedule at 6:30 AM.

**Framework 12: The Slack Firefighter**
- After being offline 2+ hours
- Surface threads where explicitly mentioned or waiting on approval
- Draft brief responses to unblock team. Ignore casual chatter.
- Run after deep work blocks or back-to-back meetings.

**Framework 13: The Cold Email Responder**
- Inbound lead emails
- Review inbound email from [Name]. Research on LinkedIn + company website.
- Assess against ICP. Draft personalized response referencing their specific situation.
- Run within 1 hour of receiving — speed kills deals.

**Framework 14: The Meeting Prep Brief**
- 30 minutes before any call
- Meet with [Name] from [Company]
- Provide one-page briefing: background, recent company news, shared connections, 3 talking points from past email history.
- Include: 'Find anything they've said publicly about [Your Industry]' for instant rapport.

**Framework 15: The Follow-Up Machine**
- After meetings
- Check calendar for meetings in last 48 hours
- Cross-reference with sent email folder for anyone not followed up
- Draft follow-up emails with proposed action items.
- Schedule as daily 5 PM routine.

### Part 6: Client Management Frameworks (5)

**Framework 16: The Client Health Scorecard**
- Weekly client check (especially with 3+ active clients)
- Scan Slack messages, emails, meeting notes from last 7 days
- Score each client: Green / Yellow / Red (sentiment, response time, open action items)
- Red = call today. Yellow = check in this week.

**Framework 17: The Deliverable Tracker**
- Mid-week project status check
- Scan project folders for pending vs completed deliverables
- Identify items past deadline.
- Constraint: 'only flag items more than 2 days overdue' filters noise.

**Framework 18: The Onboarding Packet**
- New client signs
- Client name: [Name], industry: [Industry], service type: [Service]
- Compile welcome doc: intake questions, project timeline template, communication preferences form.
- Build once, iterate forever — Claude remembers templates.

**Framework 19: The QBR Builder**
- Quarterly business review prep
- Pull 90 days of communication, deliverables, metrics, wins for [Client Name]
- Draft structured QBR presentation.
- Schedule on the 25th of the last month of each quarter.

**Framework 20: The Escalation Detector**
- Scheduled daily or every 48 hours
- Scan all client communications for frustration signals, repeated requests, negative language, escalation phrases like 'still waiting.'
- Early warning alert with flagged messages + recommended responses.
- Prevents 90% of surprise cancellations.

### Part 7: Operations & File Management Frameworks (4)

**Framework 21: The File Organizer**
- Weekly desktop cleanup
- Scan Downloads and Desktop folders
- Sort into project folders by type (PDFs, images, spreadsheets, docs)
- Rename with consistent naming convention.
- Set rule: 'YYYY-MM-DD_ClientName_Description' in Claude's memory.

**Framework 22: The Data Consolidator**
- Monthly reporting or multi-source analysis
- Pull from [Source 1], [Source 2], [Source 3]
- Merge into one clean spreadsheet with consistent formatting + source attribution.
- Specify column headers so Claude matches format exactly.

**Framework 23: The SOP Writer**
- Right after completing a new process
- Rough steps: [Dump steps]
- Formalize into standard operating procedure with tool references, screenshot locations, decision trees.
- Write for someone who has never done this task before — forces thoroughness.

**Framework 24: The Invoice Processor**
- Monthly bookkeeping
- Scan inbox for invoices received this month
- Extract vendor name, amount, date, category.
- Compile into tracking spreadsheet.
- Schedule on the 1st of each month.

### Part 8: Strategic & Weekly Review Frameworks (4)

**Framework 25: The Weekly Review**
- Sunday evening or Monday morning
- Compile this week's metrics: revenue, content performance, client status
- List wins and blockers. Generate top 3 priorities for next week.
- Most valuable scheduled task — set for Sunday at 7 PM.

**Framework 26: The Decision Memo**
- Any time you face a strategic decision
- Here is the decision context: [Context]
- Research both sides, present pros/cons, make recommendation, include risk assessment.
- Include: 'assume I have [budget/timeline]' to force practical recommendations.

**Framework 27: The Competitive Intel Brief**
- Weekly or before strategic planning session
- Scan websites, LinkedIn profiles, public announcements from last 7 days for 5 competitors: [List]
- Summarize their moves and threats.
- Track exact same competitors for 4 weeks to spot hidden patterns.

**Framework 28: The Goal Tracker**
- Monthly progress check
- Here are my quarterly OKRs/goals: [List]
- Check progress, calculate completion %, flag what's off-track with specific reasons.
- Pair with Decision Memo when a goal is significantly off-track.

## Key Principles

**Train The Memory:** Feed Claude your brand voice, pricing sheets, SOPs. Tell it how you like data formatted. It remembers — you never explain yourself twice.

**Rule of Three Delegations:** Manual task once = fine, twice = take notes, three times = build a framework. Never repeat rote work. Turn it into a prompt.

**Automated Chains:** Single prompts are good. Chains are better. Combine frameworks: Revenue Pulse → Churn Predictor → Escalation Detector. One click Monday morning. Total visibility.
