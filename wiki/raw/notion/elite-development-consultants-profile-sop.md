## Core value proposition
Elite Development Consultants USA helps property owners, investors, developers, lenders, and project sponsors reduce execution risk from early feasibility through construction and closeout. The firm should be positioned as the client’s independent project advocate—coordinating professionals, monitoring scope, schedule, budget, and risk, and helping ownership make timely, documented decisions.
## Ideal client profile
### Priority client segments
1. **Private real estate developers** pursuing multifamily, mixed-use, hospitality, retail, industrial, or infill projects.
2. **Landowners and family offices** that control property but lack an internal development team.
3. **Out-of-state and international investors** entering California or other U.S. markets and requiring local oversight.
4. **Lenders, debt funds, and equity partners** needing independent project monitoring or troubled-project review.
5. **General contractors and design firms** requiring owner-representation, preconstruction, entitlement, or project controls support.
6. **Nonprofits, faith organizations, and community groups** developing housing, community facilities, or mixed-use assets.

## Buyer personas
### Developer principal
**Goal:** Deliver a profitable project without losing control of cost, schedule, or consultants.  
**Pain:** Too many moving parts, weak reporting, delayed decisions, and unreliable budgets.  
**Message:** “We act as your owner-side development partner and give you disciplined control over risk, schedule, budget, and accountability.”
### Family office or private investor
**Goal:** Protect invested capital and gain objective visibility.

**Message:** “We provide independent oversight and decision-ready reporting so your capital is protected throughout execution.”

**Goal:** Identify project risk early and preserve collateral value.  
**Pain:** Incomplete reporting, delayed draws, cost-to-complete uncertainty, and sponsor underperformance.  
**Message:** “We provide independent project review, construction monitoring, and early-warning reporting.”
### Landowner without a development team
**Goal:** Convert land value into a viable development opportunity.  
**Pain:** Uncertainty around highest-and-best use, approvals, consultants, costs, and capital.  
**Message:** “We organize the path from property concept to executable development plan.”

## Recommended entry offers

A fixed-scope review of the site, concept, approvals, preliminary economics, team, schedule, and major risks. Deliverable: executive scorecard and 90-day action plan.
### Project Risk Diagnostic
A rapid review for delayed, over-budget, or underperforming projects. Deliverable: risk register, cost-to-complete observations, recovery priorities, and owner action plan.
### Owner’s Representative Advisory Retainer
Monthly strategic oversight for owners who need senior development leadership without hiring a full internal team.
### Lender Construction Monitoring Package
Independent milestone, draw, schedule, budget, and risk reporting for lenders and capital partners.
## Client-acquisition SOP
### SOP 1 — Market and account selection
**Objective:** Build a focused list of organizations with active development needs.
1. Select one geography and one asset class for each campaign.
2. Identify 50–100 target accounts using permit activity, planning agendas, development announcements, acquisitions, financing events, and project distress signals.
3. Score each account:
	- Project activity: 0–5
	- Estimated project value: 0–5
	- Need for external expertise: 0–5
	- Access to decision-maker: 0–5
	- Urgency signal: 0–5
4. Prioritize accounts scoring 18 or higher.
5. Record source, evidence, likely need, contact, and next action.
### SOP 2 — Prospect research brief
For every priority account, prepare a one-page brief containing:
- Company and decision-maker
- Active or planned project
- Location and asset class
- Current stage
- Known consultants, contractors, lenders, or partners
- Likely risks or bottlenecks
- Relevant Elite service
- Personalized opening hypothesis
- Credibility proof required
- Recommended call to action
### SOP 3 — Outreach sequence
**Day 1:** Personalized email tied to a specific project signal.  
**Day 3:** LinkedIn connection or professional follow-up.  
**Day 6:** Send one useful observation, checklist, or risk question.  
**Day 10:** Call or voicemail.  
**Day 15:** Share a short relevant case-style insight.  
**Day 21:** Close-the-loop message and place into nurture.
### SOP 4 — Discovery call
Use the following structure:
1. What project or property is the client working on?
2. What stage is it currently in?
3. What outcome is ownership trying to achieve?
4. What is creating the greatest uncertainty or delay?
5. Who is on the team, and where are the accountability gaps?
6. What are the current budget, schedule, approval, and capital constraints?
7. What happens if the issue is not resolved in the next 30–90 days?
8. What decision must be made next?
9. Agree on a defined diagnostic, assessment, or advisory scope.
### SOP 5 — Qualification
Qualify opportunities using:
- **Project:** Real project with identifiable property or development scope
- **Problem:** Material cost, schedule, approval, execution, reporting, or oversight issue
- **Power:** Access to owner, sponsor, lender, or authorized executive
- **Priority:** Clear timeline or triggering event
- **Payment:** Budget and willingness to engage professional consulting
- **Proof:** Information sufficient to define the first deliverable
### SOP 6 — Proposal
Every proposal should include:
- Client situation and desired outcome
- Scope and exclusions
- Deliverables
- Information required from client
- Timeline and milestones
- Roles and communication cadence
- Fixed fee, retainer, or milestone pricing
- Assumptions and change-control process
- Acceptance and kickoff steps
### SOP 7 — Onboarding
Within two business days of engagement:
1. Confirm contract and initial payment.
2. Create project workspace and contact list.
3. Request documents using a standard checklist.
4. Establish weekly meeting and reporting cadence.
5. Create initial decision log, risk register, milestone schedule, and action tracker.
6. Deliver a 14-day mobilization plan.
### SOP 8 — Weekly project reporting
Each report should contain:
- Executive status: green, yellow, or red
- Decisions required from ownership
- Schedule status and milestone variance
- Budget, commitments, changes, and forecast
- Entitlement or permit status
- Top five risks and mitigations
- Consultant and contractor actions
- Next seven- and thirty-day priorities
