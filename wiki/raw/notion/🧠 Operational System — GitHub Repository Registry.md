# Purpose
This workspace is the system of record for GitHub repositories, frameworks, agent skills, workflow tools, and reference architectures that may contribute to DennisOS, KlickSmartAI, LeadSniperAI, Mortgage CoPilot, and related operating systems.
# How to use this registry
1. Add every potentially useful GitHub repository to the catalogue.
2. Record the business capability it supports—not only its technical category.
3. Assign an adoption decision: **Inbox**, **Researching**, **Shortlisted**, **Pilot**, **Adopted**, **Reference Only**, or **Rejected**.
4. Capture the specific component worth reusing so the team does not repeatedly re-read entire repositories.
5. Link each repository to the product or operating-system layer it can improve.
6. Review shortlisted repositories monthly and adopted dependencies quarterly.
# Evaluation standard
Evaluate each repository on strategic fit, implementation effort, maturity, security, extensibility, documentation quality, maintenance activity, licensing, and time-to-value.

# Gemini Notebook Deep Research Layer
Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) should operate above the scraping and discovery stack as the **domain intelligence, synthesis, and content-production layer**.
## Role in the operating system
- Create one notebook for each domain, market, client, campaign, or strategic research question.
- Load curated outputs from Scrapling, Crawl4AI, Crawlee, Firecrawl, Exa, Parallel, government sources, uploaded documents, websites, videos, Google Docs, and Google Slides.
- Use Gemini Deep Research to expand the evidence base with current web research and, where appropriate, connected Drive or Gmail sources.
- Use Gemini Notebook for source-grounded analysis, cross-source comparison, citations, themes, contradictions, gaps, and reusable knowledge.
- Convert research into briefings, reports, mind maps, data tables, audio or video overviews, infographics, slide decks, FAQs, content briefs, and campaign assets.
## Recommended workflow
1. **Define the domain:** target market, business problem, geography, audience, decision, and desired outputs.
2. **Discover sources:** Exa, Parallel, Serper, government APIs, industry sources, and Gemini Deep Research.
3. **Collect evidence:** Scrapling, Crawl4AI, Crawlee, Firecrawl, Playwright, approved files, and connected Google sources.
4. **Curate and normalize:** remove duplicates, label source type, capture dates, assess credibility, and preserve source URLs.
5. **Create the domain notebook:** group the most useful sources into a project-specific Gemini Notebook.
6. **Generate intelligence:** market structure, competitors, customers, pain points, trends, opportunities, risks, funding signals, search demand, and GTM implications.
7. **Create content:** executive briefs, research reports, website copy, SEO/AEO briefs, social content, outreach messaging, sales enablement, presentations, and knowledge-base material.
8. **Operationalize findings:** send approved outputs to Notion, Supabase, CRM, n8n, website publishing, outreach, and strategy agents.
9. **Refresh:** rerun collection and research on a scheduled or signal-triggered basis and record what changed.
## Grounding model
- **Gemini Notebook:** use when answers must stay grounded exclusively in the selected notebook sources.
- **Gemini Deep Research:** use when the task requires current web research, broader discovery, or additional connected Google sources.
- **Combined workflow:** use Deep Research to discover and draft the research base, then curate authoritative sources into Gemini Notebook for repeatable, source-grounded intelligence and content production.

## Governance
- Preserve original source links and dates.
- Separate source facts from AI interpretations and recommendations.
- Prefer primary and authoritative sources for high-impact decisions.
- Review sensitive, legal, financial, or client-facing conclusions before publication.
- Do not treat a notebook as a permanent database; export approved findings to the system of record.
- Track notebook owner, research question, source freshness, confidence, intended audience, and next review date.
