# Source: Notion (page 3a49e94c-f0a4-8153-8b04-f623b3353385)

## Purpose
A reusable digital marketing program for any local service business, professional practice, contractor, clinic, broker, advisor, home-service company, or location-based brand.
The system converts business facts and market evidence into a measurable growth program:
**Research → Intelligence → Strategy → Campaigns → Lead Generation → Sales Conversion → Measurement → Optimization**
---
## 1. Program outcomes
The program is designed to produce five business outcomes:
1. Increase qualified local visibility.
2. Generate more calls, forms, bookings, assessments, and quote requests.
3. Improve lead quality and sales conversion.
4. Build a reusable content and campaign engine.
5. connect marketing activity to pipeline, revenue, and customer value.
### Core success metrics
- Qualified leads per month
- Cost per qualified lead
- Lead-to-appointment rate
- Appointment-to-sale rate
- Customer acquisition cost
- Marketing-sourced revenue
- Revenue by service and location
- Local search visibility
- Google Business Profile actions
- Organic landing-page conversions
- Review volume, rating, and response rate
- Repeat and referral revenue
---
## 2. Universal engagement model
### Phase 0 — Qualification and readiness
Before launching a campaign, verify that the business can convert demand.
#### Readiness checklist
- Clear services and service areas
- Working phone, forms, booking, and email routing
- Response time target under five minutes during business hours
- Named lead owner
- CRM or lead-tracking system
- Reliable fulfillment capacity
- Review request process
- Ability to identify booked and closed revenue
- Advertising and content approval owner
- Compliance requirements documented
#### Go / conditional go / no-go decision
**Go:** offer, capacity, tracking, and response process are ready.
**Conditional go:** demand can be generated, but conversion or tracking gaps must be corrected in parallel.
**No-go:** the business cannot answer leads, fulfil work, substantiate claims, or measure outcomes.

### Evidence classification
Every material statement should be tagged as one of:
- **Verified fact** — supported by business records or an authoritative source
- **Market evidence** — supported by search, review, competitor, or demand data
- **Customer evidence** — supported by interviews, CRM, calls, reviews, or surveys
- **Working assumption** — plausible but unverified
- **Opinion** — stakeholder preference or interpretation
Do not convert assumptions into public claims without verification.

## 21. Expanded delivery methodology
The operating system should be delivered as a controlled sequence of evidence gathering, strategic decisions, production, activation, and optimization. Each phase has defined inputs, outputs, owners, and exit criteria.
### Phase 1 — Business discovery and baseline
**Objective:** establish the commercial reality of the company before recommending channels or campaigns.
**Inputs**
- Owner and stakeholder interviews
- Website, Google Business Profile, CRM, analytics, advertising accounts, and sales records
- Service list, pricing approach, margins, capacity, and delivery constraints
- Existing brand, content, creative, and sales assets
**Process**
1. Document the business model and revenue mechanics.
2. Identify priority services by margin, demand, strategic importance, and delivery capacity.
3. Map the current customer journey from first discovery to payment and repeat purchase.
4. Establish baseline metrics for visibility, leads, appointments, sales, revenue, reviews, and response time.
5. Record all known operational constraints.
6. Separate verified facts from stakeholder beliefs and assumptions.
**Outputs**
- Verified company profile
- Baseline scorecard
- Current-state customer journey
- Service economics summary
- Constraint and risk register
- Initial measurement gaps
**Exit criteria**
- At least one measurable business objective is approved.
- Priority services and locations are selected.
- A lead owner and revenue owner are identified.
- Critical tracking gaps have an assigned remediation plan.
### Phase 2 — Evidence collection
**Objective:** build a dependable evidence base before deciding what to market.
**Evidence streams**
- Search demand and SERP evidence
- Competitor and category evidence
- Customer, CRM, sales-call, and review evidence
- Local market and geographic evidence
- Operational and financial evidence
- Channel performance evidence
**Evidence record standard**
Every material finding should include:
- Finding
- Source
- Collection date
- Geography
- Time period
- Evidence type
- Confidence level
- Business implication
- Recommended next action
- Owner
**Confidence scale**
- **High:** verified across authoritative or multiple independent sources
- **Medium:** supported by one credible source or a consistent directional pattern
- **Low:** limited, indirect, outdated, or assumption-based evidence
**Evidence quality test**
Ask:
- Is the source relevant to the target geography and service?
- Is the evidence recent enough for the decision?
- Does it measure real demand, stated interest, or only visibility?
- Can the claim be reproduced or independently checked?
- Is there conflicting evidence?
- What decision would change if the evidence is wrong?
### Phase 3 — Diagnosis and opportunity mapping
**Objective:** convert raw evidence into prioritized business opportunities.
**Diagnostic lenses**
- Demand exists but visibility is weak
- Visibility exists but conversion is weak
- Leads exist but qualification is weak
- Appointments exist but sales conversion is weak
- Customers exist but retention, review, or referral capture is weak
- High-value service exists but is under-promoted
- Geographic demand exists but local relevance is weak
- Competitors rank but do not satisfy customer needs well
- Customer questions are not being answered clearly
- Marketing spend exists without reliable attribution
### Opportunity statement template
**Because** \[evidence\], **the business can** \[commercial opportunity\] **by** \[recommended action\], **for** \[priority segment or location\], **measured by** \[target KPI\].
### Opportunity scoring model
Score each opportunity from 1–5 on:
- Demand strength
- Commercial value
- Strategic fit
- Competitive attainability
- Speed to impact
- Evidence confidence
- Execution effort
- Fulfilment capacity
- Measurement readiness
Use the following decision categories:
- **Activate now:** high value, strong evidence, feasible, measurable
- **Test:** promising but uncertain; run a controlled experiment
- **Build foundation:** valuable, but dependencies must be fixed first
- **Monitor:** insufficient urgency or evidence
- **Reject:** poor economics, weak fit, excessive risk, or inability to deliver
**Outputs**
- Opportunity backlog
- Prioritization matrix
- Recommended channel and asset requirements
- Dependency map
- Assumption register

### Channel role framework
Each channel should have one primary role:
- **Capture demand:** search, maps, directories, referral landing pages
- **Create demand:** social, video, partnerships, community, sponsorships
- **Build trust:** reviews, case studies, email, educational content, local proof
- **Convert demand:** landing pages, phone, chat, forms, booking, assessments
- **Nurture demand:** email, SMS, retargeting, remarketing, reactivation
- **Retain and expand:** customer communication, maintenance plans, referrals, upsells
Avoid using a channel without a documented role, audience, offer, and measurement method.
### Phase 5 — Experience and conversion design
**Objective:** ensure that marketing traffic can become qualified opportunities and revenue.
**Conversion path design**
1. Customer recognizes a need.
2. Customer discovers the business.
3. Customer sees a relevant message.
4. Customer receives sufficient proof.
5. Customer accepts a low-friction next step.
6. Business responds within the service-level target.
7. Lead is qualified.
8. Appointment, quote, assessment, or purchase occurs.
9. Revenue outcome is recorded.
10. Review, referral, retention, and expansion workflows begin.
### Landing-page standard
Every campaign landing page should contain:
- Specific audience and service relevance

- Outcome-focused headline
- Concise service explanation
- Credible proof
- Process or next steps
- Objection handling
- Appropriate risk reduction
- Primary call to action
- Secondary call to action only where needed
- Qualification or disqualification information
- Mobile click-to-call
- Fast form or booking flow
- Privacy and compliance disclosures
- Conversion tracking

- Number of form fields

- Booking complexity

4. Create landing page or conversion destination.
5. Create ads, email, SMS, social, and sales-support assets.
6. Implement tracking and routing.
7. Complete QA.
8. Approve launch.
### Asset acceptance criteria
Every asset must be:
- Relevant to one audience and intent
- Consistent with the approved positioning
- Supported by verified claims
- Clear about the next action
- Appropriate for the channel
- Legible on mobile
- Trackable
- Compliant
- Version controlled
- Assigned an owner and review date

- Campaign

### Phase 7 — Launch and operational activation
**Objective:** activate marketing without losing leads through routing, ownership, or tracking failures.
### Pre-launch test
Test the complete journey using desktop and mobile:
- Ad or source link
- Landing page
- Phone call
- Form
- Booking
- Confirmation page
- Email and SMS notifications
- CRM record creation
- Lead-owner assignment
- Response workflow
- Qualification fields
- Revenue attribution
### Launch control rules
- Start with controlled budgets and defined learning goals.
- Avoid changing multiple major variables simultaneously.
- Establish minimum data thresholds before judging performance.
- Use stop-loss rules for tracking failures, compliance concerns, unusable lead quality, or fulfilment problems.
- Record every material campaign change.
### Launch log
Record:
- Launch date
- Version
- Budget
- Audience
- Offer
- Assets
- Tracking status
- Owner
- Known risks
- First review date
- Changes made
- Reason for change

### Phase 8 — Lead operations and sales feedback
**Objective:** connect marketing decisions to actual lead quality and revenue.
### Closed-loop lead process
1. Capture source, campaign, service, and location.
2. Record first-response time.
3. Apply qualification criteria.
4. Record appointment, estimate, proposal, and outcome.
5. Require standardized loss reasons.
6. Attribute revenue and gross profit where possible.
7. Return lead-quality feedback to marketing weekly.
### Lead-quality score
Score 1–5 on:
- Service fit
- Location fit
- Urgency
- Budget or economic fit
- Decision authority
- Timeline
- Contactability
- Readiness for the next step
Separate:
- Raw leads
- Contactable leads
- Qualified leads
- Appointments
- Sales opportunities
- Won customers
Do not optimize campaigns solely for raw lead volume.
### Sales feedback questions
- Which campaigns produce the best-fit customers?
- Which messages create incorrect expectations?
- Which objections occur repeatedly?
- Which services are easiest or hardest to sell?
- Which locations convert best?
- Which lead sources generate higher revenue or margin?
- Why are prospects choosing competitors?
- Which proof assets help close sales?
### Phase 9 — Measurement and optimization
**Objective:** identify what is working, why it is working, and what to change next.
### Measurement framework
**North-star outcome**
Choose one primary business measure, such as qualified revenue opportunities, booked jobs, new patients, funded files, or gross profit from marketing-sourced customers.
**Driver metrics**
- Qualified visibility
- Landing-page visits
- Conversion rate
- Contact rate
- Qualification rate
- Appointment rate
- Close rate
- Average revenue
- Gross margin
- Repeat and referral rate
**Guardrail metrics**
- Lead complaints
- Refunds
- Unqualified lead rate
- Response time
- Fulfilment capacity
- Brand or compliance issues
- Customer satisfaction
### Diagnostic sequence
When performance is below target, diagnose in order:
1. Tracking accuracy
2. Demand and audience quality
3. Offer relevance
4. Message clarity
5. Landing-page conversion
6. Lead routing and response time
7. Qualification
8. Sales process
9. Pricing or commercial fit
10. Fulfilment and customer experience
Do not assume that poor campaign results are always a media-buying problem.
### Optimization decision rules
- **Scale:** target exceeded, lead quality confirmed, capacity available
- **Maintain:** target achieved, but evidence is not yet sufficient for scaling
- **Iterate:** directional promise with a specific identifiable weakness
- **Pause:** tracking, quality, compliance, capacity, or economic failure
- **Retire:** repeated failure after meaningful tests or strategic misalignment
### Experiment methodology
Every experiment must define:
- Hypothesis
- Evidence supporting the hypothesis
- Variable being changed
- Control or comparison
- Audience
- Primary metric
- Guardrails
- Minimum sample or decision window
- Result
- Decision
- Learning to preserve
### Phase 10 — Institutional learning and renewal
**Objective:** make each campaign improve the company's long-term marketing intelligence.
At the end of every cycle:
- Record validated customer insights.
- Update objections and FAQs.
- Preserve winning offers and messages.
- Archive failed tactics with reasons.
- Update keyword and SERP evidence.
- Refresh competitor observations.
- Update service economics and capacity.
- Add sales and customer-experience learnings.
- Reprioritize the opportunity backlog.

## 22. Stage-gate governance
Each phase should end with a formal decision.
### Gate 1 — Readiness approval
Approve when:
- The business can respond to and fulfil new demand.
- Priority services and locations are confirmed.
- Tracking and ownership are adequate or have a remediation plan.
### Gate 2 — Evidence approval
Approve when:
- Material findings have sources and confidence ratings.
- Search, competitor, customer, and business evidence are sufficiently represented.
- Major assumptions and conflicts are documented.
### Gate 3 — Strategy approval
Approve when:
- Audience, offer, positioning, channels, conversion path, and targets are explicit.
- The strategy fits capacity, economics, and compliance requirements.
### Gate 4 — Production approval
Approve when:
- Campaign and creative briefs are complete.
- Claims and source material are approved.
- Required landing pages, tracking, routing, and nurture assets are assigned.
### Gate 5 — Launch approval
Approve when:
- The entire journey has been tested.
- Lead ownership and follow-up are active.
- Dashboards and stop-loss rules are ready.
### Gate 6 — Scale approval
Approve when:
- Lead quality and revenue outcomes are confirmed.
- Fulfilment capacity is available.
- Marginal economics remain acceptable.

## 23. Roles and accountability
### Recommended roles
**Business owner or executive sponsor**
- Approves objectives, budget, positioning, claims, and strategic priorities
**Program strategist**
- Owns research synthesis, diagnosis, strategy, opportunity prioritization, and performance recommendations
**Local search and SEO lead**
- Owns keyword evidence, SERP analysis, site architecture, local pages, Google Business Profile, and organic visibility
**Campaign manager**
- Owns briefs, production coordination, launches, budgets, tests, and optimization
**Content and creative lead**
- Owns content briefs, copy, video, design, brand consistency, and asset library
**CRM and automation lead**
- Owns lead capture, routing, email, SMS, lifecycle workflows, attribution, and data quality
**Sales owner**
- Owns response time, qualification, appointments, proposals, outcomes, and loss reasons
**Data and measurement owner**
- Owns analytics, dashboards, definitions, validation, and reporting
**Compliance or professional reviewer**
- Reviews claims, disclaimers, regulated content, privacy, and channel requirements
### RACI principle
For every major deliverable, identify:
- **Responsible:** completes the work
- **Accountable:** approves the outcome
- **Consulted:** provides expertise or evidence
- **Informed:** receives the decision or result
No campaign should launch without one accountable owner.

## 27. Quality assurance framework
### Research QA
- Sources are documented
- Geography and date are correct
- Assumptions are labelled
- Conflicting evidence is retained
- Search volume is not treated as the only demand indicator
- SERP conclusions use live result evidence
### Strategy QA
- Recommendations connect to evidence
- Commercial economics are considered
- Capacity and operational constraints are considered
- Exclusions and trade-offs are explicit
- KPIs connect to business outcomes
### Content and creative QA
- Claims are verified
- Audience and intent are clear
- Local relevance is genuine
- Content is useful rather than repetitive
- Brand tone is consistent
- Accessibility and mobile readability are reviewed
- Calls to action and tracking are present
### Campaign QA
- Landing page and offer align
- Audience and geography are correct
- Budgets and controls are documented
- Forms, calls, CRM, email, and SMS are tested
- Lead owner is active
- Reporting is operational
### Measurement QA
- Metric definitions are documented
- Duplicate and spam leads are handled
- Qualified lead criteria are consistent
- Revenue attribution is reconciled
- Channel dashboards match source systems
- Decisions are not made from incomplete data without qualification

## 28. Universal 30-60-90 day execution method
### Days 1–30 — Diagnose and establish foundations
- Complete discovery and baseline
- Fix critical tracking and lead-routing failures
- Validate priority services and locations
- Complete market, competitor, search, and customer research
- Establish initial positioning and offers
- Prioritize opportunities
- Improve critical website and Google Business Profile elements
### Days 31–60 — Build and activate
- Produce priority landing pages and content
- Launch review and referral workflows
- Activate email and SMS lead-response sequences
- Launch controlled organic, paid, partnership, or community campaigns
- Train sales and intake teams
- Begin weekly closed-loop reporting
### Days 61–90 — Optimize and scale
- Diagnose funnel performance
- Improve lead quality, response, and conversion
- Run controlled message, offer, landing-page, and audience experiments
- Scale proven campaigns within capacity
- Retire weak tactics
- Create the next 90-day opportunity roadmap

## 29. Core operating principle
The system should never move directly from data to content production without a documented business implication and strategic decision.
The required chain is:
**Evidence → Finding → Business implication → Opportunity → Strategic decision → Campaign or asset → Qualified lead → Revenue outcome → Learning**
This ensures the program functions as a growth operating system rather than a collection of disconnected marketing activities.
