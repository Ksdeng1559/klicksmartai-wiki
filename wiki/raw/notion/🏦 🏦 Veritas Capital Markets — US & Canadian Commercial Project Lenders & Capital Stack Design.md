# 1. Core Principle — Design the Capital Stack Before Choosing the Lender
A commercial project should not be approached as a single-loan problem. Break the capital requirement into layers, determine what risk each layer is funding, and then source the most appropriate capital provider for that layer.
> **Capital Stack Logic:** Project Cost → Senior Debt Capacity → Subordinate / Gap Capital → Sponsor Equity → Remaining Funding Gap.

# 2. Project Financing Lifecycle
Use this sequence to identify which lender universe should be searched.
1. **Raw Land / Acquisition** — purchase of unserviced or partially serviced land.
2. **Entitlement / Pre-development** — zoning, approvals, permits, design, soft costs.
3. **Horizontal Development** — roads, utilities, grading, site servicing, subdivision infrastructure.
4. **Vertical Construction** — buildings / homes / commercial improvements.
5. **Bridge / Lease-up / Stabilization** — financing between completion and permanent takeout.
6. **Permanent Debt / Refinance** — stabilized long-term mortgage or term debt.
> 
	Do not treat all "construction lenders" as interchangeable. Some finance only vertical construction after land is entitled and serviced, while others will finance acquisition, land development, or horizontal improvements.

# 4. Canadian Commercial Project Capital — Primary Categories
The Canadian lender universe should be divided by **capital function**, not simply by lender type.
## A. Schedule I / Institutional Banks
Examples include **BMO, RBC, TD, Canadian Western Bank / National Bank-related platforms, Equitable / EQ, VersaBank and other regulated institutions** represented within the broader Canadian lending ecosystem.
**Best use:** stabilized commercial real estate, strong sponsors, conventional construction, term debt, owner-occupied properties, relationship banking.
**Typical advantages:** lower pricing, longer amortization, stronger permanent financing options.
**Typical limitations:** tighter underwriting, slower execution, greater recourse / covenant requirements, limited appetite for speculative or early-stage development.
## B. Credit Unions / Regional Financial Institutions
Use for local commercial real estate, owner-user transactions, construction and relationship-driven transactions where the institution understands the local market.
**Best use:** smaller-to-mid-market commercial projects and borrowers with strong local relationships.
## C. MICs / Private Mortgage Funds
Examples from the working Canadian lender categorization include **Morex Capital / MIC, CMI Group, CHMIC and other private real-estate credit providers**.
**Best use:** bridge, land, transitional assets, time-sensitive closings, borrowers who fall outside conventional bank criteria.
## D. Commercial / Real Estate Finance Companies
Examples include **CMLS Financial** and other non-bank commercial mortgage platforms.
**Best use:** multifamily, commercial mortgage, term debt, construction or structured real-estate financing depending on program.
## E. Private Credit / Alternative Capital
Includes Canadian and cross-border debt funds, family offices and private lenders.
**Best use:** land acquisition, development, bridge, subordinate debt, special situations, higher leverage or execution-sensitive transactions.
## F. Equipment / Asset-Based Capital
The broader Canadian lender list also includes equipment lenders such as **FF Equipment Leasing, EconoLease and IFS Leasing**. These are useful when part of the project cost can be financed separately from the real-estate facility.
**Capital-stack strategy:** remove equipment from the real-estate construction budget where practical and finance it with equipment debt, reducing senior CRE loan requirements.
## G. SMB / Working-Capital Lenders
Canadian examples include **Merchant Growth, Greenbox Capital, Journey Capital, Source One Financial, Capital Advance and other alternative business lenders**.
These are generally **not primary project-construction lenders**, but can finance operating-company liquidity, deposits, soft costs or working-capital needs when appropriately structured.
# 5. Cross-Border Canada ↔ U.S. Considerations
For U.S. projects, do not assume that a Canadian commercial lender will lend directly against U.S. real estate simply because it finances commercial transactions in Canada.
Assess:
- Location of collateral.
- Borrower / SPE jurisdiction.
- Canadian versus U.S. guarantor.
- Cross-border tax structure.
- Ability to perfect security in the state where the asset is located.
- Foreign-ownership and entity requirements.
- Currency exposure.
- Source and proof of equity.
- Sponsor experience in the U.S. market.
A U.S.-based lender, U.S. subsidiary, cross-border debt fund or U.S. affiliate will often be the more practical source for the U.S. real-estate layer.
# 6. Capital Stack Design Workflow
## Step 1 — Establish Total Project Cost
Include:
- Land / acquisition
- Closing costs
- Hard construction costs
- Soft costs
- Financing costs
- Interest reserve
- Contingency
- Marketing / lease-up
- Developer fee where permitted
## Step 2 — Establish Project Values
Record:
- Purchase price
- As-is value
- Land value
- As-completed value
- Stabilized value
- Expected NOI / DSCR where applicable
## Step 3 — Calculate Senior Debt Capacity
Evaluate lender limits using:
- LTV — Loan-to-Value
- LTC — Loan-to-Cost
- LTARV / completed-value leverage
- DSCR for stabilized assets
- Debt yield where relevant
The lowest binding constraint determines practical senior debt capacity.
## Step 4 — Calculate the Equity Requirement
**Equity Requirement = Total Project Cost − Senior Debt − Other Eligible Debt / Grants / Incentives.**
## Step 5 — Identify the Capital Gap
If sponsor equity is below the requirement, evaluate:
- Mezzanine debt
- Preferred equity
- JV equity
- Vendor take-back / seller financing
- Landowner contribution
- Government incentives / tax credits
- Equipment financing
- Subordinate private debt
## Step 6 — Match Each Capital Layer Separately
Never require one lender to solve every layer.
Example:
- **Senior construction loan:** debt fund / bank
- **Mezzanine:** private-credit fund
- **Preferred equity:** real-estate equity investor
- **Equipment:** equipment lender
- **Sponsor equity:** developer / JV partners

# 10. Commercial Financing Expert Workflow
1. **Diagnose the project**, not merely the requested loan.
2. **Build the sources-and-uses statement.**
3. **Calculate maximum realistic senior debt.**
4. **Identify the equity / capital gap.**
5. **Determine which capital layer solves each gap.**
6. **Shortlist 5–10 capital providers by fit.**
7. **Prepare one lender-ready executive summary.**
8. **Approach lenders strategically rather than mass-submitting.**
9. **Compare term sheets on total economics, structure and certainty—not interest rate alone.**
10. **Build the takeout / exit strategy before closing the construction or bridge loan.**

## C. Company-Level Economics and Partner Participation
Where legally appropriate, the preferred model is for the operating company to earn legitimate commercial-capital advisory revenue and then compensate owners through ordinary company economics rather than ad-hoc transaction referral splits.
**Preferred flow:**
Project / Client → Veritas Capital Markets → Gross Advisory / Financing Revenue → Operating Expenses → Taxes / Reserves → Retained Earnings → Owner / Shareholder Distributions pursuant to governing agreements.
This should be distinguished from paying an unlicensed person a percentage of a specific financing transaction merely for introducing the borrower.
Operating rules:
- Avoid automatic mortgage-commission splits with unlicensed parties.
- Use documented employment, consulting or ownership compensation structures.
- Separate company profit distributions from transaction-specific referral compensation.
- Require compliance review before any referral or success-based payment to a non-licensed third party.
- Maintain accounting records linking revenue to services actually provided.
