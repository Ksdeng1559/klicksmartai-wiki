# 03 - Variable 2: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
## How Hormozi frames it
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	*"Let's use a doctor in the weight loss example - the liposuction guy. Say Dr. A has done 10,000 surgeries. Dr. B has done one. Same surgery, same dream outcome, same amount of time, same effort for you. Which one are you going with? Probably the 10,000 guy. Why? Perceived likelihood of achievement. You believe that if you pay, you'll actually get the outcome."*
</callout>
\>
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	- Alex Hormozi
</callout>
Perceived likelihood is arguably the hardest variable to nail in cold outreach.
With warm traffic, trust is already partially built. Someone watched your content. They read a case study. They saw reviews. They walked into the conversation with some belief that you can deliver.
With cold email, you're starting from **zero**. You landed in the prospect's inbox uninvited, making a claim. Their default mental response is *"why would I even believe you?"* The burden of proof sits entirely on you and the message. Your job is to pre-empt that doubt inside the email itself.
---
## The common mistake
Generic social proof. You've seen it - and you've probably written it:
- *"We've worked with hundreds of clients…"*
- *"Here's what one of our clients had to say:"*
- A random testimonial pasted in a block quote below
Every inbox has seen that pattern dozens of times. If the proof isn't specific to the prospect's exact situation, they skim past it. Worse, the pattern itself signals *template*, which triggers the delete reflex.
Generic proof is close to useless in cold email. It should be avoided in most cases.
---
## The 2026 way
Build the perceived likelihood into **the offer itself**, not into a testimonial.
One of the strongest moves is the **two-pronged front-end offer**:
- **Prong 1 - a quick deliverable they can receive immediately.** A video breakdown, a resource, a teardown of a relevant brand. The probability you'll actually deliver this is close to 100% because it's already recorded or easy to package.
- **Prong 2 - a done-for-them version.** A free audit or teardown of *their* specific account. The probability they'll get real value from this is high because it's small in scope, specific to them, and modeled after something that already worked for a comparable brand.
Neither prong asks the prospect to believe you'll hit the ultimate dream outcome. Both prongs are small enough that they can easily believe you'll deliver exactly what you said. That's the point.
You're not selling the \$80M revenue. You're selling the next small step - and the next small step has a very high perceived likelihood of delivery.
---
## Real script: the \$80M audit, read through the likelihood lens
Look at the same email from page 02, but now read every line for what it's doing to perceived likelihood.
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	**Prong 1 (the video):** *"Just finished an audit… I recorded a quick breakdown."* The deliverable already exists. Probability of delivery: essentially 100%. There's nothing to doubt.
</callout>
\>
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	**Prong 2 (the PS audit):** *"Happy to run the same teardown on your account."* The scope is specific and small, and the process is repeatable across brands. The probability that a teardown modeled after one that worked for a comparable brand will produce useful insights for this prospect is very high - and the scope is small enough that they can believe you can actually do it.
</callout>
Nowhere in the email is there a line like *"We helped Brand X go from \$1M to \$10M in three years."* No testimonials. No "trusted by 500+ companies." No awards.
The proof is woven into the offer itself. Perceived likelihood stays high because the email never asks the prospect to believe you can hit the ultimate dream - only that you can deliver the next small piece.
---
## Three ways to stack perceived likelihood in cold email
1. **Proof-by-adjacency.** Reference a brand just like theirs and what was done there. The closer the match on niche, revenue, and stage, the higher the perceived likelihood.
2. **Two-prong front-end offer.** Split the offer into a zero-friction deliverable and a targeted done-for-them step. Each prong is independently credible on its own.
3. **Specificity over volume.** One specific result with full context beats ten generic testimonials. Name the brand type. Name the revenue band. Name the tactic. Let the detail do the work.
---
## A note on testimonials
Testimonials aren't dead everywhere. They work on landing pages, sales pages, and case study pages where the prospect has already self-selected into high intent. They are weak in cold email because the prospect hasn't chosen to engage yet - they're a stranger being interrupted.
If you want to use proof in a cold email, make it a **recent, specific, named-brand result** woven into the offer. Keep the "here's what our clients say" block in the inbox drafts.
---
## Checklist before sending
- [ ] Have you cut every line that sounds like "we've worked with hundreds of"?
- [ ] Is there a specific, comparable brand referenced or implied in the email?
- [ ] Is the first deliverable something you can send within a day or two?
- [ ] Does the email ask the prospect to believe the *next step* is deliverable - not the ultimate outcome?
