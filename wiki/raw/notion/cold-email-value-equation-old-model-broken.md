# 01 — Why The Old Value Equation Stopped Working
## The formula that used to print replies
Picture the template that ran the B2B cold email world from about 2020 through 2022:
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	*"Hey \{first_name\}, if we could help you \{dream outcome\} in the next \{X days\}, guaranteed or you don't pay — would that be worth a chat?"*
</callout>
You'd promise a dream outcome, layer on a guarantee, add a short timeline, and close with a soft CTA.
For a while, that was enough. Most cold emails were lazy, so a specific promise with a risk reversal and a timeline was genuinely novel in the inbox. Reply rates were strong because the pattern was rare.
---
## What happened
Everybody started using it. That exact shape of email started hitting inboxes every single week. Prospects noticed the pattern. As soon as something reads like a template they've already seen three times this month, they stop reading and move on — no matter how good the offer underneath is.
The Value Equation didn't break. **The surface-level application of it did.**
When the lead pattern-matches your email to *"template I've seen before,"* your offer doesn't even get evaluated. They're already gone.
---
## What still works (the equation itself)
Hormozi's 4 variables still describe exactly how prospects evaluate what you're selling. The goal is still the same: maximize the top, minimize the bottom.
<table header-row="true">
<tr>
<td>Top of the equation — maximize</td>
<td>Bottom of the equation — minimize</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>\*\*Dream Outcome\*\* — the result they want</td>
<td>\*\*Time Delay\*\* — how long it takes to get there</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>\*\*Perceived Likelihood of Achievement\*\* — do they believe you can deliver it</td>
<td>\*\*Effort & Sacrifice\*\* — what they'll have to do to get there</td>
</tr>
</table>
The equation is still a diagnostic tool. Run every cold email through it and you'll catch most of what's wrong before you hit send.
---
## The 2026 shift in one line
<callout icon="ℹ️">
	Stop *stating* the variable. Start *demonstrating* it — passively, with evidence, woven into the offer itself.
</callout>
The old way was explicit. *"We'll help you hit \$80M in revenue in 90 days, guaranteed."* Every variable was called out on the nose.
The 2026 way is implicit. The email doesn't shout *"here is our dream outcome, here is our proof, here is our ETA."* It offers a small, specific, valuable thing, and the four variables are baked into the shape of what's offered — so the prospect never feels pitched.
