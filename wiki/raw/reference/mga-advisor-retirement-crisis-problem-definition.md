## Executive Summary

The North American life insurance industry faces a demographic crisis threatening MGA survival and the financial security of millions. The advisor workforce is aging out faster than it can be replaced, and recruiting systems built for a different era are incapable of closing the gap.

| Metric | Value |

| Millennial interest in insurance careers | 4% |

| Adults needing more coverage (US) | 100 million |
| Uninsured/uncertain Canadians | 42% |

## The Core Problem

**Demographic cliff:** 1.37 million insurance professionals aged 55+ (1 in 4 workers). Only 214,000 aged 20-24. The 55+ cohort has grown 74% in 10 years. 50% of workforce retires within 15 years.

**Recruitment pipeline failure:** 4% millennial interest, 70-80% Year 1 washout, no university pipeline, commission-only barrier.

**Institutional knowledge loss:** Senior advisors carry books worth $500K-$5M+ in annual premium, 20-30 years of client relationships, and irreplaceable referral networks (60-80% of new business).

## Financial Impact on MGAs

| MGA Size | Advisors Retiring (5yr) | Annual Revenue at Risk |
|----------|------------------------|------------------------|
| Small (20 advisors) | 10 | $150K-$400K/yr |
| Mid-size (75 advisors) | 37-38 | $555K-$1.5M/yr |

**Compounding costs:** Lapse revenue (15-25%/yr on orphaned books), $60K-$95K/yr recruiter overhead, $3K-$8K per failed recruit, 30-40% leadership time on recruitment.

## Root Causes — Five Structural Failures

1. **Volume ceiling** — 50-100 candidates/month max (need 2,000+)
2. **No scoring science** — subjective assessment, no predictive model
3. **Candidate experience failure** — slow follow-up, high drop-off
4. **Untapped talent pools** — real estate agents, mortgage brokers, teachers, veterans, network marketers
5. **No continuous pipeline** — reactive, not proactive

## Ideal Candidate Sources

- Real estate agents (200-500 warm contacts)
- Mortgage brokers (homebuyers at max insurance need)
- Teachers/educators (community trust, communication)
- Military veterans (discipline, mission-driven)
- Financial services professionals (product knowledge overlap)
- Network marketing professionals (commission model familiarity)
