# Nonprofit Capital Stack Strategy

## Purpose

This strategy defines how Spectra Holdings can use a nonprofit or CDC-aligned structure, including Propel Community Development or qualified nonprofit partners, as the mission-capital entry point for grants, recoverable grants, HUD resources, CRA-aligned bank capital, CDFI capital, philanthropic capital, and tax-credit-aligned funding.

The objective is to create a clean separation between mission capital, capital deployment, and operating execution.

```text
Nonprofit / CDC Layer
        ↓
Mission capital, grants, compliance, affordability
        ↓
Project SPV / MCF Layer
        ↓
Deployment, construction, reporting, recovery
        ↓
Spectra Operating Layer
        ↓
Development, manufacturing, construction, delivery
```

This allows donor and public-purpose capital to enter through a compliant nonprofit or community development vehicle while Spectra remains the vertically integrated execution platform.

---

## Strategic Role of the Nonprofit

The nonprofit should not be positioned as a passive charity. It should be positioned as a **mission-capital administrator**.

### Nonprofit Responsibilities

- Receive restricted grants.
- Receive recoverable grants.
- Apply for CMF, HUD, CDBG, Section 4, and other eligible programs.
- Partner with CDFIs and CRA-motivated banks.
- Document affordability outcomes.
- Maintain impact reporting.
- Monitor restricted use of funds.
- Administer revolving capital.
- Support homebuyer readiness.
- Support workforce and community benefit programs.

### Spectra Responsibilities

- Project execution.
- Master developer role.
- Construction delivery.
- Manufacturing and supply chain execution.
- Housing production.
- Cost control.
- Technical operations.
- Long-term asset and community operations.

## Nonprofit Capital Stack Structure

```text
Layer 1 — Philanthropic / Donor Capital
Layer 2 — Recoverable Grants
Layer 3 — Federal / Public Capacity Capital
Layer 4 — CDFI / CRA Bank Capital
Layer 5 — Tax Credit Capital
Layer 6 — MCF Construction Capital
Layer 7 — Developer / Sponsor Equity
```

| Layer | Source | Entity Recipient | Use | Repayment / Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Donor / Foundation / DAF | Propel CDC or nonprofit partner | Mission support, predevelopment, readiness | Usually forgivable or recoverable |
| 2 | Recoverable Grant | Propel CDC → Project SPV | Soft subordinate capital, affordability gap | Repaid from defined triggers |
| 3 | HUD Section 4 | Qualified nonprofit / intermediary | Capacity building, compliance, technical assistance | Generally not repayable |
| 4 | CDFI / CRA Bank | Propel CDC / SPV / MCF | Acquisition, infrastructure, bridge, gap capital | Debt repayment |
| 5 | CMF | Propel CDC or eligible nonprofit | Affordable housing finance, loan loss reserves, risk-sharing | Reinvested if recovered |
| 6 | NMTC / LIHTC / OZ | Project SPV / CDE / QOF | Tax credit equity and subsidy | Investor tax benefit / compliance hold |
| 7 | MCF | Project SPV | Vertical construction capital | Paid through sales, refinancing, or lease proceeds |
| 8 | Developer Equity | Spectra / affiliate | Sponsor alignment | Return after senior obligations |

## Capital Source Fit

### Donor / Foundation Capital

Best used for:

- Predevelopment.
- Homebuyer readiness.
- Financial literacy.
- Workforce training.
- Subsidy for LMI buyers.
- Nonprofit capacity.
- Community engagement.

Strategic position: **first-loss / mission-aligned layer**.

### Recoverable Grants

Best used for:

- Affordability gap.
- Subordinate capital.
- Bridge funding.
- Site-readiness.
- Infrastructure support.
- Developer credits.

Strategic position: **soft capital that can recycle**.

### HUD Section 4

Best used for:

- Organizational capacity.
- Technical assistance.
- Compliance systems.
- Internal controls.
- Pipeline development.
- Project management readiness.

Strategic position: **capacity-building layer**. This is not construction capital.

### Capital Magnet Fund / CMF

Best used for:

- Affordable housing finance.
- Loan loss reserves.
- Risk-sharing loans.
- Subordinate debt.
- Revolving loan fund.
- Financing support for LMI housing.

Strategic position: **public-purpose gap capital**.

### CDFI / CRA Bank Capital

Best used for:

- Acquisition.
- Construction bridge financing.
- Infrastructure.
- Senior or subordinate debt.
- Revolving credit.
- Bank CRA credit.

Strategic position: **institutional debt and CRA-aligned leverage**.

### Tax Credits

Includes:

- LIHTC.
- NMTC.
- Opportunity Zones.

Best used for:

- Affordable rental housing.
- Community facilities.
- Economic hubs.
- Manufacturing and job creation.
- OZ-designated development.

Strategic position: **leverage and subsidy layer**.

### Master Credit Facility / MCF

Best used for:

- Vertical construction.
- Repeated production batches.
- Controlled deployment.
- Capital recycling.
- Pay-as-we-sell distributions.

Strategic position: **construction deployment engine**.

The MCF should not be presented as competing with nonprofit capital. It should be presented as the disciplined deployment system that turns restricted capital into measurable housing output.

## Nonprofit Strategy by Use Case

### Use Case 1 — Affordable For-Sale Housing

```text
DAF / Foundation recoverable grant
+ CMF
+ CRA bank debt
+ MCF construction capital
+ buyer subsidy / developer credit
```

Primary outcome:

- Reduced mortgage.
- Lower monthly payment.
- Ownership pathway.

### Use Case 2 — Affordable Rental Housing

```text
LIHTC
+ CMF
+ CDFI debt
+ municipal land
+ HUD / HOME / CDBG support
```

Primary outcome:

- Long-term affordability.
- Stable rental housing.
- Compliance-friendly structure.

### Use Case 3 — Economic Hub / Manufacturing

```text
NMTC
+ OZ equity
+ CDFI debt
+ state/local incentives
+ recoverable grant
+ MCF
```

Primary outcome:

- Job creation.
- Local supply chain.
- Economic development.

### Use Case 4 — Capacity Building

```text
HUD Section 4
+ foundation grant
+ technical assistance
+ municipal support
```

Primary outcome:

- Nonprofit readiness.
- Compliance systems.
- Pipeline preparation.

## Governance Documents Required

Every nonprofit capital stack should include:

1. Community Development Partnership Agreement.
2. Recoverable Grant Agreement.
3. Use of Funds Agreement.
4. Restricted Account Agreement.
5. Escrow Instructions.
6. Reporting Template.
7. Impact Measurement Framework.
8. Conflict-of-Interest Policy.
9. Related-Party Transaction Policy.
10. Reinvestment Policy.

---

## Decision Rule

Use the nonprofit capital stack when the project has at least one of these characteristics:

- LMI households.
- Affordable housing.
- Workforce housing.
- Census tract eligibility.
- Opportunity Zone location.
- Municipal partnership.
- Nonprofit partner.
- Health / education / workforce benefit.
- CRA eligibility.
- CDFI eligibility.
- Donor or foundation interest.

Do not use the nonprofit capital stack for purely market-rate projects unless there is a documented community development purpose.

---

## Final Positioning Statement

The nonprofit capital stack allows Spectra to convert donor, foundation, HUD, CDFI, CRA, and tax-credit capital into controlled, measurable, recoverable community development funding.

Propel CDC or an equivalent qualified nonprofit serves as the mission-capital administrator, while Spectra serves as the vertically integrated execution platform.
