# Whatcom County CDFI / MCF Pilot Application

## Executive Decision Summary

Whatcom County, Washington should be treated as a high-priority pilot market for a Spectra CDFI / Master Credit Facility deployment strategy.

The county presents a rare convergence of:

- documented housing shortage,
- public-sector political will,
- county-level co-investment precedent,
- community land trust infrastructure,
- faith-aligned community support,
- missing-middle zoning urgency,
- and a structural affordability gap that conventional financing cannot solve.

## Recommended Decision

**Recommendation: CONDITIONAL GO**

Proceed to structured partner validation and capital-stack design, subject to:

1. confirmation of Kulshan CLT partnership interest,
2. verification of project-level readiness,
3. confirmation of county infrastructure and permitting support,
4. validation of CDFI / CMF eligibility,
5. and creation of an MCF pilot-level pro forma.

---

## 1. Strategic Problem Definition

Whatcom County is experiencing a structural workforce housing crisis.

Median household income is insufficient to support current median home prices. Homes below the $750,000 threshold remain supply-constrained, while higher-priced homes show emerging softening. This indicates that the unmet demand is concentrated in the attainable ownership and workforce housing segment.

The problem is not only supply. It is a structural mismatch between:

- local wages,
- migration-driven price pressure,
- short-term rental conversion,
- infrastructure constraints,
- and limited affordable housing developer capacity.

---

## 2. Why Whatcom County Matters Now

Three windows appear open at the same time:

### 1. Regulatory Window

Washington's HB 1110 missing-middle housing mandate creates urgency around upzoning and permitting.

### 2. Political Window

County-level support already exists through housing and infrastructure co-investment.

### 3. Market Window

Demand below the upper-market threshold remains strong, while the top of the market is beginning to bifurcate.

This creates a time-sensitive opportunity for a disciplined pilot.

---

## 3. Primary Partner Logic: Kulshan Community Land Trust

Kulshan CLT is the most logical local partner because the CLT model provides:

- permanent affordability,
- community trust,
- pre-qualified buyer pipeline potential,
- deed-restricted resale controls,
- and mission alignment with CDFI and faith-based capital.

The CLT structure is strategically valuable because affordability remains attached to the asset, not merely to a temporary subsidy.

---

## 4. MCF Use Case

The Master Credit Facility can fill the construction finance gap between:

- public grants,
- CDFI capital,
- county co-investment,
- buyer-side financing,
- and completed unit sales.

### MCF Role

The MCF should be modeled as:

- senior secured construction capital,
- pay-as-we-sell repayment facility,
- controlled batch deployment,
- and project-level capital recycling mechanism.

### Required Controls

- 60% presale or buyer-readiness threshold.
- escrow governance.
- project-specific SPE structure.
- construction draw controls.
- cost-to-build reporting.
- county / partner reporting package.

---

## 5. CDFI / CMF Fit

Whatcom County appears aligned with CDFI concepts because the opportunity includes:

- workforce housing,
- affordability constraints,
- public-private partnership potential,
- community impact,
- and permanent affordability through CLT structure.

### Potential CDFI Role

CDFI capital may support:

- subordinate debt,
- buyer-side mortgage pathways,
- credit enhancement,
- loan loss reserves,
- CMF-supported leverage,
- and affordable housing project financing.

### CMF Concept

A CMF-aligned structure could use grant capital as catalytic support to attract additional private and public capital.

The strategic question is whether a certified CDFI or nonprofit partner can use CMF-aligned resources to support the project stack.

---

## 6. Proposed Pilot Capital Stack

### Illustrative Structure

1. **Senior Secured / MCF Capital**  
   Construction deployment, controlled draws, pay-as-we-sell repayment.

2. **CDFI / Mission Capital**  
   Subordinate debt, credit enhancement, or buyer-side financing support.

3. **Public Capital**  
   County grants, infrastructure support, state housing programs, or municipal participation.

4. **CLT Contribution**  
   Land stewardship, permanent affordability, buyer pipeline, community trust.

5. **Buyer-Side Financing**  
   CDFI end mortgage, FHA, USDA, state housing finance agency, or other qualified buyer financing.

6. **Faith-Based / Donor-Advised Capital**  
   Mission-aligned capital for community revitalization, housing stability, or disaster-resilient housing.

## 8. Key Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---:|---|
| Infrastructure constraints | High | Focus on infill / UGA parcels with existing water and sewer capacity |
| NIMBY resistance | High | Use CLT/community-benefit narrative and pre-application engagement |
| Permitting delays | Medium-High | Engage county and city housing staff before site control |
| Developer capacity gap | High | Position Spectra as construction capacity partner, not just financier |
| STR / Airbnb distortion | Medium | Use deed restrictions and permanent affordability controls |
| Interest rate pressure | Medium | Use buyer-side credit enhancement and below-market capital where possible |
| Capital stack complexity | Medium | Standardize pilot structure before scaling |

## 11. Go / No-Go Framework

### GO if:

- Kulshan CLT confirms project-level interest.
- County or municipality confirms permitting pathway.
- Infrastructure feasibility is validated.
- CDFI or mission-capital partner is identifiable.
- MCF pro forma shows repayment feasibility.
- Affordability structure is clear and enforceable.

### NO-GO if:

- No viable pilot project is identified.
- Infrastructure capacity is unresolved.
- Political resistance blocks entitlement.
- Buyer pipeline cannot support presale threshold.
- CDFI eligibility is weak.
- Capital stack depends on uncertain grants without fallback.

Whatcom County is a strong candidate for Spectra's first CDFI / MCF pilot application because it combines documented need, public-sector action, mission-aligned local partners, and a time-sensitive regulatory window.

The opportunity should now move from county intelligence into structured deal validation.

**Decision:** Conditional Go — proceed to partner validation, capital stack modeling, and pilot pro forma development.
