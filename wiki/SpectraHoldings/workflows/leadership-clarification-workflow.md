# Leadership Clarification Workflow

## Purpose

This workflow captures strategic clarification from Spectra Holdings leadership so the wiki, agents, models, and reports reflect the company's actual operating thesis, capital strategy, legal/finance logic, and execution capacity.

This workflow expands the earlier CEO-originator clarification idea to include:

- Willis Andrews - CEO / Originator / Master Developer
- Eric Katz - finance, legal, capital structure, or transaction review source
- Emmanuel Okoye - operations, execution, construction, and delivery source

The goal is to convert leadership explanations into validated source material for:

- Master Credit Facility modeling
- capital stack design
- investor narratives
- CDFI and municipal bond strategy
- incentive optimization
- work-in-progress funding logic
- county and landowner reports
- operations and execution validation
- executive decision briefs

## Leadership Source Roles

## 1. Willis Andrews - CEO / Originator

Primary clarification areas:

- company origin and strategic thesis
- long-term vision
- vertical integration philosophy
- MCF purpose and investor story
- county partnership strategy
- landowner strategy
- public-private partnership thesis
- anchor hub strategy
- strategic risk and opportunity framing

Best used to answer:

- Why does Spectra exist?
- What is the big picture thesis?
- What makes the model different?
- What should investors, counties, and landowners believe?
- What is strategic intent versus current operating fact?

## 2. Eric Katz - Finance / Legal / Capital Structure Review

Primary clarification areas:

- capital stack mechanics
- investor terms
- legal structure
- SPV / SPE structures
- MCF governance
- securities-sensitive language
- bond counsel / tax counsel requirements
- NMTC / LIHTC / OBBBA legal or tax validation needs
- risk disclosures
- lender and investor documentation readiness

Best used to answer:

- What structure is legally viable?
- What claims are investor-sensitive?
- What needs counsel review?
- What is committed versus projected?
- What should not be represented as guaranteed?
- What entity receives or deploys funds?
- What approvals are required before capital is drawn?

## 3. Emmanuel Okoye - Operations / Execution / Delivery Validation

Primary clarification areas:

- project execution capacity
- construction sequencing
- subsidiary operational readiness
- build cycle assumptions
- procurement and supply chain assumptions
- staffing and deployment capacity
- site readiness
- operations risk
- project management controls
- timeline validation

Best used to answer:

- Can the project actually be delivered as modeled?
- What construction timeline is realistic?
- What subsidiaries are active versus planned?
- What bottlenecks exist?
- What resources are needed before execution?
- What assumptions affect build velocity?
- What can break the construction schedule?

## Core Principle

Leadership explanations are high-value internal source material, but they must still be classified, documented, and validated before being used in investor-ready outputs.

## Required Output After Each Leadership Session

Every clarification session should produce:

1. raw transcript or notes
2. summarized leadership thesis
3. extracted assumptions
4. open questions
5. contradictions or unclear claims
6. model implications
7. capital stack implications
8. operational implications
9. decision rules
10. required validation
11. wiki updates
12. database updates

## Interview Categories

## 1. Strategic Thesis

Primary source: Willis

Questions:

- What problem was Spectra originally created to solve?
- Why is Spectra different from a traditional homebuilder or developer?
- What is the long-term vision of the company?
- What are the non-negotiable principles of the operating model?

## 2. Capital Structure and Legal Readiness

Primary source: Eric Katz

Questions:

- What legal structure should hold investor capital?
- What entity controls the Master Credit Facility?
- What entity receives project-level funds?
- What capital sources can be represented as available?
- Which investor return claims require review?
- What documents are required before investor-facing materials are finalized?
- What tax credit, municipal bond, CDFI, or OBBBA claims require counsel review?

## 3. Operational Execution and Delivery Capacity

Primary source: Emmanuel Okoye

Questions:

- Which subsidiaries are currently active?
- Which subsidiaries are planned or not yet operational?
- What construction timeline is realistic today?
- What timeline is possible after anchor hub infrastructure is active?
- What procurement risks exist?
- What staffing or contractor capacity is required?
- What site conditions could delay delivery?
- What must be validated before promising build velocity?

## 4. Master Credit Facility Mechanics

Primary sources: Willis + Eric Katz

Questions:

- What is the exact purpose of the MCF?
- Who contributes capital into the MCF?
- What entity owns or manages the MCF?
- What projects can draw from the MCF?
- What is the required approval process before a draw?
- How is investor return calculated?
- When is investor return paid?
- What is the repayment source?
- What happens if sales or refinancing are delayed?
- Can one investor fund the full project instead of the MCF?
- Can the MCF fund only a project segment?

## 5. Work in Progress Funding Logic

Primary sources: Willis + Eric Katz + Emmanuel

Questions:

- What project stages require funding?
- Which stages are hardest to fund?
- Can investor capital fund an entire project?
- When should Spectra preserve MCF capacity?
- When should MCF capital be deployed instead of investor funds?
- How should capital be recycled?
- What stage creates the most risk?
- What stage creates the best investor entry point?
- Which execution assumptions must Emmanuel validate before capital is deployed?

## 6. CDFI and Municipal Bond Strategy

Primary sources: Eric Katz + Willis

Questions:

- Which projects are best suited for CDFI financing?
- Which counties or states are best suited for CDFI use?
- What is the public-purpose story for municipal bonds?
- What infrastructure could bonds finance?
- Could bonds support workforce housing?
- Could bonds support disaster relief or recovery housing?
- Who would be the issuer or conduit?
- What repayment source should be used?
- What approvals are required?

## 7. Incentive Optimization - NMTC, LIHTC, OBBBA

Primary sources: Eric Katz + Willis

Questions:

- Where does NMTC best fit in the Spectra model?
- Could NMTC support anchor hubs, manufacturing, or mixed-use redevelopment?
- Where does LIHTC fit versus ownership housing?
- Which projects are rental, ownership, or mixed-income?
- How should tax credits be placed in the capital stack?
- What OBBBA-related benefits does Spectra believe may apply?
- What must be confirmed by tax counsel, bond counsel, CDEs, or public agencies?

## 8. Investor Narrative and Risk

Primary sources: Willis + Eric Katz

Questions:

- What should investors believe about Spectra?
- What is the most compelling investor argument?
- What investor objections are expected?
- What risks should be disclosed clearly?
- What breaks the model?
- What improves the model?
- What proof points matter most?
- What claims should not be made until validated?

## 9. County, Landowner, and Execution Strategy

Primary sources: Willis + Emmanuel

Questions:

- What should a county hear first?
- What does Spectra need from municipalities?
- What makes a county attractive?
- What makes a county a poor fit?
- What landowner structures are acceptable?
- How does Spectra compensate landowners?
- When should Spectra walk away from a land opportunity?
- What operational conditions must exist before accepting a project?

## Classification Rules

Classify leadership statements as:

- confirmed operating fact
- strategic intent
- projected assumption
- hypothesis
- requires third-party validation
- investor-sensitive claim
- legal / tax sensitive claim
- operations-sensitive claim

## Governance Rule

Leadership explanations are high-value internal source material, but investor-facing outputs must still distinguish between:

- proven facts
- management assumptions
- projections
- third-party validated evidence
- legal / tax claims requiring counsel review
- operational claims requiring delivery validation
