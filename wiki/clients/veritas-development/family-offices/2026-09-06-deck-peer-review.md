## THE STRUCTURAL ISSUE (most important)

The deck builds a **"100% tax-free exit"** thesis on an **industrial Land Reserve asset that does not exist** publicly, then the collateral (Evermont) is a **residential** product where the "tax-free" framing is weaker (recapture exposure, no §1031 on taxable exit).

**Recommendation:** Re-anchor the deck to a **verifiable** project (Evermont District) and a **defensible** claim ("100% MO-state capital-gains deduction for MO-domiciled pass-through investors"). Drop the "tax-free" absolutism. State federal + recapture caveats in one footnote. Then the family-office list (see `2026-09-06-findings.md`) is a clean, credible pitch.

---

## Bottom Line
The HB 594 mechanics are correct and well-sourced — that's the strong core. But as written, the deck **overreaches on "tax-free," ships an unverifiable asset, and mislabels a Kansas-only highway.** Fix those three and it's pitch-ready within the 10-target family-office list.
