## 1. Interview Objective

The purpose of this interview is to document Gilbert's direct observations of recycling depots, e-waste processing, and material recovery facilities.

The goal is to validate whether current urban mining workflows are primarily manual and whether there is a fundable opportunity to introduce AI-assisted, human-in-the-loop recovery systems.

This interview should help answer:

> Are current recycling and e-waste recovery operations manual enough to justify an AI-assisted urban mining pilot?

## 4. Facility Context Questions

1. What facility or facilities did you observe?
2. Where were they located?
3. What type of facility was it?
   - E-waste depot
   - ITAD processor
   - metal recycling yard
   - appliance recycler
   - mixed recycling depot
   - municipal transfer station
   - other
4. Was the facility privately owned, municipal, nonprofit, or part of a larger industrial operator?
5. What types of materials were being processed?
6. Were electronics, computers, servers, GPUs, batteries, appliances, or circuit boards present?
7. Was the operation focused on resale, recycling, dismantling, sorting, export, or downstream refining?

---

## 5. Manual Workflow Observation Questions

1. Which parts of the workflow appeared manual?
2. Did workers manually unload materials?
3. Did workers visually identify materials by hand?
4. Did workers manually sort electronics or components?
5. Did workers manually remove batteries or hazardous parts?
6. Did workers manually separate circuit boards, wires, metals, plastics, or components?
7. Did workers manually stage recovered materials for resale or downstream processing?
8. Were tools used mainly hand tools, powered tools, conveyors, scanners, or robotics?
9. Did you observe any automated inspection, robotic picking, AI vision, or sensor-based sorting?
10. What part of the manual process looked slowest or most inefficient?

---

## 6. Automation Gap Questions

1. Did the facility use any computer vision, scanners, robotics, or AI?
2. Did the facility use conveyors or semi-automated sorting equipment?
3. Was material identification done by trained workers rather than technology?
4. Were batteries or hazards detected manually?
5. Was there any automated classification of material type?
6. Was there any barcode, QR code, RFID, or digital asset tracking?
7. Did the facility appear to have a digital workflow system?
8. Where could AI assistance have helped immediately?
9. Which station would be the easiest place to test a partial automation pilot?

---

## 7. Safety and Hazard Questions

1. Did workers handle batteries directly?
2. Were lithium batteries or battery-containing devices present?
3. Were workers exposed to sharp metal, broken glass, wires, heavy components, or hazardous materials?
4. Were there clear safety protocols for battery removal or hazardous component handling?
5. Did any part of the process appear physically repetitive or ergonomically difficult?
6. Did workers need to make fast visual judgments about hazardous materials?
7. Could AI-assisted detection reduce risk at intake or sorting?

---

## 8. Chain-of-Custody and MRV Questions

1. How was incoming material tracked?
2. Were weights recorded by material category?
3. Were batches tracked from intake to recovery or sale?
4. Was there visible software used for tracking?
5. Could the facility produce a chain-of-custody report?
6. Could the facility produce a recovery report by material type?
7. Could the facility produce an environmental impact report?
8. Could the facility support grant reporting or carbon/circular economy reporting with its current data?
9. What reporting gaps did you observe?
10. What data should be captured automatically in a better system?

---

## 9. Material Recovery Value Questions

1. Which materials appeared to have the highest recovery value?
2. Were circuit boards separated from lower-value materials?
3. Were copper wires, aluminum, steel, batteries, or rare earth-containing components separated?
4. Were servers, data-center equipment, GPUs, CPUs, or enterprise electronics present?
5. Were any materials being under-sorted or treated as mixed waste?
6. Did the operation appear to prioritize high-value components?
7. Was there any visible connection to downstream buyers, refiners, or resale channels?
8. What material stream looked most promising for an Urban Mining pilot?

---

## 10. Worker Augmentation Questions

1. Did the facility appear labor-constrained?
2. Did workers perform repetitive visual sorting tasks?
3. Did workers make judgment calls that AI could support?
4. Could an AI guidance screen help workers classify materials faster?
5. Could AI flag hazards before workers touch materials?
6. Could semi-automated sorting reduce repetitive handling?
7. Would partial automation improve worker safety without replacing workers?
8. How should the solution be framed to operators and funders: workforce replacement or workforce augmentation?

---

## 11. Pilot Design Questions

1. Based on what you saw, what would be the best first pilot?
2. Would AI-assisted intake classification solve a real problem?
3. Would battery/hazard detection solve a real problem?
4. Would chain-of-custody and MRV reporting solve a real problem?
5. Would robotic assistance be useful now, or should the first pilot focus on AI vision and data tracking?
6. What would make a facility willing to test this?
7. What metrics would convince a facility owner that the system works?
8. What would a realistic pilot budget be?
9. What would a realistic pilot timeline be?
10. What partner type should Breakthrough approach first?

## 15. Grant-Ready Problem Statement Template

> Existing urban mining and e-waste recovery facilities often depend on manual sorting, visual identification, component removal, and informal material tracking. These manual workflows limit throughput, increase safety risk, reduce recovery accuracy, and make it difficult to produce audit-ready chain-of-custody and environmental reporting. Breakthrough Management proposes to validate an AI-assisted, human-in-the-loop recovery workflow that supports operators with computer vision, hazard detection, material classification, and MRV reporting.
