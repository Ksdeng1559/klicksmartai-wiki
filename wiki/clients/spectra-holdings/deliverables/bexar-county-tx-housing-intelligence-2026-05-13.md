# Bexar County, TX — Housing Intelligence Report

> *"So are farmers who wait for the precious harvest from the land and make it their goal — to receive the blessing from God."* — 2 Timothy 2:6

Bexar County presents a large-scale MCF opportunity with a 115,000+ unit structural deficit, a proven homeless response system, and a uniquely identifiable intervention target: 30 properties that drive 12% of all eviction filings. The challenge is Texas's low-subsidy political environment — the MCF must be more creative and patient than in markets with strong public co-investment. The opportunity is equally large: the gap between what San Antonio's workforce earns and what the market produces has created a structural housing crisis at a scale that only very large, very patient capital can address.

## 4. The Eviction Intervention Thesis

**The most actionable MCF entry point in Bexar County:**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Eviction filings (2024) | **27,012** |
| % resulting in tenant vacated | **55%** |
| Filings from 30 properties | **12% of all filings** |
| Highest single property | Forest Oaks — 128 of 166 units |

**Why this is a CDFI intervention target:**

1. **Concentrated:** 30 properties are identifiable, addressable, and iterable — not a diffuse social problem
2. **Economically rational:** At $8.3M to prevent 2,758 evictions ($3,000/household), anti-eviction intervention has better ROI than building from scratch
3. **Politically viable:** No zoning battles; the intervention is with property owners, not neighborhoods
4. **Scalable:** If 30 properties drive 12% of filings, targeting the top 100 properties addresses a large fraction of filings

**Intervention structures:**
- Acquisition of at-risk NOAH (Naturally Occurring Affordable Housing) properties before eviction action
- Pre-eviction mediation program (funded by MCF as program-related investment)
- Tenant legal defense fund (preventive, not reactive)
- Bridge financing for property owners facing financial pressure (preventing conversion to market-rate)

## 5. The Missing Middle Opportunity

San Antonio's missing middle is the hardest housing problem to solve — and the most underserved by conventional capital:

**The gap:**
- Large developers cannot make 2-to-20 unit projects pencil at market-rate financing
- Small community developers lack the gap financing to get these projects off the ground
- The city is actively trying to address developer roadblocks (TPR, 2026) but the financing gap remains

**MCF role:**
- Provide gap financing (soft debt, forgivable loans, or equity) that makes 2-to-20 unit projects financially viable
- Partner with SA Affordable Homes or similar CDFIs who have the community trust and development capacity
- Structure ground leases or shared equity to maintain long-term affordability

**Product type targets:**
- 4-to-12 unit courtyard buildings (workforce rental)
- 2-to-4 unit missing middle (for-sale to households earning $50–75K)
- ADU/cottage court combinations (on larger lots in emerging neighborhoods)

## 7. Investment Structuring Recommendation

**Recommended deal architecture:**

| Layer | Source | Role |
|-------|--------|------|
| Equity (25–35%) | Spectra Holdings MCF | Patient capital; focus on NOAH acquisition |
| Equity (20–30%) | National Christian Foundation / faith-aligned CDFIs | Mission-aligned patient capital |
| Debt (20–30%) | Conventional lender / CDFI loan fund | Market-rate debt on stabilized assets |
| Public incentive | City of San Antonio (TIRZ, tax abatement) | Reduces carrying costs; limited direct capital |
| Program-related investment | MCF anti-eviction fund | $8.3M FY24 precedent — $3K/household ROI |
| Developer fee | SA Affordable Homes or co-developer | For-profit partner with community trust |

**Target deal size:** $3–10M equity per project; $10–30M total project cost at 75–80% LTV
**Geographic priority:** Council District 8 (NW Side) — 15% of all evictions, highest concentration
**Product type:** NOAH acquisition-rehab (30-property thesis); missing middle ground-floor layer

**Note on TX political risk:** Unlike Washington State (HB 1110 mandate), Texas does not have a state-level missing middle zoning requirement. This means San Antonio's missing middle opportunity depends entirely on local zoning reform outcomes (uncertain) and private capital creativity. MCF structuring should assume no public zoning tailwind.
