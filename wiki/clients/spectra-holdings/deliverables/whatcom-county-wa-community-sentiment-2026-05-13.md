# Whatcom County, WA — Community Sentiment Report

> *"Do not forget to show hospitality to strangers, for by so doing some people have shown hospitality to angels without knowing it."* — Hebrews 13:2

Whatcom County's housing crisis is not abstract. It is felt in school hallways (972 homeless students), in congregations (families priced out of their own community), and in the growing geographic divide between the amenity-driven coastal corridor and the working families of the Bellingham metro. The community knows it. The county has acknowledged it. The question for an MCF is whether the political will and institutional infrastructure are aligned enough to absorb capital at impact scale.

---

## 1. Community Perception of the Housing Crisis

Whatcom County has formally acknowledged a **5,000-unit housing shortfall** over five years. The county's September 2025 5-Year Housing Plan is unambiguous: the crisis is structural, supply-constrained, and requires braided public-private-faith capital to solve.

**Perception markers:**

- The Bellingham School District logged **972 homeless students** in the 2024–25 school year — a number that generates visible urgency in school board and city council discussion
- Housing advocacy organizations (Whatcom Housing Alliance, Habitat for Humanity Whatcom) have been public and consistent in framing this as a moral issue, not merely an economic one — language that resonates with faith-aligned investors
- Birch Bay and Blaine homeowners have pushed back on density proposals, citing concerns about character and infrastructure capacity — a predictable NIMBY signal that creates political complexity for upzoning
- The coastal corridor's 26.3% vacancy rate generates community anger: units exist, but they sit empty as short-term rentals, removed from the workforce housing supply

## 2. Faith Infrastructure & Alignment

Whatcom County has a well-established interfaith infrastructure. The **Interfaith Coalition of Whatcom County** is an active partner in housing advocacy, and the county's $750,000 co-investment in Telegraph Townhomes and LaFreniere Court signals that faith-framed community development is politically viable.

**Key faith-aligned signals:**

| Entity | Role |
|--------|------|
| **Kulshan Community Land Trust** | Primary CDFI/CLT vehicle — community development framing, faith-aligned board culture |
| Interfaith Coalition of Whatcom County | Active in housing advocacy and community organizing |
| Opportunity Council | Faith-adjacent social services provider |
| Local churches | Active in homeless services, rapid rehousing coordination |
| Whatcom County government | Committed $750K to CLT pipeline (Sept 2025) — political will demonstrated |

> Kulshan CLT's model — permanently affordable homeownership, community control, resident stewardship — is specifically the kind of institution that resonates with Christian foundation investment committees and CDFIs seeking high-impact, measurable outcomes.

---

## 3. Displacement Risk Assessment

**Displacement risk — HIGH and ongoing:**

- 47% of homeless individuals lost housing *in* Whatcom County — the crisis is locally generated, not imported
- Out-of-area cash buyers (Seattle, Bay Area) remain active above the $650K threshold, compressing the middle market
- Airbnb/VRBO saturation on Semiahmoo Spit removes entire condominium complexes from long-term rental supply — this is a structural, not cyclical, distortion
- The corridor's aging demographic (median age 46–50) combined with its popularity as a retirement destination means demand for low-density, single-family product will continue to outpace workforce housing supply

**Anti-displacement infrastructure present:**
- Kulshan CLT's community land trust model is specifically designed to prevent displacement by removing land from the speculative market permanently
- The county's 5-Year Plan includes anti-displacement as an explicit goal
- $3.3M in relocation/anti-displacement funds were deployed in Bexar County in FY24 — a comparable model for Whatcom County

## 4. Developer & CDFI Ecosystem

Whatcom County has a functioning, if small, affordable housing developer ecosystem:

| Organization | Type | Pipeline Relevance |
|-------------|------|-------------------|
| **Kulshan CLT** | Community Land Trust | Primary MCF partner — for-sale affordability |
| Opportunity Council | Social services + housing | Referral network, population insight |
| Habitat for Humanity | Affordable homeownership | Potential co-developer for single-family |
| Mercy Housing | Multi-family rental | Potential co-developer for rental product |
| Foundation Housing Partners | Affordable housing dev | Potential co-investor |

**Gap:** The corridor (Birch Bay / Blaine / Semiahmoo) has **zero active affordable housing developers**. Kulshan CLT's pipeline is in the Bellingham metro. Any corridor deployment requires either recruiting a developer to the corridor or extending Kulshan CLT's geographic scope.

## 5. Political Risk

| Risk | Level | Note |
|------|-------|------|
| Zoning resistance (corridor NIMBY) | **Medium** | Birch Bay / Blaine homeowners active against upzoning |
| State mandate override (HB 1110) | **Low** | State mandate actually expands supply opportunity |
| County election cycle | **Low-Medium** | 2025 plan passed 7-0; no current opposition visible |
| Canadian cross-border purchasing pressure | **High** | Underscores corridor premium; not addressable by MCF |

**Key political signal:** Whatcom County approved increased Birch Bay density **unanimously (7-0, June 2025)** and committed $750K to the corridor pipeline in September 2025. The political window for density and co-investment is currently open.

---

## 6. Momentum Signals

**Positive momentum:**
- HB 1110 (Missing Middle) compliance deadline of **June 2026** creates urgency for development permits — first-mover advantage before state-mandated uniformity
- Mayor Kim Lund signed executive order (Nov 2024) to accelerate missing-middle zoning compliance
- County $750K co-investment (Sept 2025) de-risks private capital entry
- Kulshan CLT has **three projects in active pipeline**: LaFreniere Court, Thornton Road, Telegraph Townhomes
- City Council voted April 2025 to increase density to 6 units/lot near major transit stops

**Persistent headwinds:**
- Interest rate environment suppresses for-sale demand while rental demand remains elevated
- Developer capacity in the county is limited — no large-scale affordable housing developer has active corridor projects
- Corridor NIMBY resistance will slow permit approvals for multi-family projects
