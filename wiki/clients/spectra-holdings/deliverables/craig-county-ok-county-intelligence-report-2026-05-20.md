# Craig County, OK — County Intelligence Report

> *"The harvest is plentiful, but the workers are few."* — Matthew 9:37

Craig County, Oklahoma sits in the northeast corner of the state, anchored by the city of Vinita along the I-44 / Will Rogers Turnpike corridor between Tulsa and Joplin. It is a small, slow-shrinking rural county with a meaningful Native American population, elevated poverty, and elevated housing vacancy — a profile that creates conditions for CDFI/CLT intervention rather than speculative growth. The county does not have the affordability crisis of a coastal market; it has a structural dysfunction crisis — population loss, vacant stock, depressed incomes, and a housing market that is cheap in absolute terms but still unreachable for many residents at the local median income. The opportunity is not to outbid a hot market; it is to arrest decline and provide a stable platform for workforce retention.

Craig County peaked in 1940 at 21,083 and has declined steadily since, with the steepest losses in the 1950s–1970s. The 2020–2025 reversal (+2.9%) is small but notable — it suggests the county may be approaching a stabilization floor rather than continued contraction. Vinita itself is holding near flat. The countywide growth is partly spread across smaller communities (Welch 638, Ketchum 449, Bluejacket 243, Big Cabin 176) and balance-of-county residents (7,624).

The small population base is a structural fact. Craig County is not a growth market. Intervention here must be framed as **stabilization and retention** — keeping workers in place, reducing vacancy, arresting decline — not as a scalebox play.

Craig County's most distinctive demographic feature is its **20.2% Native American / Alaska Native population** — more than double the state rate (8.4%) and well above the national rate. Vinita sits within the historic Cherokee Nation territory. This creates specific levers absent in most rural counties:

- **Tribal partnership pathways** — Cherokee Nation and other regional tribes have workforce development funds, community development programs, and housing assistance dollars that can be braided into a capital stack
- **Federal funding access** — BIA (Bureau of Indian Affairs) programs, HUD Indian Community Development Block Grant (ICDBG) funds, and USDA Rural Development are all more accessible with a significant tribal population
- **NMTC (New Markets Tax Credit) priority** — Census tracts with ≥20% AI/AN population frequently qualify for NMTC allocations; the county seat and several surrounding tracts may qualify

The median age of 43.2 (county) and 38 (Vinita) is slightly older than the state median (36.9), consistent with rural outmigration of younger adults. The 20.3% senior population (vs. 17% state) creates demand for age-restricted and accessibility-focused housing product.

**Critical findings:**

Vinita's **46.5% owner-occupied rate** vs. the county's **70.1%** is a sharp sub-market signal. Vinita has a substantially renter-dominated housing profile — consistent with a smaller city where older housing stock, rental tenure, and limited for-sale supply coexist. This creates an entry point for a CLT model targeting workforce homeownership: the gap between local rents and a path to ownership is where CLT intervention is most needed.

The **rental vacancy rate of 10.4%** (county) and **18.0%** (Vinita city) is elevated above the healthy 5–7% threshold. These are not hot-market conditions — they reflect **underperforming rental demand**, a symptom of population loss and weak wage growth. This vacant rental stock is a primary opportunity: it can be acquired at distressed basis, rehabilitated, and resold to workforce buyers via CLT resale-restricted mechanism, converting problem vacant units into stabilized homeownership.

Median home values of **$92,200–$150,500** (county) are among the most affordable in the Spectra pipeline. In absolute terms, the capital required to close any affordability gap is substantially lower than coastal markets. However, the income base is also substantially lower — requiring a per-unit capital contribution rather than relying on market appreciation.

At county MHI ($50,182), the standard 30% DTI mortgage can support up to ~$523,840 — well above the actual median home price of $150,500. There is no county-level affordability gap.

**Affordability analysis — Vinita city (MHI $41,140):**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Monthly payment ceiling | $41,140 × 0.30 ÷ 12 = **$1,028.50/mo** |
| Max mortgage | $1,028.50 × 360 ÷ 0.80 = **$386,439** |

At Vinita MHI ($41,140), the maximum affordable home price is ~$429,377 — nearly 4.7× the actual median. Vinita's housing market is structurally *underpriced* relative to local incomes. This is not a market failure in the traditional sense — it reflects the consequence of population loss, weak demand, and limited new construction. The CLT/affordable housing opportunity here is not to bridge an affordability gap; it is to **provide a stable, deed-restricted ownership vehicle** that prevents further disinvestment, keeps workforce residents in place, and creates a predictable resale mechanism for investors.

The more pressing financial challenge is the **poverty rate of 18.8%** (county) and **24.8%** (Vinita) — well above state (15.3%) and national (12.5%) averages. At $26,443 per capita income (county), a large share of the population is effectively excluded from both homeownership and market-rate rental. The intervention thesis here is **workforce stabilization + poverty income support via braided public funds** — not a conventional affordability gap capital stack.

## 5. Economy & Employment

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Key historical industries | Oil/refining (Sinclair Oil), farming, ranching |
| Current base | Agriculture, light manufacturing, regional services |
| Major employers (estimate) | Local government, healthcare (St. John's Hospital region), agriculture processing, school district |
| Unemployment | ~4–5% (state avg, county not separately tracked) |
| Labor force participation | 50.4% (county, age 16+) |
| Female labor force participation | 52.3% |

Craig County's economy has shifted from oil-dependent (Sinclair Oil refinery era) to a more diversified but modest base centered on agriculture, regional services, and local government employment. The county lacks a major economic anchor — no large university, no port, no manufacturing cluster of scale. This is both a vulnerability and an opportunity: without a dominant employer, MCF deployment could be positioned as **anchor employer recruitment infrastructure** — the housing stability to attract and retain workers that make the county competitive for light manufacturing or distribution.

The I-44 / Will Rogers Turnpike corridor provides logistics access. Vinita sits ~90 minutes from Tulsa and ~60 minutes from Joplin — positioning it as a potential **light manufacturing or last-mile distribution satellite** for larger metros. The CLT/housing platform could be explicitly framed to future employers as: *"Stable workforce housing available — workforce is already here."*

**Educational attainment constraints:**
- Only 15.3% bachelor's degree or higher (county) vs. 28.4% state
- 85.3% high school grad+
- This is a workforce training opportunity — braided with tribal workforce grants, Oklahoma Works, and regional community college programs

**Key opportunity — Tribal partnership (Cherokee Nation):**
Craig County's 20.2% AI/AN population creates eligibility for ICDBG (Indian Community Development Block Grant) funds, BIA facility grants, and HUD Section 184 Indian Home Loan Guarantee Program. A CLT structure partnered with Cherokee Nation or adjacent tribal entities could access **federal tribal housing dollars not available to conventional CDFIs** — a differentiated stack that strengthens the investor thesis significantly.

**County co-investment precedent:** Unlike Whatcom County (which has an active county co-investment signal at $750K), Craig County has not been identified as having an active precedent. This is both a gap and an opportunity — the first institutional partner to the county can help define the co-investment terms rather than entering an already-established framework.

## 10. Capital Attraction Thesis

Craig County, OK is not Whatcom County, WA. The investment thesis must be reframed:

**Core thesis:** *"In a county where population loss has created vacant stock and depressed values, the opportunity is not to outbid a hot market — it is to arrest decline. CLT intervention at the Vinita anchor stabilizes the workforce base, converts distressed rental vacancy into permanent affordable ownership, and positions the county as a credible partner for light manufacturing recruitment."*

**Why this county compels capital:**
1. **Low capital requirement per unit** — median home $92,200–$150,500 means per-unit subsidy (if needed) is a fraction of coastal markets
2. **Tribal federal pathway** — 20.2% AI/AN population opens ICDBG, BIA, NMTC, and tribal workforce funds unavailable in most markets
3. **I-44 logistics anchor** — 90 min Tulsa / 60 min Joplin positions Vinita for last-mile manufacturing or distribution satellite
4. **Workforce pipeline** — high labor force participation (50.4%), moderate unemployment, tribal workforce training programs
5. **No competition** — no other CLT or CDFI-identified program currently operating in Craig County; first-mover advantage is real
6. **Faith-aligned narrative** — conservative rural county where economic development framing (not advocacy framing) unlocks church-linked capital
