# Whatcom County, WA — Housing Intelligence Report

> *"So are farmers who wait for the precious harvest from the land and make it their goal — to receive the blessing from God."* — 2 Timothy 2:6

Whatcom County presents the strongest MCF entry thesis in the current Spectra Holdings pipeline. A $200,000+ affordability gap, a county-acknowledged 5,000-unit shortfall, near-zero vacancy, and a proven CLT partner with three active pipeline projects create a high-conviction deployment opportunity. The HB 1110 regulatory window (June 2026 compliance deadline) creates a time-limited first-mover advantage for capital that moves before state-mandated zoning uniformity compresses deal margins.

## 2. The Affordability Math

**County-wide:**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Median home price | **$585,800** (Census QuickFacts 2025) |
| Median household income | **$81,784** (QuickFacts) / $85,471 (FRED 2024) |
| Affordable mortgage at $81,784 | ~$385,000 |
| Affordability gap | **$200,800** |

**What a $200,000 gap means in practice:**
- A household earning the median income ($81,784) cannot qualify for a standard 30-year mortgage on the median-priced home in Whatcom County without a down payment of approximately $200,000 — or income nearly 2x current median
- A household earning $50,000 (the Whatcom County renter median) can afford approximately $235,000 — against a median home price of $585,800
- Rent burden is 21.7% of income for the median household — above the 20% cost-burden threshold and rising

**Corridor (Birch Bay / Blaine):**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Median home value | $439,100 (Birch Bay) |
| Affordable at $77K MHI | ~$385,000 |
| Gap at corridor premium frontage | $60,000–$215,000 |
| Birch Bay vacancy | **26.3%** (structurally non-market) |

## 5. HB 1110 — The Regulatory Window

Washington State's Missing Middle Housing Bill (HB 1110) requires Bellingham to adopt middle housing zoning in all residential zones by **June 2026** — or the state overrides local zoning.

**Investment implication:**
- Developers and investors who move before June 2026 can shape project economics before state-mandated uniformity compresses margins
- Permit applications submitted before the state override deadline have priority under current (more favorable) local zoning
- The county's June 2025 unanimous vote to increase Birch Bay density signals that local government is proactively using the regulatory window

**Timeline risk:** June 2026 is approximately 12 months away. An MCF structured in 2026 can still catch the window if permits are pulled before the state override takes effect — but the clock is running.
