## Executive Summary

Bexar County is a high-priority secondary pilot candidate for Spectra Holdings because it combines:

- a structural housing deficit of **115,125 units**,
- one of the most effective homeless response systems in the country,
- measurable and concentrated eviction pressure (27,012 filings, 12% from 30 identifiable properties),
- strong CDFI and faith-aligned capital ecosystem,
- a large affordable housing waitlist (65,920 households),
- and growing population growth (+6.4% 2020–2024) that is deepening the supply gap.

San Antonio is the **7th largest U.S. city** with a young median age (34.9) and a projected +1M additional residents by 2040 — creating sustained demand fundamentals.

The county's poverty rate (17.1%) and low median household income ($65,056) relative to national medians create an affordability gap requiring workforce and affordable housing product, not purely market-rate development.

## 8. Missing-Middle Opportunity

San Antonio's housing market has a structural hole between single-family detached homes and large apartment complexes. Proposed city target: 2-to-20 unit multi-family projects. Smaller projects lack economies of scale and face financing gaps.

The urban core is losing families to surrounding counties due to lack of attainable ownership product. This creates an opportunity for:
- Missing-middle ownership product (townhomes, cottage courts, small multifamily)
- NOAH (Naturally Occurring Affordable Housing) acquisition-rehab
- CLT-aligned ownership structures

## 9. Infrastructure and Political Context

**Municipal Sentiment:**
- The City of San Antonio has demonstrated a commitment to housing affordability through its FY24 bond program and homeless response system.
- SA Tomorrow comprehensive plan provides a framework for targeted investment corridors.
- Eastside Promise and other neighborhood-targeted programs create geographic specificity for deployment.

**Known Infrastructure Constraints:**
- Water and sewer capacity limits in some outlying corridors
- Flood plain considerations in low-lying areas
- Traffic and mobility bottlenecks in high-growth corridors

---

## 10. Strategic Decision Implication

Bexar County should move from intelligence collection to structured pilot validation with a focus on:

1. NOAH acquisition-rehab as the first capital deployment use case.
2. Anti-eviction stabilization strategy targeting the 30-property concentration.
3. Missing-middle workforce ownership product as the primary housing thesis.
4. CDFI co-lending partnership to leverage public and mission capital.
5. Faith-aligned capital sidecar structure for community trust and narrative alignment.
