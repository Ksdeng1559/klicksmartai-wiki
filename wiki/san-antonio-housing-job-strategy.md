1. OPERATING FRAME
This plan is written to outline Spectra’s Housing Delivery and Job Creation Strategy in the City of San
Antonio. This Strategy is a layout to build the first 5,000 single -family and multi-family units within
the city limits over a five (5) year time period  and create 1,500+ direct/indirect jobs. This strategy
focuses on Spectra’s four pillars of Health, Housing, Economics, and Education  models, spurred by
innovation technology. Homelessness and Continuum of Care (CoC) metrics may be referenced only
as supporting context; the execution geography, siting, and delivery commitments in this plan are
strictly within City limits.

4.
HOUSING & ECONOMIC CAMPAIGN
Actual affordability is achieved by lowering monthly housing costs and eliminating the recurring
premium that renters typically pay to landlords. Accordingly, converting renters into homebuyers is
a core solution to the housing crisis, paired with a sustai nable economic plan that expands
opportunity for households entering Spectra’s ecosystem.
This strategy intentionally links housing access to economic mobility by offering residents a pathway
to join the Spectra workforce and/or launch their own business, supported by structured workforce
development and small business training programs in whic h Spectra serves as a sponsor and
implementation partner.
5.
HOUSING NEED AND AFFORDABILITY REALITY
San Antonio’s core constraint is not demand; it is deliverable supply at price points that working
households can sustain. City reporting indicates poverty remains materially higher than the national
average, and that educational attainment and income disp arities correlate with housing instability.
City data also reflect that a very large share of renter households are cost -burdened, meaning they
spend more than 30% of their income on housing, and that the City’s affordability challenge varies
dramatically by neighborhood.
The practical implication for this strategy is that “affordability” must be defined as reduced monthly
cost of living , not just a purchase price. Reduced mortgages, reduced energy costs, and reduced
maintenance exposure are the levers that convert a unit into a stable household outcome.

7.
SITE STRATEGY
Spectra will pursue a three-lane site pipeline so production is not bottlenecked by any one land type.
The pipeline is designed to produce early wins quickly, while also building a scalable base of
subdivision and mixed-use production.
7.1 Lane A — Infill Clusters
Infill clusters are small groups of lots that can be permitted and
started quickly. This lane is used to demonstrate early wins,
establish an inspection rhythm, and place families into ownership
rapidly.
Execution steps: identify target neighborhoods with existing
utilities and clear title; standardize a small set of plan types for repeat permitting; batch procurement
and scheduling to reduce per-unit overhead.
7.2 Lane B — Subdivision-Scale Tracts
Subdivision tracts provide the volume required to materially
affect supply and pricing. Spectra will prioritize partnerships
with landowners that include a profit -sharing model. This
structure can create tax advantages for the landowner and can
generate 2 –3 times the profit opportunity relative to the
property’s current value when executed under a coordinated
entitlement and vertical construction plan. Landowners are
treated as co-developers by contributing land value to the project structure, which lowers  Spectra’s
upfront costs, accelerates site control, and supports faster delivery at attainable price points.
Landowner profit participation is realized at closing under the program’s pay-as-we-sell distribution
mechanics.
Execution steps: secure tract control through landowner partnership and profit -sharing programs;
complete entitlement and engineering with City coordination; phase horizontal and vertical
construction to maintain continuous closings.

8.
CAPACITY STRATEGY
Housing delivery at scale must be matched to capacity. Spectra will not advance sites strictly on land
availability; every site must be screened for capacity constraints and upgrade requirements,
including:
• School capacity and enrollment pressure (coordination with ISDs and City/County partners).
• Water and wastewater capacity, lift stations, and treatment constraints.
• Drainage and flood mitigation requirements.
• Roadway access, traffic impacts, and emergency response access.
• Power and broadband readiness where required for technology-enabled housing systems.
Spectra will coordinate with the City and County to make determinations based on current
infrastructure conditions and to jointly identify funding pathways for upgrades. This includes
aligning infrastructure projects with economic development incentives, p ublic infrastructure
funding, and performance -based tools so that infrastructure becomes a catalyst for housing starts
rather than a bottleneck.

11.2 Pay-As-We-Sell Distributions
Spectra’s mission is to remain debt-free at the corporate level by leveraging debt that is paid through
a “pay as we sell” strategy. This allows landowners who contributed land to get paid profit sharing at
closing, and it allows lenders, investors, institutions, and government agencies to r eceive their
defined distributions at closing. The homeowner receives a new home with a reduced mortgage
supported by a partner and later a subsidiary of Spectra. Spectra’s development firm and subsidiaries
retain substantial profits through this community wealth-building model.

To further drive down the cost of living, residents in Spectra’s ecosystem and their families will
become members of a Spectra community membership program. This membership will allow
participants to shop and receive special discounts and offers from Spect ra commercial brands and
participating partner small businesses. The purpose is to lower the cost of living, create community
