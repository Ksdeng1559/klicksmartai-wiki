## 1. Executive Summary

**Can recoverable grants become a repeatable capital source for Spectra Holdings Group and Propel Community Development?**  
Yes — with conditions. Recoverable grants are viable as **gap-filler and pre-development bridge capital** within a larger capital stack. They are not a primary capital source and should not be positioned as such.

**Bottom line:** Recoverable grants function as a "first-loss" philanthropic layer that reduces equity dilution and unlocks senior debt. When structured correctly, they fill the pre-development and early-stage gap that prevents projects from reaching construction closing.

**Probability of success:** 65% for a pilot within 18 months, assuming Propel Community Development has or obtains 501(c)(3) status and maintains an active project pipeline.

## 2. Definition: What Is a Recoverable Grant?

A **recoverable grant** is a time-bound charitable grant where return of the original grant amount — in whole or part — is tied to achievement of certain pre-set objectives or trigger events.

**Critical distinction:** It is a **grant, not a loan**. Repayment is not legally enforceable. If the recipient fails to meet objectives, the grant converts to a traditional (unilateral) grant with no repayment obligation.

This creates a fundamentally different risk profile for the recipient compared to any form of debt or Program-Related Investment (PRI).

### How It Works — Process Flow

```
Grant Provider (DAF / Foundation / Faith-Based Fund)
        ↓
   Grant Agreement
   (milestones + repayment triggers defined)
        ↓
   Recipient: Propel Community Development
        ↓
   Project Deployment
        ↓
   Revenue Source / Exit Event
   (construction close, government contract, lease-up)
        ↓
   Repayment Mechanic
   (0% interest, non-binding, recycled to next project)
```

### Key Structural Elements

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Repayment trigger** | Achievement of defined milestones (construction close, lease-up, government contract payment) |
| **Interest rate** | Typically 0% — can range from 0% to a below-market rate |
| **Duration** | 18 months (pre-development) to 10 years (enterprise-level) |
| **Legal form** | Grant agreement — NOT a loan, NOT a PRI |
| **Balance sheet** | Treated as expense — does NOT appear as a liability |
| **Covenant risk** | Unlikely to violate existing loan covenants — legally subordinate to traditional debt |
| **Foundation treatment** | Counts toward 5% annual charitable distribution requirement for private foundations |

## 3. Recoverable Grant vs. Other Capital Instruments

| Instrument | Repayment Obligation | Legal Form | Balance Sheet | Foundation Distribution Credit | Best Use Case |
|------------|----------------------|------------|---------------|-------------------------------|---------------|
| **Recoverable Grant** | Conditional / non-binding | Grant | Expense (no liability) | Yes — counts as grant | Pre-development, gap-filler, pilot programs |
| **Traditional Grant** | None | Grant | Expense | Yes | Operating support, capacity building |
| **Program-Related Investment (PRI)** | Yes — with modest return expected | Loan / Investment | Asset on balance sheet | Yes | Revenue-generating projects with demonstrated track record |
| **Forgivable Loan** | Yes — debt obligation; forgiven on milestone achievement | Loan | Liability | No — counts as investment | Working capital, emergency relief, employment incentive programs |
| **Revolving Fund** | Yes — recycling of repayments | Loan | Asset | Depends on structure | Long-term, evergreen capital deployment |
| **Senior Debt** | Yes — with interest | Loan | Liability | No | Primary construction financing |
| **Equity** | None — ownership stake | Equity | No liability | No | Reduces capital stack cost; increases IRR expectations |

### Why Recoverable Grants Are Distinct from PRIs

PRIs are labeled and treated as investments — they appear on a foundation's balance sheet as an asset and require the foundation to meet IRS program-related investment criteria. Recoverable grants are listed as expenses on the funder's statement of activities, just like traditional grants.

If a recoverable grant is structured too much like a loan — using binding language or requiring repayment — the IRS may reclassify it as a PRI or debt, which changes the accounting treatment and potentially creates adverse legal and financial consequences for both parties.

## 5. Capital Structure — How Recoverable Grants Fit

### 5.1 Where Recoverable Grants Sit in the Stack

In a typical affordable housing capital stack, layers from bottom to top:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         TRADITIONAL GRANTS (no repayment)        │  ← Top: philanthropies, public grants
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│     RECOVERABLE GRANTS (bridge/gap-filler)       │  ← Recovery on milestones; 0% interest
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│         TAX CREDITS (LIHTC/NMTC equity)          │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│      SUBORDINATE DEBT (CMF, CDFI, public)        │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│      SENIOR DEBT (construction/permanent)        │  ← Bank loan, 60–80% of TDC
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│         SPONSOR EQUITY (developer)               │  ← Bottom: at risk, highest cost
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Recoverable grants sit **above equity and senior debt** in the stack — meaning they reduce the equity requirement and improve debt service coverage ratios without adding liability.

### 5.2 Repayment Mechanics

| Trigger Type | Example | Recovery Rate |
|-------------|---------|----------------|
| **Construction close** | Grant repaid from construction loan proceeds | 100% — full recovery |
| **Lease-up achievement** | Repaid from rental income upon occupancy threshold | 80–100% |
| **Government contract payment** | Repaid when pending contract is paid | 100% |
| **Revenue milestone** | Repaid if earned income exceeds defined threshold | 50–100% |
| **Refinancing event** | Repaid from permanent financing proceeds | 100% |
| **Mission failure** | Project does not achieve objectives | 0% — grant converts to unilateral |

## 7. Eligibility — Who Qualifies for Recoverable Grants

### Standard Eligibility Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Tax-exempt status** | 501(c)(3) public charity or equivalent |
| **Organizational track record** | Demonstrated capacity to manage and report on grant funds |
| **Project pipeline** | Clear path to revenue or repayment trigger |
| **Governance** | Functional board, financial controls, audit readiness |
| **Site control** | Proof of ownership, lease, or option for real estate projects |

### Common Exclusions

- For-profit entities without expenditure responsibility mechanisms
- Religious organizations without 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor
- Projects without a credible path to repayment trigger
- General operating support (not suitable for recoverable structure)
- Advocacy-only organizations with no earned income trajectory

### CDFI Fiscal Sponsor Pathway

If Propel Community Development is not yet a 501(c)(3), a **CDFI fiscal sponsor** can receive and deploy recoverable grants on behalf of Propel. Cinnaire, LISC, and NFF all serve in this capacity.

## 10. Risk Assessment Matrix
