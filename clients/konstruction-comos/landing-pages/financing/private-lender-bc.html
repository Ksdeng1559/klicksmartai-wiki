Definition

What is a private lender in BC?

A private lender in British Columbia is a non-bank individual or company that provides mortgages secured by real estate. BC private lenders typically charge 8-15% annual interest, approve applications in 5-14 days, fund in 2-6 weeks, and accept non-conventional applications — non-stated income, bruised credit, urgent closings, unconventional properties — that Schedule I banks and most B-lenders decline.

Most BC private lenders operate through mortgage brokerages registered with the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) under the Mortgage Services Act (in force October 13, 2026). Working with a registered brokerage protects both the borrower and the lender, and ensures your mortgage is enforceable.

Decision guide

When to use a private lender (vs. CMHC, bank, or B-lender)

Private lending is the right tool for specific situations. Use this decision framework to know when it makes sense — and when it doesn't.

✓ Use a BC private lender when
  • →You need funding in under 30 days
  • →Bank or B-lender declined you
  • →Property is unconventional (raw land, construction, mixed-use, remote, distressed)
  • →Income is non-standard (self-employed, commission, foreign)
  • →You have bruised credit but strong equity
  • →Bridge for acquisition or repositioning
✗ Use CMHC MLI Select when
  • →Building or buying 5+ unit residential
  • →Project qualifies for affordability, accessibility, or energy efficiency
  • →You can wait 4-8 weeks for approval
  • →LTV 75-85% works for your deal
  • →Long-term hold (10+ years)
  • →You can tolerate CMHC admin overhead
✗ Use a Schedule I bank when
  • →Standard commercial property (office, retail, industrial)
  • →Verified income, clean credit, 30%+ down
  • →You can wait 60-90 days
  • →Best rate is the priority
  • →Loan under $5M with no construction
  • →Long-term relationship value matters

FAQ

Common questions about BC private lenders

What is a private lender in BC?

A private lender in British Columbia is a non-bank individual or company that provides mortgages secured by real estate. BC private lenders typically charge 8-15% annual interest, approve applications in 5-14 days, fund in 1-3 weeks, and accept non-conventional applications (non-stated income, bruised credit, urgent closings, unconventional properties) that Schedule I banks and most B-lenders decline.

How much do private lenders charge in BC in 2026?

BC private lenders in 2026 charge between 8% and 15% annual interest, with most deals landing at 10-12%. Lender fees range from 1% to 3% of the loan amount. Legal, appraisal, and broker fees are additional and typically total $3,000-$8,000 per deal.

When should I use a private lender instead of a bank?

Use a BC private lender when: (1) you need funding in under 30 days, (2) your application has been declined by a bank or B-lender, (3) the property is unconventional, (4) your income is non-standard, (5) you have bruised credit but strong equity, or (6) you're doing a short-term bridge.

Is private lending regulated in BC?

Yes. As of 2026-10-13, BC's Mortgage Services Act regulates mortgage brokerages, sub-brokers, and individual mortgage brokers. Private lenders who fund mortgages but do not broker them have lighter registration requirements, but most BC private lenders work through licensed mortgage brokerages (BCFSA-registered) to ensure compliance.

How fast can a BC private lender fund?

Most BC private lenders fund in 2-6 weeks from application. The fastest (Shelter Lending, Equity Loans BC, some syndicate lenders) can fund in 5-10 business days for straightforward deals with strong equity. Complex deals with construction, land assembly, or zoning changes typically take 3-6 weeks.

What is the maximum LTV from a BC private lender?

BC private lenders typically cap LTV at 65-75% for residential and 60-70% for commercial. Construction loans cap at 55-65% of completed value. Some specialty lenders (land, construction, distressed) cap at 50-60% to account for higher risk.

Do BC private lenders lend on construction projects?

Yes, but at lower LTVs (typically 55-65% of completed value) and with progress draw schedules. Most BC private construction lenders require: building permits, fixed-price contract with a qualified GC, cost-to-complete analysis, and lender-approved draw inspectors. Rates are 12-15% for construction private lending.

Can I get a private mortgage in BC with bad credit?

Yes, if you have strong equity. Most BC private lenders focus on the property's value and your down payment rather than credit score. Common minimums: 25-35% down, clear title, no recent foreclosure or bankruptcy.
