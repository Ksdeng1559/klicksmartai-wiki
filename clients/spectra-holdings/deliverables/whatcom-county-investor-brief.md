# Whatcom County Housing Investment Brief — Investor Edition (Refreshed)
**Client:** Spectra Holdings Group | **Date:** May 12, 2026 | **Classification:** Investor-facing

---

## The Opportunity

Whatcom County, Washington — home to Bellingham (pop. 93,000) — is experiencing a structural housing shortage that has pushed median home prices to **$750,000** (Feb 2026), creating a gap so severe that a household must earn **167% of Area Median Income ($180,591/year)** to afford a median-priced home with a standard 3% down payment.

This is not a cyclical dip. It's a supply-constrained market with state mandate momentum, political will for upzoning, and a formal county acknowledgement of a **5,000-unit shortfall** over five years.

For a family earning the median ($106K / year): the gap between what they can afford (~$318K) and the median home ($750K) is **$432,000**. That gap is not speculative — it's structural.

## Market Conditions (May 2026)

- **Below $750K:** <2 month inventory — firmly a seller's market
- **Above $750K:** 5+ month inventory — buyer's market emerging
- Interest rates keeping purchase demand subdued while rental demand softens
- Out-of-area cash buyers (Seattle, Bay Area) still active
- Cost of living 20.8% above national average (WWU, Q1 2025)

---

## The State Mandate Accelerator

Washington's HB 1110 (2023 Missing Middle Housing Bill) requires Bellingham to adopt middle housing zoning in all residential zones by **June 2026** — or the state overrides local zoning.

- Mayor Kim Lund signed executive order (Nov 2024) to accelerate compliance
- City Council voted April 2025 to increase density: **4→6 units per lot** within half a mile of major transit stops
- Whatcom County approved increased Birch Bay density (unanimous 7-0, June 2025)

This creates a **regulatory window**: developers and investors who move before June 2026 can shape project economics before state-mandated uniformity compresses margins.

## Entry Points for Private Capital

**1. Braided Funding Model**
Whatcom's own 5-year plan (Sept 2025) explicitly calls for combining:
- Public funding (county/state grants)
- Nonprofit partner (land trust, CDC)
- Private investment (equity, debt)

The Kulshan Community Land Trust (Kulshan CLT) is the primary local vehicle. Christian foundations and CDFIs are natural co-investors given the community development framing.

**2. Missing Middle Development**
- ADUs, duplexes, triplexes, courtyard apartments
- Lower per-unit cost than multifamily
- State mandate creates guaranteed market expansion
- First-mover advantage in neighborhoods slow to adopt

**3. Faith-Framed Investment**
Interfaith Coalition of Whatcom County is active. $6.6M county commitment signals partnership appetite. Ideal for investors seeking community development + financial return.

---

## What We're Building — Kulshan CLT Pilot

Spectra Holdings is structuring a pilot investment vehicle with Kulshan Community Land Trust to develop missing middle housing in Whatcom County, specifically targeting:
- For-sale affordability (land trust keeps cost below market)
- Community ownership structure (residents as stakeholders)
- Faith-aligned investor co-investment

The braided model reduces per-unit risk while maintaining return profile appropriate for patient community capital.

## Risk Factors

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|------------|------------|
| Interest rate spike | Medium | Rent-to-own structure; rental demand softening reduces pressure |
| State override of local zoning | Low | State mandate actually expands supply — positive for long-term |
| Seattle cash buyer competition | Medium | Focus on projects below $650K/unit where institutional buyers less active |
| Rental softening | Medium | For-sale model (land trust) de-links from rental market |
| Developer capacity | Medium | CLT partner provides local knowledge; pilot scale limits exposure |
