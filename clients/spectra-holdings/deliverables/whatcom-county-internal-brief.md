## 1. Market Reality

**The numbers have shifted materially since Q4 2025:**

| Metric | Old Estimate | Updated (Brave/Cascadia Daily, Feb 2026) |
|--------|-------------|------------------------------------------|
| Median home price | ~$650,000 | **$750,000** |
| Income needed to afford median | ~$106K (family of 4) | **$180,591 (167% AMI)** |
| Housing affordable to median earner | <30% of stock | **<30% confirmed** |
| 5-year affordable unit shortfall | ~5,000 | **~5,000 (Whatcom County 5-yr plan, Sept 2025)** |
| Inventory (under $750K) | ~2 months | **<2 months (seller's market)** |
| Inventory (over $750K) | — | **5+ months (buyer's market)** |

**Bottom line:** To buy a median-priced home in Whatcom County, a household needs to earn 167% of the Area Median Income. That's not a housing affordability problem — it's a housing exclusion problem.

## 2. Policy Context

**HB 1110 (2023 Washington Missing Middle Housing Bill)**
- Mandates middle housing (ADUs, duplexes, triplexes, courtyard apartments) in all residential zones
- Bellingham must adopt compliant zoning by **June 2026** or state overrides
- Mayor Kim Lund signed executive order (Nov 2024) accelerating compliance
- Council voted April 2025 to increase density: **4→6 units/lot** within half a mile of major transit stops

**Whatcom County 5-Year Homeless Housing Plan (Sept 2025)**
- County formally acknowledges ~5,000 unit shortfall
- $6.6M housing/infrastructure commitment
- Braided funding model (nonprofit + private + public) is the preferred mechanism

## 6. Risks

- Interest rate sensitivity: $675K home at 7% = ~$3,600/mo mortgage
- Out-of-area cash buyers still active (Seattle investors)
- State override risk if Bellingham misses June 2026 deadline (uncertain implementation)
- Renter market softening may pressure development ROI

**Recommended action:** Proceed with Kulshan CLT pilot; frame as community development + patient capital + faith-aligned. Market timing is actually favorable (buyer's market above $750K, still constrained below).
