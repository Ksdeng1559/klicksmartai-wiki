# Bexar County, TX — Community Sentiment Report

> *"Religion that is pure and undefiled before God the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their affliction, and to keep oneself unstained from the world."* — James 1:27

San Antonio operates one of the most effective homeless response systems in the country — but the scale of need is enormous and growing. With 27,012 eviction filings in 2024 (more than double the pre-COVID rate), a 115,000-unit housing deficit, and 65,920 households on affordable housing waitlists, the crisis is deeply felt in congregations, neighborhood associations, and city hall. The community's institutional infrastructure — anchored by a large Catholic diocese, active Protestant denominations, and a mature CDFI ecosystem — is ready to absorb and deploy faith-framed capital.

---

## 1. Community Perception of the Housing Crisis

San Antonio's housing crisis is visible, measurable, and generating sustained public urgency. The 27,012 eviction filings in 2024 are not abstract statistics — they represent families in specific neighborhoods, concentrated in predictable patterns.

**Geographic concentration of harm:**
- **Council District 8 (NW Side):** 15% of all city-limit evictions — the epicenter
- 30 properties drive **12% of all eviction filings** — a quantifiable intervention target
- Forest Oaks (128 of 166 units) represents the most extreme single-property eviction concentration in the county

**Community framing:**
- The San Antonio Report, Texas Housers, and Close to Home SA consistently frame the crisis as both a moral and economic imperative — language that maps directly to faith-framed investment
- The Catholic Archdiocese of San Antonio has been publicly active on housing advocacy, particularly for immigrant and Hispanic families at the intersection of poverty and displacement
- The SA Housing Authority's **~4% acceptance rate** generates significant community anger and media coverage

## 3. Displacement Risk Assessment

**Displacement risk — HIGH and accelerating:**

- Eviction filings have more than doubled from pre-COVID baseline (~13,000/yr) to 27,012 in 2024 — the rate of displacement is accelerating, not stabilizing
- 1-br rents up +14.8% and 2-br rents up +14.9% from 2020–2024 — wage growth has not kept pace
- The "missing middle" problem is driving families out of the urban core to surrounding counties (Bexar, Comal, Guadalupe) — a documented out-migration pattern
- Only 25 affordable units exist per 100 extremely low-income (ELI) households in Texas, vs. a national average of 34 — the worst affordable unit deficit in the country

**Anti-displacement infrastructure:**
- San Antonio's homeless response system demonstrated that targeted intervention works: $8.3M in rental/utility assistance prevented 2,758 evictions in FY24 alone — ROI of approximately $3,000/household vs. the far higher cost of homelessness
- $3.3M in relocation/anti-displacement funds (FY24) reached 1,237 households — a proven model that can be scaled with MCF capital

## 5. Political Risk

| Risk | Level | Note |
|------|-------|------|
| TX state-level affordable housing policy | **High** | TX is a low-regulation, low-subsidy environment — capital must be patient and creative |
| Eviction filing concentration | **Medium** | 30 properties identifiable; legal/property owner landscape is known |
| SAHA (Opportunity Home) waitlist closure | **Medium** | 65,920 waitlist frozen / cut by 65% — signals severity of system overload |
| Missing middle zoning reform | **Medium** | TPR (2026) reports SA attempting to address developer roadblocks — outcome uncertain |
| State CDBG / federal flow-through | **Medium** | TX does not expand federal housing subsidies — MCF must fill the gap directly |

**Political opportunity:**
- San Antonio's city government is actively seeking private capital to complement its $150M affordable housing bond (now in final allocation year)
- The FY24 Homeless Response system's demonstrated effectiveness (unsheltered down 25%) gives city leadership a success story to defend and expand
- SA's mayor has made housing affordability a visible administrative priority

---

## 6. Momentum Signals

**Positive momentum:**
- Unsheltered reduction **-25% (2019–2024)** vs. national **+29.7%** — the system works, it just needs more capital
- 2,458 people transitioned from shelter to housing in FY24 — a proven throughput model
- Eviction concentration (30 properties = 12% of filings) makes targeted intervention scalable
- City's 10-year affordability plan targets 95,000 cost-burdened households — an enormous but measurable goal

**Persistent headwinds:**
- TX political environment limits public subsidy leverage — MCF must be more creative and patient than in blue-state markets
- 65,920 households on affordable housing waitlists with no near-term resolution
- Rapid population growth (+1M by 2040 projected) means supply must scale faster than any current trajectory
- Family homelessness rising +11.7% YoY — the fastest-growing homeless demographic
