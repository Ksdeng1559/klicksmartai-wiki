## Where We Stand

Whatcom County has formally acknowledged the scale of its housing affordability crisis — and the data is unambiguous:

- **Median home price: $750,000** (Cascadia Daily, Feb 2026)
- **Income needed to afford a median home: $180,591/year** — that's 167% of Area Median Income
- **Less than 30%** of the housing stock in Bellingham is affordable to households earning median family income
- **5,000-unit shortfall** over five years — acknowledged in Whatcom County's own 5-Year Homeless Housing Plan (Sept 2025)
- **<2 months** inventory for homes under $750K (firmly seller's market)

---

## The Policy Response Is Active — And Moving Fast

**Washington State HB 1110 (Missing Middle Housing Bill, 2023)**
- Requires all Washington jurisdictions to allow middle housing (ADUs, duplexes, triplexes, courtyard apartments) in all residential zones
- Bellingham's compliance deadline: **June 2026** — state overrides local zoning if missed
- Mayor Kim Lund signed executive order (Nov 2024) accelerating local adoption
- City Council voted April 2025 to increase density: **4→6 units per lot** within half a mile of major transit stops

**Whatcom County Actions**
- September 2025: $6.6M housing and infrastructure investment commitment
- June 2025: County Council voted 7-0 to increase Birch Bay density (urban growth area)
- Adoption of Resolution 2025-011 (March 2025): formalized housing allocations by income band for UGAs

---

## What's Driving the Crisis

**Supply constraints:**
- Geographic: mountains, water, tribal land, ag preservation — finite buildable land
- Regulatory: single-family zoning dominance until HB 1110 mandate
- Cost: construction costs + land costs + interest rates compressing feasibility

**Demand pressures:**
- Out-of-area cash buyers (Seattle, Bay Area) with higher purchasing power
- Remote work migration from high-cost metros
- Retirement destination appeal (natural amenities, university town)

**Structural gap:**
- Family earning median income ($106K) can afford ~$318K
- Median home sells for $750K
- The $432K gap cannot be bridged by conventional financing

## The Opportunity for County Leadership

The braided funding model endorsed in the county's own 5-year plan creates a concrete pathway for public-private-nonprofit partnership:

| Funding Layer | Source | Role |
|---------------|--------|------|
| Public | Whatcom County + State grants | Infrastructure, land assembly |
| Nonprofit | Kulshan CLT, Habitat, Opportunity Council | Community anchor, land stewardship |
| Private | Faith-aligned CDFIs, community investors | Patient equity, development capacity |

This model is already active in Washington state. Enterprise Community Partners, Opportunity Finance Network, and Washington CDFIs are actively seeking projects.
