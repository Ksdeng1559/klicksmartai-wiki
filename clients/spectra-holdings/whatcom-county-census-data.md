## Housing Type Inventory (Missing Middle Gap)

Whatcom County has a severe shortage of missing-middle housing types — duplexes, triplexes, townhomes, cottage courts, courtyard apartments, and ADUs. These types historically filled the gap between single-family homes and apartment buildings.

The county's housing stock skews heavily toward large-lot single-family. Missing-middle types represent a small fraction of total units, creating a gap in options for workforce households earning 80–120% AMI.

> **Note on missing-middle data:** The Whatcom County Housing Needs Analysis (Appendix I, 2026) is the authoritative source for housing type inventory by units in structure. It was not yet retrieved at time of writing. This section should be updated with that data once the document is sourced.

| Cause | % citing |
|-------|---------|
| Housing affordability | 47% |
| Job loss / unemployment | 37% |
| Mental illness | 28% |
| Family rejection / kicked out | 24% |

| Alcohol / substance use | 22% |
| Domestic violence | 20% |

> 69% of homeless individuals lost their last residence in Whatcom County — they were not newcomers. The crisis is local and structural.

## Why This Matters for Spectra / Kulshan CLT

- Kulshan CLT builds **permanently affordable** housing (LaFreniere Court, Thornton Road, Telegraph Townhomes) — the exact income tier Where the supply gap is most acute
- The $300M MCF pilot targets this geography because the affordability crisis creates forced demand for the product being built
- Census data validates: demand is **structural, not cyclical** — population growing, supply constrained, affordability gap wide, vacancy near-zero
- 47% of homeless households cite affordability as the primary cause — the market failure Spectra/Kulshan are directly addressing
