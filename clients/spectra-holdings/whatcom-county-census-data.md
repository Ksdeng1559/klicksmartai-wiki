# Whatcom County, WA — Census & Housing Brief

**For:** Spectra Holdings Group — MCF Whatcom County Pilot
**Compiled:** 2026-05-08
**Focus:** Kulshan CLT pipeline / LaFreniere Court / Thornton Road / Telegraph Townhomes
**Audience:** Investor brief / National Christian Foundation donor program

## The Crisis in Numbers

Every statistic below represents a family, a child, a neighbor — someone Image in the image of God, caught in a housing market that has turned its back on them. Whatcom County is not just short on housing. It is short on *justice*.

---

## Population

Whatcom County sits in the northwestern corner of Washington State, anchored by Bellingham. Its population has grown steadily for over a decade, adding nearly **10,000 new residents** in the last five years alone.

| Metric | Value | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Population (2025 est.) | **236,392** | `[1]` |
| Population (2020 census) | ~226,852 | `[1]` |
| Growth 2020–2025 | **+4.2%** (+9,540 persons) | `[1]` |
| Growth vs. 2010 | **+14.7%** | `[2]` |
| Persons per household | **2.40** | `[1]` |
| Foreign-born residents | **9.93%** (2024) | `[2]` |

> Growth is not the problem — it is the *context*. New families arrive seeking the good life this region offers. The housing supply has not kept pace. The market has failed them.

## Housing Stock

Whatcom County has **106,281** housing units as of 2024. On the surface this sounds adequate. But the units are the wrong types, in the wrong places, at the wrong prices.

| Metric | Value | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Housing units (2024) | **106,281** | `[1]` |
| Owner-occupied rate | **63.7%** | `[1]` |
| Rental vacancy rate | ~3% (healthy: 5–7%) | `[3]` |
| Owner vacancy rate | ~1% (healthy: 2%) | `[3]` |
| Building permits issued (2024) | **1,190** | `[1]` |

> Vacancy rates well below healthy thresholds are a market distress signal — not a sign of strength. When almost every rental unit is spoken for, there is no buffer for families in crisis, no flexibility for workers relocating for jobs, no room for the Holy Spirit to move someone into stability.

## Housing Costs & Affordability

This is where the wound is deepest.

| Metric | Value | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Median home value | **$585,800** | `[1]` |
| Median gross rent | **$1,555/mo** | `[1]` |
| 1-br rent (Q1 2024) | $1,320/mo | `[3]` |
| 2-br rent (Q1 2024) | $1,605/mo (+2.6% YoY) | `[3]` |
| Rent burden (≥30% income on housing) | **>50% of renters** | `[3]` |

### The Affordability Gap

At standard underwriting — **30% gross debt-to-income ratio** — the median household earning $81,784 can support a mortgage of approximately **$385,000**. The median home sells for $585,800. The gap: **$200,000+**.

| Income Tier | Affordable Home Price | Median Home Price | Gap | Source |
|-------------|----------------------|-------------------|-----|--------|
| Workforce household (median) | ~$385,000 | $585,800 | **$200K+** | `[1]` + `[3]` |

> For every family that *cannot* afford the median home, there is a family that *should* be able to stay. The market is not neutral. It is selecting for wealth, and the people doing the daily work of this community — teachers, nurses, construction workers, social servants — are being priced out of the very neighborhoods they serve.

> These are not poverty wages. At $81,784 median household income, Whatcom County families are working — and still, half of renters are cost-burdened. The problem is not fecklessness. The problem is **structural**: wages have not kept pace with the cost of housing, and the supply of homes at attainable price points has not kept pace with population growth.

## Homelessness & Social Impact (2024)

The Point-in-Time Count and the housing pool data reveal a crisis that is **local, not imported**. 69% of homeless individuals lost their last residence *in Whatcom County* — they are our neighbors, not strangers.

| Metric | Value | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Housing Pool — total households | **684** (328 unsheltered, 351 sheltered) | `[4]` |
| Emergency shelter PITC | 465 households (↓11% from 2023) | `[4]` |
| K-12 homeless students (2024–25) | **972** (~4% of pre-K enrollment) | `[4]` |
| Households receiving services (2023) | ~1,912 (~2,000+ individuals) | `[4]` |
| Unsheltered families | +131% since 2020 | `[4]` |

|-------|---------|--------|
| **Housing affordability** | **47%** | `[4]` |
| Job loss / unemployment | 37% | `[4]` |
| Mental illness | 28% | `[4]` |
| Family rejection / kicked out | 24% | `[4]` |
| Physical health / disability | 22% | `[4]` |

> Nearly half of all homelessness in Whatcom County traces back to a single, actionable cause: **the cost of housing**. This is not fate. This is a market failure — and market failures can be corrected.

## The Missing Middle

Whatcom County's housing stock skews heavily toward large-lot single-family homes. Duplexes, triplexes, townhomes, cottage courts, courtyard apartments, and ADUs — the housing types that historically enabled economic diversity in neighborhoods — represent a small fraction of total inventory.

This "missing middle" is precisely where Kulshan Community Land Trust builds. The CLT model produces permanently affordable homeownership at density that pencil works for cities and counties.

> **CLT Impact:** When Kulshan builds, the home stays affordable *in perpetuity* — not for 15 years, not for one generation. The asset remains in the community. The equity stays with the family. The land is held in trust for the next steward.

## CDFI Intervention Thesis

Whatcom County is a **compelling MCF pilot market** for Spectra Holdings Group:

**Demand is structural, not cyclical**
Population growth + constrained supply + near-zero vacancy = sustained demand for attainable housing regardless of economic cycle.

**Affordability gap is wide and quantified**
$200K+ gap between what the workforce can afford and the median home price. This gap is *why* families fall into rental burden, and *why* they eventually fall into homelessness.

**Homelessness is locally generated**
69% of homeless individuals lost housing *in* Whatcom County. The crisis is not being imported. Every intervention dollar stays local.

**The solution is proven**
Kulshan CLT has a track record — LaFreniere Court, Thornton Road, Telegraph Townhomes — and is ready to deploy capital at scale with the MCF's patient, below-market financing.

**Faith-aligned impact is measurable**
972 homeless students. 47% citing affordability. Families sleeping in cars because $1,605/month for a two-bedroom is simply more than they earn. These are faces, not statistics — and they are addressable.
