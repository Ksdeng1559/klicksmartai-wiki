> *"Religion that is pure and undefiled before God the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their affliction, and to keep oneself unstained from the world."* — James 1:27

# 1. Executive Housing Sentiment Summary

Bexar County presents a distinctive housing sentiment environment defined by strong institutional recognition of need and significant grassroots pressure, set against a city with one of the most effective homeless response systems in the country.

San Antonio's municipal leadership, housing authority, and nonprofit ecosystem have already demonstrated the capacity to coordinate large-scale housing responses. The city's Point-in-Time count, landlord engagement programs, and rapid rehousing infrastructure provide a proven platform for deploying additional affordable housing capital. Sentiment at the city and county level is supportive of solutions that deliver permanent affordability.

At the neighborhood level, sentiment mirrors other high-growth Texas metros: rapid appreciation has displaced long-term residents, eviction rates have more than doubled since pre-COVID, and homeownership has become unreachable for median-income households. Community concern is focused on stability, displacement, and whether new housing will actually remain affordable rather than appreciating into the market.

The faith and CDFI community represents the strongest sentiment asset. San Antonio's institutional religious infrastructure and mature community development financial ecosystem are primed for a faith-framed capital deployment — a positioning that is both authentic to the community and aligned with Spectra's investment thesis.

# 2. Housing Need Sentiment

## Sentiment Level

**Urgent and Widely Recognized**

## Core Signals

- 115,125-unit housing deficit documented in San Antonio Housing analysis.
- Median home value of $235,700 — roughly double 2020 levels — has pushed homeownership out of reach for median-income households.
- 65,920 households on affordable housing waitlists — demand far exceeds current supply capacity.
- 27,012 eviction filings in 2024 — more than double pre-COVID rates — reflecting acute rental instability.
- San Antonio's homeless response system is nationally recognized, but the pipeline of permanent affordable housing remains insufficient to meet demand.
- Displacement pressure is felt acutely in historically low-income neighborhoods, where property tax increases and speculative investment have accelerated gentrification.

## Interpretation

The community broadly recognizes that the housing crisis is real and deepening. Unlike markets where affordability is a technocratic debate, San Antonio residents experience it as an immediate daily stress — in their churches, neighborhoods, schools, and city council meetings. The challenge is not awareness. The challenge is capacity: translating recognition into delivered units.

## Messaging Implication

Frame housing delivery as scaling a proven response, not inventing a new solution:

> San Antonio has built the systems. Spectra is here to bring additional permanent capital to scale what is already working.

# 3. Institutional and Government Housing Sentiment

## Sentiment Level

**Supportive and Experienced**

## Supporting Signals

- Opportunity Home San Antonio (the housing authority) manages 55,000+ clients and operates one of the most active landlord engagement and rapid rehousing programs in the country.
- City of San Antonio's NHS (Neighborhood Housing Services) Department runs multiple affordability programs and has demonstrated capacity to co-invest in housing delivery.
- Bexar County has engaged in criminal justice and housing coordination through the Bexar County Criminal Justice Advisory Board and related initiatives.
- San Antonio's homeless response Continuum of Care is recognized nationally as an effective coordinated entry system.
- Texas Housers and other local advocacy organizations have built a documented evidence base that strengthens municipal case-making for affordable housing investment.

## Interpretation

City and county officials are experienced housing policy practitioners who will engage seriously with partners that bring real capital, delivery discipline, and permanent affordability commitments. They will be skeptical of speculative development framed as affordability. The key to institutional engagement is demonstrating:

- actual construction and delivery capacity,
- permanent affordability controls (not just rent-restricted units that convert after compliance periods),
- alignment with San Antonio's existing homeless response and affordability pipeline,
- and respect for local political constraints, including Texas's property tax environment.

## Engagement Strategy

Spectra should enter government conversations as:

**A mission-capital partner with construction delivery capacity, here to add permanent affordable units to San Antonio's proven housing pipeline — not to displace or duplicate existing efforts.**

Lead with:

- permanent affordability (CLT / deed restrictions),
- alignment with existing CoC and housing authority systems,
- capital stack that does not depend on HOME bond authority limitations,
- local hiring and community benefit commitments.

# 4. Resident Housing Sentiment

## Sentiment Level

**Anxious and Stability-Focused, Persuadable with Right Framing**

## Likely Supportive Groups

- renters facing eviction or rent increases,
- households on the 65,920-unit waitlist,
- service workers, hospitality workers, healthcare support staff,
- teachers and municipal workers priced out of homeownership,
- long-term San Antonio residents facing displacement from appreciating neighborhoods,
- formerly homeless individuals and families in the CoC pipeline,
- faith congregations with members experiencing housing instability,
- employers facing worker recruitment and retention challenges due to housing costs.

## Likely Concerned Groups

- homeowners worried about property tax increases tied to new development,
- residents in appreciating neighborhoods who view rapid change as displacement,
- landlords who have benefited from tight rental markets and may resist rent stability provisions,
- some neighborhood associations accustomed to single-family zoning who will resist missing-middle density.

## Core Resident Concerns

- will this housing actually remain affordable, or will it flip to market rate after a few years,
- will new development bring property tax increases that price out existing homeowners,
- will rental housing stability programs actually reach the households on the waitlist,
- will construction disruption and gentrification accelerate in my neighborhood,
- will the homeless response system actually connect people to permanent housing,
- and is there a credible plan for the 115,000+ unit deficit, or is this another pilot that won't move the needle.

## Engagement Strategy

The resident-facing message must answer the permanence question first:

> Every unit delivered through this program carries permanent affordability restrictions — not just a 15-year compliance period, but genuine, enforceable, community-controlled long-term affordability.

Recommended language:

> This is about helping San Antonio families who are here today — the teacher, the nurse, the service worker — stay in the community they help sustain.

# 5. Housing Affordability Sentiment

## Sentiment Level

**High Emotional Salience / Crisis-Level Urgency**

## Interpretation

Affordability is the dominant housing sentiment across all San Antonio stakeholder groups. It is not abstract: families are on 65,920-household waitlists, eviction filings have doubled, and homeownership is unreachable for median-income households in a market where the median home has doubled to $235,700 in six years.

Even residents who are not currently housing-insecure have family members, coworkers, or congregants who are. The affordability crisis has a face in every pew, every school parent meeting, and every neighborhood association.

## Messaging Opportunity

Use specific, local human-scale framing:

- the hospitality worker who cannot afford to live in the city where they serve tourists,
- the teacher who commutes 45 minutes because San Antonio homes are out of reach,
- the senior on a fixed income facing eviction after decades in the same home,
- the family that has been on the waitlist for three years.

## Avoid

Abstract supply-side language alone. "Housing deficit" and "absorption pace" are not resonant with residents. Pair every data point with a human consequence.

# 6. Eviction and Displacement Sentiment

## Sentiment Level

**Alarmed and Action-Oriented**

## Core Issue

27,012 eviction filings in 2024 — more than double pre-COVID rates — signals a rental market under severe stress. This is not a technical housing problem; it is a household stability crisis playing out in San Antonio's courts, shelters, and congregations every week.

## Interpretation

High eviction rates create both urgency and political opportunity. Evictions generate visible community costs — shelter utilization, school instability, emergency service demand, congregational support burden — that extend well beyond the immediate housing transaction. Local leaders are aware of these costs even when they cannot fully articulate them.

## Strategic Implication

Position permanent affordable housing as an eviction prevention mechanism, not just a supply-side response:

> Every permanently affordable unit delivered is a household that does not enter the eviction pipeline, a shelter bed that remains available for the most acute cases, and a school district that does not have to absorb another displaced family.

# 7. CLT / Permanent Affordability Sentiment

## Sentiment Level

**Strong Positive — Especially Among Faith and CDFI Stakeholders**

## Why CLT / Permanent Affordability Matters in San Antonio

San Antonio's institutional community — particularly its faith-based and CDFI sectors — has deep experience with community development models that prioritize permanent stewardship over market returns. The Catholic Diocese of San Antonio, local CDFIs, and community development corporations operating in the city are philosophically aligned with affordability models that remove units from speculative circulation.

The CLT model directly answers San Antonio's most pressing housing sentiment question: **will this housing actually stay affordable?**

## Strategic Benefits

CLT structure supports:

- permanent affordability without government subsidy recapture risk,
- local stewardship through community land trust governance,
- resale restrictions that prevent speculative appreciation,
- community trust through transparent mission alignment,
- and mission-aligned capital from faith-based funders who want durable impact.

## Messaging Implication

Lead with the permanence answer:

> These are not rent-stabilized units with an expiration date. These are permanently removed from the speculative market — owned in trust for the community, now and for future generations.

# 8. Infrastructure Sentiment

## Sentiment Level

**Moderate Concern — Manageable with Proper Planning**

## Core Concerns in San Antonio / Bexar County

- water and wastewater capacity in rapidly growing suburban corridors,
- transportation infrastructure connecting job centers to available affordable housing sites,
- floodplain constraints in low-lying areas of Bexar County,
- school capacity in high-growth areas where affordable housing demand is highest,
- and neighborhood infrastructure strain in areas receiving both rapid growth and affordable housing investment.

## Interpretation

San Antonio's infrastructure concerns are more manageable than in comparable Texas metros. The city's established grid, existing utility corridors, and relatively flat geography make infill and transit-oriented development feasible. The risk is not infrastructure paralysis but rather misallocation of sites to locations where capacity constraints create unnecessary opposition.

## Mitigation Strategy

Spectra should commit to:

- site selection that prioritizes infill locations with existing utility capacity,
- early coordination with SAWS (San Antonio Water System), CPS Energy, and TxDOT,
- phased deployment that does not over-commit before infrastructure assumptions are validated,
- and realistic timeline language that accounts for Texas local government review processes.

# 9. Density and Neighborhood Character Sentiment

## Sentiment Level

**Sensitive — Texas-Specific Nuance Required**

## Interpretation

San Antonio has a complex relationship with density shaped by distinct dynamics: historic neighborhoods with strong preservation identities, Texas's single-family zoning tradition, rapid suburban growth that has displaced long-term residents, and a downtown core that has invested heavily in revitalization and fears losing momentum.

Missing-middle housing — cottage courts, townhomes, small multifamily — is more likely to gain acceptance than large-scale apartment complexes, but only if framed with explicit community benefit commitments and design sensitivity.

## Recommended Framing

> This is not market-rate densification. This is workforce and affordable housing integrated into existing neighborhoods — designed to match community scale, serve local residents, and remain permanently affordable.

# 10. Landowner Housing Sentiment

## Sentiment Level

**Opportunity-Oriented with Texas-Specific Constraints**

## San Antonio / Bexar County Context

San Antonio's housing market has appreciated rapidly — median home values have roughly doubled since 2020 — creating significant landowner equity but also significant tax displacement anxiety as appraisals rise. Landowners in transitioning neighborhoods face a complex set of pressures:

**Likely Motivations:**

- fair value for underutilized or surplus land,
- relief from property tax burden through charitable disposition,
- participation in a mission-aligned project with institutional credibility,
- ground lease income streams that provide retirement cash flow without full sale,
- reduced entitlement risk through public-private partnership with known institutional partners.

**Likely Concerns:**

- Texas's limitation on municipal authority to condemn property (Eminent Domain restrictions),
- uncertainty about the depth and durability of the affordable housing market,
- concern about construction risk and timeline,
- community resistance that could complicate permitting,
- and whether faith-based or CDFI partners will have the operational capacity to close.

## Recommended Engagement

Offer flexible participation options: outright sale, ground lease, phased disposition, joint development. Position participation as:

**Fair value plus enduring community legacy — and a partner with the capital discipline and mission accountability that protects the landowner's reputation.**

# 11. Faith and Nonprofit Housing Sentiment

## Sentiment Level

**Strongly Positive — San Antonio's Most Distinctive Sentiment Asset**

## Core Strength

San Antonio's institutional religious infrastructure is one of the most significant and distinctive community assets available for a faith-framed housing capital deployment. The Catholic Diocese of San Antonio is one of the largest in the United States. Active Protestant denominations, megachurches with established community investment arms, and Catholic Charities of San Antonio provide a ready network for community trust-building, congregational engagement, and mission-capital mobilization.

This is not a community where faith-framed capital must be explained or justified. It is a community where faith-framed capital is **expected** and **demanded** by institutional stakeholders who want to know that capital is pursuing durable impact, not just financial return.

## Messaging Themes

- families staying together in community — children not displaced from schools, faith communities, networks,
- workers living near their places of worship and employment,
- permanent affordability as a spiritual commitment — housing that serves the least of these, not the most profitable tenant,
- stewardship of capital for multigenerational community impact,
- and Catholic and Protestant teaching on land, property, and the common good as the philosophical foundation for the investment thesis.

## Recommended Approach

Engage the Catholic Diocese of San Antonio and Catholic Charities as primary institutional anchors. Engage Protestant community development arms (Lutheran Services, Methodist Local Church community development corporations, SBC-affiliated community development organizations) as secondary partners. Position Spectra's capital not as charity but as **justice-informed investment** — correcting a market failure that has displaced the communities these institutions serve.

# 15. Recommended Engagement Sequence

## Step 1 — Faith and CDFI Institutional Alignment

Engage Catholic Charities of San Antonio and the Catholic Diocese of San Antonio as primary institutional anchors. Simultaneously engage Opportunity Home San Antonio and the City's NHS Department to confirm project alignment with existing housing authority priorities.

## Step 2 — City of San Antonio / Bexar County Listening Session

Meet with the City of San Antonio's NHS Department, the County Judge's housing staff, and relevant City Council members to understand housing priorities, infrastructure concerns, site availability, and political constraints specific to Bexar County.

## Step 3 — Site Feasibility and Infrastructure Review

Validate water, wastewater, transportation, and school capacity at candidate sites. Confirm that CLT site acquisition can proceed within existing regulatory and entitlement timelines.

## Step 4 — Community Benefit and Permanence Narrative

Prepare clear resident-facing explanation of permanent affordability — what it means, how it is enforced, and why it matters for San Antonio families. Engage local faith leaders as messengers, not just endorsers.

## Step 5 — Capital Stack Presentation

Present MCF, CDFI, CMF, and faith institution capital only after the housing benefit story and institutional partnership framework are clear. CDFIs and faith institutions should be co-investors from the beginning, not just funders at the back end.

# 16. Go / No-Go Implications

## Go Conditions

Proceed if:

- Catholic Diocese / Catholic Charities of San Antonio alignment is confirmed,
- Opportunity Home San Antonio and City NHS Department view Spectra as a constructive capital partner,
- at least two viable sites with infrastructure capacity are identified,
- CDFI co-investment terms are confirmed,
- CLT governance structure is established with local community representation,
- and faith institution participation is secured as both capital and community legitimacy.

## No-Go / Pause Conditions

Pause if:

- institutional partners (housing authority, diocese, CDFIs) are not aligned on the partnership structure,
- site feasibility reveals infrastructure constraints that cannot be resolved within project economics,
- community resistance forms before the permanence and community benefit narrative is established,
- capital stack cannot be assembled without dependence on HOME bond authority (Texas political risk),
- or faith institution participation is not secured and the project would rely purely on conventional CDFI capital.

# 17. Executive Conclusion

Bexar County and San Antonio present one of the strongest community sentiment environments for a faith-framed, permanent affordability housing capital deployment in Spectra's pipeline. The city's nationally recognized homeless response system, mature CDFI ecosystem, large Catholic diocese, and documented 115,125-unit housing deficit create a clear demand signal. The question is not whether San Antonio needs this housing — it clearly does. The question is whether Spectra can assemble the institutional partnerships, site control, and capital stack to deliver units that are permanently, measurably, and enforceably affordable.

**Final Housing Sentiment Assessment:** Supportive — institutional and faith infrastructure is strong; execution capacity and partnership alignment are the primary variables.

**Recommended Action:** Proceed with institutional partner engagement — faith, CDFI, and housing authority — before investor positioning. Build the permanence narrative with San Antonio's own leaders as the primary voice.
