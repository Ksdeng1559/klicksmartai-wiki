# Whatcom County Corridor Brief — Semiahmoo / Birch Bay / Blaine

> *"The harvest is plentiful, but the workers are few."* — Matthew 9:37

---

## Corridor Overview

Three distinct sub-markets form a single economic and housing corridor along the northern Whatcom County coastline and border:

| Sub-Market | Character | Primary Role in Corridor |
|------------|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Semiahmoo** | Upper-income enclave, resort/seasonal | Premium residential; high-value comps |
| **Birch Bay** | Dense CDP, middle-income | Workforce housing pressure zone |
| **Blaine** | City, commercial border gateway | Primary residential base; affordability anchor |

Total corridor footprint: ~23,000 residents across three distinct sub-markets.

---

## Population & Growth

| Sub-Market | Population | Growth | Density |
|------------|------------|--------|---------|
| **Blaine** | 6,100 | +14.8% (2019–2024) | ~2,500/sq mi (city) |
| **Birch Bay CDP** | 10,837 | Steady | 634/sq mi |
| **Semiahmoo** | ~800–1,200 est. | Seasonal spike | ~800/sq mi |
| **Corridor Total** | ~17,000–23,000 | Growing | — |

Blaine is the fastest-growing municipality in Whatcom County. The 2026 projected pop of 6,461 represents continued acceleration.

> **Key signal:** Blaine's 14.8% growth rate (vs. Whatcom County's 4.2%) means the corridor is growing faster than the county average — demand pressure is concentrated here, not in Bellingham.

### Affordability Gap — Birch Bay vs. Workforce
- Median home value: $477,200 (Birch Bay)
- At standard underwriting: workforce income \~$86K supports ~$370K mortgage
- **Gap: ~$107K** at the corridor level
- Semiahmoo ($792K) is completely out of reach for the median worker

> **Critical finding:** 18.1% vacancy in Semiahmoo signals heavy short-term rental / seasonal conversion — supply is being pulled out of the permanent housing market. This is a direct cause of Birch Bay workforce housing pressure.

## The Missing Middle Problem

The corridor's affordability gap is structural:

1. **Semiahmoo** pulls the median comp upward (luxury/seasonal) and converts units to short-term rental
2. **Birch Bay** absorbs spillover demand at $477K — still $107K above what median workers can afford
3. **Blaine** is the only sub-market with realistic affordability anchor potential — if CLT-style deed restrictions can be applied

The *missing middle* products most needed:
- Cottage courts (400–800 sq ft, $220–280K)
- Townhomes (1,000–1,400 sq ft, $300–380K)
- ADUs / carriage units (400–650 sq ft, $180–250K)

## Recent Development Activity (2025–2026)

### September 2025 — $6.6M Whatcom County Investment
- **Telegraph Townhomes**: $400K grant (24 affordable ownership units, Kulshan CLT / Habitat for Humanity)
- **LaFreniere Court**: $350K grant (18 affordable units, Kulshan CLT)
- Both projects are in the Blaine/Birch Bay corridor pipeline
- This is the county putting capital behind exactly the CLT model

### 2026 — Comprehensive Plan Update
- Whatcom County undergoing once-a-decade mandatory update (state deadline)
- Forecasts **67,000 additional residents by 2045** — a 28% increase
- Final review underway; adoption expected May 2026
- Blaine still working through its municipal plan
- Key debate: housing supply, urban growth areas, floodplain/climate constraints

> **Investor implication:** The comprehensive plan will shape *where* 67K new residents can live. Corridor land that is currently unbuildable may become entitled — existing landowners (including CLT-adjacent projects) stand to benefit significantly.

## Faith-Frame Anchor

The corridor crisis is a **workforce housing crisis**, not a poverty crisis:
- Corridor median incomes ($84–87K) are above county median
- Workers who serve the community — teachers, nurses, first responders, hospitality workers — cannot afford to live where they work
- 18% Semiahmoo vacancy + seasonal conversion = housing stock actively removed from permanent market
- Kulshan CLT deed restrictions keep homes permanently affordable across resales — the only model that solves the *forever* problem

> *"Love your neighbor as yourself."* — Mark 12:31
> 
> The neighbor who cannot afford to live nearby cannot fully serve or be served by the community.

---

## Strategic Implications for Kulshan CLT / MCF Pipeline

1. **Birch Bay** is the primary pressure zone — density and demographics support townhome / cottage court product
2. **Blaine** is the most likely expansion site for future CLT phases — city government is cooperative, county is already co-investing
3. **Semiahmoo** is a market comp and a cautionary tale — what happens when the market goes full seasonal/short-term
4. **Telegraph Townhomes + LaFreniere Court** are the current pipeline; Thornton Road may be the next expansion
5. **2026 comprehensive plan adoption** will clarify buildable capacity — monitor Blaine's municipal plan status
