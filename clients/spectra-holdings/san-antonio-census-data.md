# San Antonio, TX — Housing & Community Impact Data Brief

**Prepared for:** Spectra Holdings Group | Investor & Foundation Partner Due Diligence
**Geography:** City of San Antonio / Bexar County, Texas
**Date:** May 2026
**Audience Fit:** Investors · National Christian Foundation Donors · Advertorial Content

---

> *"The harvest is plentiful, but the workers are few."* — Matthew 9:37
>
> In San Antonio, the harvest is the urgent need for decent, affordable housing. And the workers — developers, CDFIs, faith-based funders — are few relative to the scale of need.

San Antonio is one of America's fastest-growing cities — and one of its most housing-burdened. For investors and foundation partners seeking measurable, God-honoring impact, Bexar County presents a large-scale opportunity to deploy capital that transforms communities, stabilizes families, and breaks cycles of poverty through the simple dignity of a safe, affordable home.

---

## 1. Population [1][14]

San Antonio is the **7th largest city in the United States**, now home to approximately **1.53 million residents** as of 2024. [1]

- Population, 2020 Census: **1,434,625**
- Population, 2024 estimate: **1,526,656** [1]
- Population growth (2020→2024): **+6.4%** — one of the top 5 fastest-growing large cities in the U.S. [1][3]
- Projected growth: +1 million additional residents by 2040 [7][8]
- Population density: Bexar County metro: **2.51 million** [8]
- Foreign-born residents: **15.0%** [1][14]
- Median age: **34.9 years** [14]
- Female persons: **50.5%** [1]

**Investor/Foundation Lens:** Rapid population growth means demand for housing outpaces supply every year. This is not a stable or declining market — it is an expanding one where underbuilding has created structural affordability gaps that private and philanthropic capital can address with real impact.

---

## 2. Demographics [1][14][15]

San Antonio is a young, majority-Hispanic city with deep economic diversity:

- **Hispanic or Latino: 64.6%** of population [1]
- White (non-Hispanic): **23.0%** [1]
- Black or African American: **6.9%** [1]
- Asian: **3.0%** [1]
- Two or More Races: **36.2%** (broadly defined in Census counts) [1]
- Persons under 18: **23.6%** [1]
- Persons 65+: **13.4%** [1]
- Poverty rate: **17.1%** — well above the national average of 12.5% [1][14]
  - Largest poverty demographics: Females 25–34, Females 18–24, Females 35–44 [14]
- High school graduate or higher (age 25+): **84.6%** [1]
- Bachelor's degree or higher (age 25+): **29.0%** [1]
- Veterans: **90,474** [1]
- Language other than English spoken at home: **40.4%** [1]

**Foundation Lens:** San Antonio's demographic reality — a young, predominantly Hispanic city with elevated poverty and below-average educational attainment — means that community development investment is both a social imperative and a biblical mandate to serve "the least of these" (Matthew 25:40). Housing stability is a gateway to health, education, and intergenerational economic mobility.

## 3. Housing Stock [1][8][14][16]

- **Total housing units (Bexar County metro):** ~903,779 [8]
- **Units needed to meet demand:** ~1,018,904 [8]
- **Housing deficit:** ~115,125 units [8]
- Owner-occupied rate: **52.2%** (below the national average of 65.2%) [1][14]
- Renter-occupied: **47.8%** [16]
- Median value of owner-occupied housing units (2024): **$235,700** [1][14]
  - ↑ Up 7.28% from $219,700 in 2023 [14]
  - National median comparison: $332,700 [14]
- Median monthly mortgage (with mortgage, 2024): **$1,801** [1]
- Median monthly mortgage (without mortgage): **$623** [1]
- Median gross rent (2024): **$1,324** [1]
  - Average rent (RentCafe/Yardi, 2026): **$1,255** [16]
  - 1-bedroom average: $1,081 | 2-bedroom average: $1,377 | 3-bedroom: $1,797 [16]
- Rent trajectory (2014→2024): $850 → $1,300 — a **53% increase in 10 years** [7]
- Home price trajectory (2014→2024): $188,000 → $290,000 — a **54% increase in 10 years** [7]
- Building permits (2024): [1] — market undersupply persists [8]

**Investor Lens:** San Antonio's median home value ($235,700) remains below the national median ($332,700), making it an **accessible entry point for workforce housing development**. Yet rapid appreciation (7.28% in one year) is pricing out local buyers. Mis-sized for-sale inventory and insufficient new construction permits create opportunity for developers who can deliver affordably-priced product at scale.

## 4. Household Income & Affordability [1][11][14]

- **Median household income (2024): $65,056** — among the lowest of all top 25 metros [11]
  - ↑ Up 3.4% from $62,917 (2023) [14]
- Per capita income: **$33,929** [1]
- Metro area median (8-county SA metro, 2024): **$78,000** [11]
- Household income distribution:
  - < $25,000: ~15% of households
  - $25,000–$49,999: ~25%
  - $50,000–$74,999: ~22%
  - $75,000–$99,999: ~17%
  - $100,000–$149,999: ~14%
  - $150,000+: ~7% [15]

**Affordability Gap — Workforce Housing Crisis:**

A tenant earning median income ($29,805 median individual wage) **cannot afford the median rent** of $1,275 without being cost-burdened. [8]

- Income needed to qualify for median rent ($1,275): **$45,900/year** [8]
- Affordability gap: **$16,095** — workers need to earn 35% more to afford median rent [8]
- Income needed to afford median home ($290,000): ~**$75,000/year** (assuming 30-year mortgage, 7% rate, 3.5% down)
- Share of households earning below $50,000: **~40%** [15]

**The "Missing Middle" Gap:**

San Antonio's housing market has a structural hole between single-family detached homes and large apartment complexes. Missing middle housing types — duplexes, triplexes, townhomes, bungalows, casitas — are critically undersupplied. [10]

- Proposed city program target: 2-to-20 unit multi-family projects [10]
- Challenge: smaller projects lack economies of scale, face financing gaps, and hit regulatory roadblocks [10]
- Urban core is **losing families** to surrounding counties due to lack of suitable housing options [10]

**Investor/Foundation Lens:** The gap between what San Antonians earn and what housing costs is the clearest market signal in this brief. CDFI and foundation capital deployed as gap financing, predevelopment grants, or below-market loans can unlock missing middle development that neither pure market-rate developers nor traditional affordable housing programs address alone.

## 5. Homelessness & Social Impact [2][3][4]

San Antonio's homeless response system has achieved notable results — but the scale of need still demands intervention.

### Point-in-Time Count (PIT) — January 2024 [2][3]

| Category | Count |
|---|---|
| Total experiencing homelessness | **3,372** |
| Sheltered | **2,484** |
| Unsheltered | **888** |
| Year-over-year increase | **+6.8%** |
| As % of total population | **0.01%** (relatively flat vs. growth) |

**Sheltered breakdown:** [3]
- Emergency shelter: 2,181 persons
- Transitional housing: 276 persons
- Safe Haven: 27 persons

**Unsheltered breakdown:** [3]
- Persons in families with children (unsheltered): 168
- Persons only (unsheltered): 888
- Chronically homeless (unsheltered): 888 total; 433 in shelters [3]

### System Performance — FY2024 [2]

- **4,548 people** transitioned from unsheltered → shelter (2,090) or shelter → housing (2,458) [2]
- **2,921 individuals** were new to homelessness or returned (2,271 new; 650 returned) [2]
- **$8.3 million** in rental and utility assistance provided to **2,758 households** (prevention) [2]
- **$3.3 million** in relocation assistance to **1,237 households** (anti-displacement) [2]
- Emergency shelter capacity increased **10.4%** from 2023→2024 [4]

### Five-Year Trends — San Antonio vs. National Peers [2]

| Metric | San Antonio | National |
|---|---|---|
| Change in homelessness per 10K (2015–2023) | **+0.4%** | +10.7% |
| Change in unsheltered homelessness (2019–2024) | **−25%** | +29.7% (U.S.) |
| Share of homeless population unsheltered (2024) | **26.3%** | 35.5% (U.S.) |
| Chronic homelessness (% of total) | **14%** | 20% (U.S.) |
| Family homelessness change (2023→2024) | **+11.7%** | +39.4% (U.S.) |

**San Antonio is outperforming national trends** on unsheltered reduction — its homeless response system works. But rising overall numbers and growing family homelessness [4] mean the prevention side (keeping people housed) needs more investment.

### Poverty & Housing Cost Burden [4][7][9]

- **Bexar County poverty rate:** 18% — higher than the national average of 11.5% [4]
- **Rent burden:** 1-br rents up 14.8% (2020–2024); 2-br rents up 14.9% (2020–2024) [4]
- **Extremely low-income (ELI) renter households in Texas:** ~900,000 [9]
- **Texas ELI renters who are cost-burdened (≥30% income on rent):** 91% [9]
- **Texas ELI renters severely cost-burdened (≥50% income on rent):** 79% [9]
- Nationally, there are only **34 affordable and available rental homes per 100 ELI renter households** — in Texas, that drops to **25 per 100** [9]

**Foundation Lens:** Faith-based investors and Christian foundation donors can point to measurable outcomes: San Antonio's homeless response system demonstrably works (unsheltered down 25% in 5 years). Strategic capital deployed here — prevention, rapid rehousing, affordable unit production — will show visible, reportable results. The gospel imperative to "clothe the naked, shelter the stranger" (Matthew 25:35–36) finds direct expression in housing investment.

## 6. Eviction & Displacement [5][6]

Bexar County is experiencing an eviction crisis that is a primary driver of homelessness and housing instability.

### 2024 Eviction Statistics [5][6]

- **27,012 eviction cases filed** against Bexar County households in 2024 [5][6]
  - Equivalent to **~9 in every 100 renter households** [5]
  - ↑ 2,500 more filings than 2023 [6]
- **55%** of filings resulted in a tenant being vacated (judgment for landlord) [6]
- **12%** of all 2024 filings came from just **30 properties** [5][6]
- Highest-evicting property (Forest Oaks): filed eviction against **128 of 166 units** — more than half its tenants [6]
- Highest concentration: Northwest Side / Leon Valley area near Loop 410 [5][6]
- San Antonio City Council District 8: **15% of all city-limit evictions** [6]
- San Antonio Council District 5 (West Side): lowest filings at **1,363** [6]

### Eviction Trends [6]

| Year | Bexar County Eviction Filings |
|---|---|
| ~2013 | ~13,000/year (plateau) |
| 2020 | 8,445 (COVID moratorium) |
| 2022 | 13,722 (moratorium ended) |
| 2023 | 24,533 |
| 2024 | **27,012** |

Texas law allows eviction filing **3 days after rent is due**. Once a judgment is secured, renters have **5 days to appeal and 24 hours to vacate**. [6]

**Investor Lens:** Eviction filings are concentrated in a small number of chronic evictors — meaning targeted intervention (negotiating with large landlords, acquiring high-eviction-rate properties, funding tenant legal defense) could move the needle disproportionately. CDFI capital can also be deployed as eviction prevention grants and rental arrears assistance to stop displacement before it starts.

## 7. Housing Affordability Crisis — Scale of Need [7][8][13]

These figures define the investment opportunity:

- **95,000 Bexar County households** lack access to affordable housing options [7]
- **65,920 people** on affordable housing waitlists (Opportunity Home San Antonio) [13]
  - Waitlist before cleanup: 116,144 — reflects severe under-supply [13]
  - Public housing waitlist: 48,101 | Housing Choice Vouchers: 17,124 [13]
- Housing authority acceptance rate (applicants who accept offers): **~4%** [13]
- Affordable housing bond (2022): **$150 million** — last year of allocation [13]
- Units produced/preserved under bond program: **4,600+** affordable homes [7]
- City's 10-year affordability plan target: **95,000 cost-burdened households** [13]

**The Gap in Units:** [8]
- Demand: 1,018,904 units needed
- Supply: 903,779 units
- **Gap: 115,125 units** — this is the structural deficit that makes San Antonio a high-need, high-opportunity market for housing developers.

## 9. CDFI Intervention Opportunities for Spectra Holdings

Based on the data above, San Antonio/Bexar County presents distinct product-market fit for the Master Credit Facility:

**1. Missing Middle Development (Highest Priority)**
- The 115,125-unit housing gap is concentrated in the missing middle — duplexes, townhomes, small multi-family (2–20 units) [10]
- These projects lack economies of scale and face financing gaps — ideal for CDFI gap financing and predevelopment assistance
- Target product: 2-to-20 unit workforce housing on infill lots in and around the urban core

**2. Preservation of Naturally Occurring Affordable Housing (NOAH)**
- Aging affordable housing stock is deteriorating — preservation capital prevents displacement [7]
- Acquisition-rehab of market-rate properties at risk of rent increases or condo conversion

**3. Anti-Eviction Intervention**
- 30 properties drive 12% of all eviction filings — concentrated, identifiable, addressable
- Prevention grants, rental arrears assistance, and tenant legal defense funding can stop displacement at low cost per household

**4. Rapid Rehousing & Prevention**
- San Antonio's homeless system has proven throughput — 4,548 people moved from street → shelter → housing in FY2024 [2]
- Prevention investment ($8.3M rental assistance prevented evictions for 2,758 households in one year) [2] — ROI is measurable and high

**5. Faith-Based Partnership Leverage**
- San Antonio's large Catholic and Protestant community has deep roots in community development (Catholic Charities, local congregations)
- CDFI capital can be layered with faith-based grants for deep affordability covenants
