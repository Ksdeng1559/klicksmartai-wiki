## 1. MARKET OVERVIEW

Kansas City enters 2026 as one of the strongest-positioned second-tier commercial real estate markets in the Midwest — a cost-competitive alternative to tier-one metros with an intermodal logistics advantage, a $3.7B annual investment market, and a once-in-a-generation development wave anchored by the 2026 FIFA World Cup and a $3B downtown ballpark district. The market is "balanced and opportunity-rich" per Colliers' 2026 Forecast: industrial and multifamily lead; office is healing via flight-to-quality and conversion; retail is outperforming national benchmarks.

| 2026 projected leasing | 3.5–3.7M SF | Second half recovery expected |
| Speculative pipeline | Near zero | Zero multi-tenant space under construction (1Q26) |

**Key dynamics:**
- **Flight to quality is real** — Class A leasing strong (Fidelity, Conexon upsizing downtown); large multitenant Class B faces downsize pressure
- **Conversions are absorbing obsolete stock** — 492,690 SF being converted to multifamily at Centennial and Poindexter buildings downtown
- **Debt maturity wave 2026–2027** — well-located assets expected to trade at opportunistic pricing; prime buildings with amenities win
- **Development requires 30–50% preleasing** for 100K+ SF projects — discipline built into the market
- Lockton's 458,210-SF new HQ under construction signals owner-occupier confidence

**MCF angle:** Office-to-residential conversion is already an active downtown dynamic — a direct precedent for adaptive-reuse workforce housing and a source of distressed commercial assets.

## 8. STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS FOR SPECTRA HOLDINGS

**1. The development wave de-risks the housing thesis.** $3B ballpark district + $1B riverfront + $527M West Bottoms will pull construction and hospitality workers into the urban core for a decade. The workforce-housing demand that supports the MCF vacancy-conversion thesis is being *manufactured* by public-private development.

**2. Office-to-residential conversion is an active, proven market dynamic.** Downtown's 492,690 SF of active conversion (Centennial, Poindexter) is a direct precedent — and a pipeline for acquisition of obsolete Class B/C office assets for affordable conversion, a natural extension of the MCF's adaptive-reuse muscle.

**3. The east side is the counter-cyclical play.** While $3B flows to Crown Center and the riverfront, the east-side 18,000-unit vacant stock remains the cheapest basis in the metro. The MCF can be the vehicle that ensures the World Cup-era boom doesn't simply displace — CLT-deed-restricted ownership captures the upcycle for the existing community.

**4. Commercial-anchored mixed-use economics are favorable.** Retail at 95.4% occupancy and $14.92/SF supports ground-floor retail in east-side conversions; industrial strength ($6.23/SF, 4.9% vacancy) supports the logistics-adjacent workforce.

**5. Public financing precedent is set.** Up to $600M city TIF for the ballpark district + Show Me Sports Investment Act + existing Housing Trust Fund demonstrate Kansas City's willingness to braid public capital at scale — a template for MCF co-investment.
