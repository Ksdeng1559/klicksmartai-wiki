### B.4 The $750K–$1.25M Band — Segment Read

**MoJo Luxury brackets:** $800K–$1.2M = entry luxury; $1.2M–$2M = mid-luxury. The requested band spans entry-luxury into early mid-luxury.

**Behavior in the band:**
- **Correctly priced, turnkey executive homes: 30–45 days DOM.** The $750K–$999K tier averages 32 days with 28% cash.
- **Aspirational pricing fails hard** — overpriced luxury listings "do not sell slowly. They often do not sell at all until the price is corrected" (Magnolia KC). Listings age in front of the exact audience they need to impress.
- **Buyer pool is small and well-advised** — at any moment, only a few dozen qualified buyers shop a specific luxury niche; most work with experienced agents; many are relocating executives on timelines (corporate relocation packages common).
- **Cash share rises with price** — 28% at $750K–$999K, 35% at $1M–$1.49M. Reduces financing-contingency risk and shortens effective sales cycles.
- **Rate-insensitive** — luxury buyers are driven by lifestyle, relocation, and long-term wealth positioning, not mortgage rates.

**Where the band lives (top neighborhoods):** Mission Hills, Leawood, Hallbrook, Overland Park (south), Loch Lloyd, Prairie Village, Parkville (emerging — river proximity, larger lots).

**What's NOT moving:** Above $700K (outside the luxury surge pockets), "inventory is building and taking longer to move" (KC Home Listings, Mar 2026). Condition, setting, new-construction alternatives, and resale risk are the deciding factors.

**Bottom line (executive homes):** The $750K–$1.25M band has **solid, growing absorption** (+9–14% sales growth, 32–45 day DOM for prepared homes, rising cash share) — but it is a **selective market that punishes weak presentation and stale pricing**. Scarcity-premium neighborhoods (Mission Hills, Leawood, Hallbrook) outperform; generic inventory above $700K accumulates.

## PART C — STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS FOR THE MCF THESIS

1. **Apartment occupancy validates rental-demand stability** in the east/central submarkets (4.9–5.6% vacancy, tightening) — de-risks the "rental bridge" period of any acquisition-rehab pipeline before CLT sales close.

2. **The executive tier is a separate, complementary product** — KC's +15.6% luxury price growth and +57% $1M+ sales volume demonstrate an active premium market. A developer partner could cross-subsidize workforce product with executive inventory in the same corridor (a structure worth modeling, not assuming).

3. **Absorption discipline applies to the east-side product too** — the luxury lesson (prepared, correctly priced, lifestyle-driven sells; stale inventory rots) transfers directly to CLT resale pricing and buyer-qualification velocity.

4. **Data gaps to close:** (a) apartment occupancy by *building class* (A/B/C) and by *east-side specific* asset (C&W market data is metro-level); (b) $750K–$1.25M absorption *within Jackson County specifically* (KCRAR/Heartland can segment — request from broker); (c) months-of-supply for the $750K–$1.25M band across Mission Hills/Leawood equivalents on the MO side.
