# Jackson County, MO (Kansas City) — Housing Intelligence Report

> **Scope:** Prepared for Veritas Developments (David Poole) — **Prime Lee's Summit** (Hwy 291, Lee's Summit) & **Stonehaven Estates** (Cyclone School Rd, Lee's Summit), Jackson County, MO.

> *"So are farmers who wait for the precious harvest from the land and make it their goal — to receive the blessing from God."* — 2 Timothy 2:6

Jackson County presents a distinct MCF entry thesis from the existing pipeline: not *"bridge an affordability gap"* (Whatcom), not *"targeted intervention in a structural deficit"* (Bexar), but **"convert 18,000 off-market vacant homes into permanently affordable workforce ownership."** The county-level affordability math closes at the median — the real gap is tenure, the real inventory is vacancy, and the partner stack (KCCLT, Central Bank of KC, Abundant Ground, PEDC) is the most complete of any pipeline county. The MCF is the missing capital layer that assembles them.

## 2. The Affordability Math

**County-wide:**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Median home price | **$230,500** (ACS) / **$260–270K** (market 2026) |
| Median household income | **$68,577** (ACS 2024) |
| Affordable mortgage at $68,577 | **~$301,000** (10% down, 30-yr @ 6.5%) |
| Affordability gap | **NONE at county MHI** |

**The renter gap (where the MCF actually works):**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Renter median household income | **$45,021** |
| Affordable mortgage at $45,021 | **~$198,000** |
| Median home price | $230,500–$270,000 |
| **Conversion gap** | **$32,000–$72,000 per household** |
| Median gross rent | $1,197 (ACS) / $1,300–$1,615 (market) |
| Rent burden (GRAPI) | **30.3%** — at cost-burden threshold |

**What this means in practice:**
- A household earning the county median ($68,577) CAN buy the median home — the market is not broken for the median earner
- A household earning the renter median ($45,021) CANNOT — a $32K–$72K subsidy per unit (CLT ground lease + write-down) closes the gap
- 40%+ of households earn below $50K; these are the MCF's addressable population
- East-side acquisition basis ($94K–$150K) means the *capital per converted unit is the lowest in the pipeline*

## 3. Community Based Building Fit

Community Based Building (CBB) has exceptional alignment with Kansas City's institutional and faith landscape:

| CBB Pillar | Jackson County Signal |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Community ownership | KCCLT dual-ownership model — land held in trust forever, subsidy recycled |
| Local partner depth | KCCLT, Central Bank of KC, Habitat KC, PEDC, AG/KC — functioning ecosystem |
| Faith alignment | Bible Belt density (Catholic 19.5%, SBC 18.0%, evangelical 11.5%); AG/KC church-land movement; PEDC 30+ years |
| Political will | Tenants Bill of Rights, Right to Counsel, Housing Trust Fund — city already spent political capital |
| Crisis urgency | 1,978 homeless (+11.4% YoY); 18,000 off-market vacancies; HAKC waitlists closed |
| Anti-displacement fit | CLT deed restriction is purpose-built; complements KCEDP legal defense |

**What the city's policy posture signals to MCF:** Kansas City has already crossed the threshold from discussion to commitment — but on *tenant protection*, not *ownership conversion*. The MCF/KCCLT partnership is the natural next phase: turn defended renters into stabilized owners.

## 4. KCCLT — Primary Partner Profile

| Dimension | Signal |
|-----------|--------|
| Organizational type | Community Land Trust (501c3) |
| Model | Permanently affordable homeownership; land held in trust; ground lease; subsidy recycled across generations of owners |
| Track record | Operational — acquires property like a developer, employs HUD/City/CRA subsidies |
| Geographic scope | Kansas City, MO (east side focus) |
| Faith alignment | Equity/justice mission; works with faith-based partners |
| MCF structuring opportunity | Ground lease = perpetual affordability covenant; CLT holds right of first refusal on resale |

**Complementary partners:**
- **Central Bank of Kansas City** — only CDFI bank HQ'd in KC; NMTC allocations since 2007; LIHTC capacity; 3-state trade area
- **Abundant Ground KC** — faith-based church-land housing initiative (Yes in God's Backyard originator)
- **PEDC** — faith-affiliated CDC/CHDO; 187 senior units built over 30 years
- **Habitat KC** — CLT-model builder in the region (14-home Pathway at Heritage Park)

**Ecosystem gap:** No consolidated CLT+MCF vehicle exists in the metro — the partners operate in silos. The MCF entrant that assembles them captures the integration premium.

| Layer | Source | Role |

**Geographic priority:** East-side Council Districts 3/4/5 first (18,000-unit stock); Midtown transitional zips second

## 7. Risk Matrix

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|------------|--------|------------|
| MO state preemption of tenant protections | Medium | High | Covenant enforcement via contract law + CLT deed restriction (survives policy change) |
| Rehab cost overruns (100-yr-old stock) | Medium-High | Medium | Fixed-price contracts, 15% contingency, per-unit ceiling at 85% of neighborhood median |
| Gentrification displacement | Medium | High | CLT deed restriction + tenant right-of-return + KCEDP partnership |
| Appraisal gap on distressed acquisitions | Medium | Medium | Two-appraisal review, land-bank sourcing, acquisition ceiling |
| No county-acknowledged deficit figure | High | Low | Use HAKC + PIT + vacancy as demand proxies; flag in materials |
| CDFI/grant allocation timing | Medium | Medium | Diversified stack; Central Bank NMTC/LIHTC capacity |
| East-side developer capacity | Medium | Medium | Co-developer agreement with PEDC or Habitat KC |
