**Section 1: Direct Answer (snippet bait, ~80 words)**

> **Bridge financing in Canada** is a short-term commercial loan (typically 6-24 months, interest-only) that bridges the gap between an immediate capital need and a longer-term solution. Most commercial bridge loans in Canada fund in 5-10 business days, with amounts from $200,000 to $5 million and up to 75% loan-to-value. Use cases include time-sensitive acquisitions, construction takeout, refinancing maturing loans, and equity extraction.

---

**Section 2: What Is Bridge Financing? (300-400 words, H2)**

Explain the concept:
- Short-term mortgage (6-24 months, IO)
- Secured against commercial or investment real estate
- Underwritten on the property + exit strategy (not bank committees)
- Why it's faster than conventional: asset-based, fewer conditions
- Cost reality: bridge rates are 8-14% (vs 5-7% conventional) — premium for speed

**Section 3: When to Use Bridge Financing (400-500 words, H2)**

3. **Maturing loan / renewal failure** — bank said no, need new capital
4. **Equity extraction** — pull cash from one property to fund another
5. **Partner buyout / business event** — quick capital for time-bound transaction

**Section 4: How Bridge Financing Works in Canada (400-500 words, H2)**

1. Submit deal (typically 1-page deal summary + property info)

---

**Section 1: Direct Answer (~80 words)**

> **Invoice factoring in Canada** lets businesses sell unpaid invoices to a factoring company at a discount in exchange for immediate cash — typically 80-90% of the invoice value within 24-48 hours, with the remaining balance (minus fees) paid when the customer pays. Best for B2B businesses with slow-paying clients (logistics, manufacturing, staffing, services). Loan amounts scale with your receivables, not your credit score.

---

**Section 2: What Is Invoice Factoring? (400 words, H2)**
- Sell unpaid invoices to a third party (the factor)
- Factor pays 80-90% upfront, holds 10-20% in reserve
- Customer pays the factor directly
- Factor releases reserve minus fees (typically 1-5% per 30 days)
- Recourse vs non-recourse explained

**Section 3: When Factoring Makes Sense (300-400 words, H2)**
- Slow-paying B2B clients (30-90 days outstanding)
- Need working capital for payroll, inventory, growth
- Don't have property to pledge for traditional loan
- New business without 2+ years tax returns
- Seasonal businesses with revenue spikes

**Section 4: Factoring Costs in Canada (400 words, H2)**
- Factor rate (advance rate): 80-90%
- Discount fee: 1-5% per 30 days (effective APR can be 20-60%+)
- Reserve held: 10-20%
- Example: $100K invoice, 90% advance, 3% fee, 30-day collection
  - Get $90K today
  - Customer pays $100K in 30 days
  - Factor keeps $3K fee + $7K reserve = $90K returned to you
  - Total cost: $3K for 30-day use of $90K = effective APR ~12% (lower than typical)

> A **business line of credit (LOC) in Canada** is revolving working capital that businesses draw as needed, paying interest only on the amount used. Typical limits range from $50,000 to $5 million, with variable rates (Prime + 0.5% to Prime + 6%). Banks require 2+ years in business + strong revenue + collateral; private lenders like Triple Queens and Glasslake offer LOCs with more flexible underwriting for self-employed, startup, or bank-rejected borrowers.

---

**Section 1: Direct Answer (~100 words)**

> A **commercial mortgage broker in Canada** connects borrowers with lenders for commercial mortgages — packaging your file, matching lenders to your deal, and negotiating terms. For owner-occupied commercial mortgages (where the business operates from the property), brokers typically quote 50+ lenders and earn 1.5-2% commission on funded loans (minimum $5,000). Working with a broker is free for the borrower and gives you access to lenders that don't accept direct applications — like Glasslake Funding, which offers $200K-$5M commercial mortgages with alternative underwriting for self-employed and bank-rejected borrowers.

---

**Section 2: What Does a Commercial Mortgage Broker Do? (400 words, H2)**
- Packages the file (DSCR, NOI, T12, rent roll, appraisal)
- Matches lenders to deal type (owner-occupied vs investor, amount, property type)
- Negotiates term sheets (rate, amortization, prepayment penalty)
- Coordinates underwriting through closing
- Free for borrower (paid by lender)

**Section 3: Owner-Occupied vs Investment Commercial Mortgages (400 words, H2)**
- Owner-occupied: business operates from property, DSCR may be < 1.0 (lender weights personal income)
- Investment: rented to third parties, DSCR must be > 1.2 (rent covers debt service)
- Different lenders specialize in each
- Owner-occupied is the lane Glasslake focuses on

**Section 4: How Much Does a Commercial Mortgage Cost? (500 words, H2)**

Typical commercial mortgage components:
- Rate (5-8% conventional, 7-12% alt lender)
- Amortization (15-25 years)
- Term (1-7 years)
- Prepayment penalty (often 3 months interest or Interest Rate Differential)
- Lender fee (0.5-1% of loan)
- Broker fee (1-2% of loan, paid by lender)
- Appraisal ($2-5K)
- Legal ($2-5K)
- Environmental Phase 1 ($2-3K, sometimes required)
