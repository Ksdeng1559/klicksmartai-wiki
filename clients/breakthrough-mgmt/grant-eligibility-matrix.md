---

## 1. How to Use This Matrix

This matrix is designed to help Breakthrough Management and its partners screen grant opportunities before committing time and resources to a full application.

Each opportunity should be reviewed against:

1. Applicant eligibility
2. Program fit
3. Technology fit
4. Required partners
5. Evidence requirements
6. Matching fund requirements
7. Commercialization potential
8. Community benefit alignment
9. Probability of award
10. Go / no-go recommendation

The goal is to avoid chasing generic grants and instead identify fundable work packages that match Breakthrough's strongest project themes:

- Methane reduction
- Anaerobic digestion
- Landfill gas management
- AI-enabled MRV
- Urban mining
- E-waste recovery
- Critical materials recovery
- Circular economy infrastructure
- County / Tribal / rural infrastructure
- Carbon asset generation
- Economic development

---

## 2. Core Grant Positioning

Breakthrough should not lead with a generic carbon credit narrative.

The preferred funding narrative is:

> Breakthrough Management is developing a circular carbon infrastructure and intelligence platform that helps counties, Tribal communities, rural regions, and industrial corridors convert waste liabilities into verified environmental assets through methane reduction, anaerobic digestion, urban mining, e-waste recovery, materials recovery, AI-enabled MRV, and carbon asset generation.

## 4. Priority Funding Pathways

### Pathway 1 — AI-MRV First

**Best for:** SBIR / STTR / climate tech funding

**Why it works:**

The AI-MRV platform is lower capital intensity than heavy infrastructure and can support multiple project types. It can be positioned as the technical innovation layer that enables methane reduction, circular economy, waste diversion, and carbon asset generation.

**Recommended first programs:**

- EPA SBIR
- DOE SBIR
- NSF SBIR
- Climate tech accelerators
- Carbon market innovation programs

---

### Pathway 2 — Methane Reduction First

**Best for:** EPA, DOE, state climate, county, and methane reduction programs

**Why it works:**

Methane reduction is a clear climate priority and easier for reviewers to understand than a broad carbon-credit strategy. Anaerobic digestion, landfill gas management, and organics diversion can be framed as measurable emissions reduction projects.

**Recommended first programs:**

- EPA methane/climate programs
- DOE methane-related opportunities
- State climate infrastructure grants
- County solid waste programs
- USDA rural energy programs if rural/agricultural partner exists

---

### Pathway 3 — Urban Mining / Critical Materials First

**Best for:** DOE critical minerals, Department of Commerce, EDA, circular economy programs

**Why it works:**

E-waste, server decommissioning, MSR, and recovered metals connect the project to domestic supply-chain resilience, circular manufacturing, and critical materials recovery.

**Recommended first programs:**

- DOE Critical Minerals and Materials
- Department of Commerce manufacturing / supply-chain programs
- EDA economic development programs
- State circular economy programs
- Corporate ITAD / circular procurement partnerships

---

### Pathway 4 — County Anchor Center First

**Best for:** EDA, county, state infrastructure, public-private partnership, port/economic development programs

**Why it works:**

A county anchor center creates a concrete place-based infrastructure project with job creation, waste diversion, climate benefit, and economic development logic.

**Recommended first programs:**

- EDA Public Works
- EDA Economic Adjustment Assistance
- State infrastructure funds
- County grants
- Port authority partnerships
- Public-private partnership capital

## 5. Go / No-Go Rules

### Strong GO

Proceed when the opportunity has:

- Clear applicant eligibility
- Direct alignment with one work package
- Identified site or feedstock
- Required partner available or reachable
- Reasonable evidence requirements
- Commercialization or public benefit story
- Deadline allows quality submission

### Conditional GO

Proceed only if one missing requirement can be resolved quickly, such as:

- Letter of support
- Partner MOU
- Feedstock estimate
- Baseline calculation
- Entity eligibility confirmation
- Budget refinement

### Watch

Track but do not apply yet when:

- Eligibility is unclear
- Technology fit is weak
- Deadline is too close
- Required partner is missing
- Match funding is unavailable
- Program is too broad or political

### No-Go

Do not pursue when:

- Breakthrough cannot be an eligible applicant or subrecipient
- The project does not match the funding statute or NOFO
- The technology is not ready enough
- Required public-sector partner is unavailable
- The grant would force the project away from the core strategy
- Administrative burden exceeds expected value

## 6. Required Evidence Checklist

### Applicant Evidence

- U.S. entity status
- Small business status if SBIR/STTR
- Ownership/control structure
- SAM.gov registration
- UEI number
- Financial statements where required
- Prior grant experience or partner qualifications

### Technical Evidence

- Technology description
- Technical novelty statement
- TRL assessment
- Engineering assumptions
- Methane/feedstock baseline
- MRV architecture
- Data model
- Carbon methodology screening

### Partner Evidence

- County letter of support
- Recycler / waste operator letter
- Tribal partner letter where relevant
- University or lab validation partner
- Environmental engineering partner
- Corporate buyer interest
- Site-control or access documentation

### Environmental Outcome Evidence

- Estimated methane reduction
- Estimated waste diversion
- Estimated recovered material volume
- Estimated avoided emissions
- Baseline scenario
- Measurement plan
- Verification pathway

### Commercialization Evidence

- Revenue model
- Buyer categories
- Market size
- Competitive advantage
- Replication strategy
- Go-to-market path
- Five-year scaling plan

### Community Benefit Evidence

- Job creation
- Workforce development
- Local economic development
- Tribal/community benefit
- Environmental justice alignment
- Waste reduction benefit
- Climate resilience benefit

## 7. Recommended First 90-Day Grant Preparation Plan

### Days 1–30 — Eligibility and Evidence Foundation

- Confirm Breakthrough's applicant eligibility.
- Confirm whether a U.S. small business entity exists or is required.
- Confirm SAM.gov / UEI status.
- Select the first grant pathway: AI-MRV, methane, urban mining, or anchor center.
- Create a partner target list.
- Build the first feedstock evidence table.

### Days 31–60 — Partner and Technical Package

- Obtain first partner conversations.
- Draft letters of support.
- Create technical concept paper.
- Create preliminary budget range.
- Build TRL and commercialization summary.
- Map 3–5 grant opportunities against the matrix.

### Days 61–90 — Application-Ready Package

- Finalize go/no-go list.
- Prepare first LOI or concept paper.
- Confirm grant calendar.
- Build reusable boilerplate sections.
- Prepare partner MOU template.
- Create a grant evidence folder.

## 10. Summary Recommendation

Breakthrough should prioritize grants that fund evidence creation, technical validation, MRV infrastructure, and pilot design before pursuing large infrastructure grants.

The strongest sequence is:

```text
1. SBIR / technical validation
2. Methane or urban mining pilot
3. County anchor center feasibility
4. EDA / state infrastructure funding
5. Corporate offtake and private project finance
```

This creates a realistic path from idea to funded pilot to infrastructure-scale deployment.
