## Executive Summary

Three signals converge on Whatcom County / Ferndale as a viable anchor center for an environmental asset development initiative:

1. **Washington State closed its last active landfill to new waste in 2025.** Cedarville and Birch Bay are capped and post-closure — methane currently vented or flared, not monetized. A gas collection system exists but is legacy; ACM0001 (Verra) is live for LFG-to-energy or LFG-to-combustion projects through November 2026 before the new VMR0016 revision activates.

2. **ABC Recycling is operational at 741 Marine Drive, Ferndale** — a permitted metal shredding facility processing post-consumer depolluted automobiles and appliances. Its inbound feedstock is identical to what urban mining operations need: depolluted, shredded ferrous/non-ferrous material. The MSR (metal shredder residue) outflow goes to local landfills as alternative daily cover — a bridging point to circular-economy infrastructure.

3. **Microsoft and Google are actively sourcing carbon removal credits and have explicit e-waste/circular-economy procurement pipelines.** Microsoft's FY24 server reuse/recycle rate hit 90.9%. Google's data center hardware management program saves over $1B/year in TCO. Both are undersupplied relative to their net-zero commitments — a buyer gap exists.

**The strategic question is not whether feedstock or buyers exist — they do. The question is whether a county-level anchor center can be sited,permitted, and structured to serve as the aggregation and MRV layer between feedstock generators and carbon/e-waste buyers.**

**Bottom line:** Ferndale scores higher than Bellingham for anchor center deployment. Industrial-zoned land, Port of Bellingham proximity, existing ABC Recycling feedstock adjacency, and FHLB/grant eligibility for a clean-industrial development are all present.

## Section A — Whatcom County Waste Infrastructure

### A1. Cedarville Landfill (CLOSED — Post-Closure)
- **Location:** 3463 Cedarville Rd, Bellingham WA 98226 (~1,700 ft west of Hwy 542 / Cedarville Rd intersection)
- **Owner/Operator:** Whatcom County Health & Community Services (regulatory owner); leased to Sanitary Service Company (SSC) for drop-box/recycling operation
- **Status:** Closed September 1993; active post-closure monitoring ongoing
- **Throughput (historical):** Accepted MSW from 1980–1993; no active waste receipt
- **Current system:** Final engineered geomembrane cover, LFG collection + flare system installed at closure; groundwater barrier wall constructed May 2021 (sheet pile cutoff, ~220 ft along western boundary); NPDES permit WA0501490 for leachate bioswale discharge
- **Monitoring cadence:** Groundwater sampling every 5 years (next: 2027 per 5-year cycle from 2022)
- **Methane data:** Glaciomarine drift clay underlay; low-permeability substrate; perched and lower aquifer monitoring wells; no current LFG-to-energy monetization
- **Expansion opportunity:** ACM0001 applicable — LFG capture for destruction or energy. Washington State WAC 173-408 (Landfill Methane Emissions, adopted May 13, 2024, effective June 13, 2024) imposes methane concentration limits (5 ppm instantaneous / 2.5 ppm integrated average) at surface — creates regulatory urgency for improved capture. Existing flare system may be upgradeable to energy recovery or to a higher-efficiency destruction device under Verra ACM0001.
- **Feedstock value estimate:** ~0.5–1.5 MW of landfill gas generation potential (requires gas flow rate modeling from EPA LFG calculation tool). Conservative estimate: 1,500–2,500 tCO2e/year if capture efficiency improved from current to 75%+ under ACM0001.

### A2. Birch Bay Landfill (CLOSED)
- **Location:** 4297 Birch Bay-Lynden Rd, Blaine WA 98230
- **Owner/Operator:** Whatcom County; operated under same SSC lease as Cedarville
- **Status:** Closed; now a drop-box/recycling site for citizens (open Sundays)
- **Current system:** No active LFG collection system described in permit records
- **Expansion opportunity:** Smaller footprint than Cedarville; likely lower gas generation. Birch Bay's tourist seasonal population creates variable waste volume — less reliable for LFG baseline. Not a primary candidate for gas monetization without a dedicated gas study.

### A3. Sanitary Service Company (SSC) Drop-Box / Transfer Station Network
- **Primary facility:** 1001 Roeder Ave, Bellingham (M–F 8am–5pm)
- **Other sites:** Cedarville (Sat), Birch Bay (Sun), Nooksack Valley Disposal (Lynden), Republic Services (Ferndale), Recycling & Disposal Services (Ferndale)
- **Owner/Operator:** SSC — largest franchised hauler in Whatcom County
- **Feedstock adjacency:** SSC's Cedarville site accepts household garbage + standard recyclables; it is the entry point for the county's self-haul waste stream. Any urban mining or circular-economy hub co-located at or adjacent to SSC's network would have first access to incoming recoverable material.
- **Limitation:** No dedicated e-waste stream — "household garbage" and standard recyclables only at most sites. Disposal of Toxics facility (Whatcom County) handles hazardous waste; e-waste via retailer take-back and periodic events.

### A4. ABC Recycling — 741 Marine Drive, Ferndale
- **Location:** 741 Marine Drive, Ferndale WA (Port-adjacent Heavy Impact Industrial zoned)
- **Owner/Operator:** ABC Recycling LLC (permitted 2023–2024, construction phase)
- **Throughput:** Designed for industrial-scale metal shredding; inbound material = post-consumer depolluted automobiles + kitchen appliances (all fluids removed). Output = ferrous metal, non-ferrous metal, MSR (metal shredder residue). 75–80% of inbound becomes saleable metal; 20–25% becomes MSR.
- **MSR disposition:** Transported to local landfills (Cedarville, others) for use as alternative daily cover. This is the bridging point: MSR as ADC is a transitional use; the long-term circular play is MSR as feedstock for urban mining recovery (glass, rubber, fabric, plastics, metals).
- **Significance for anchor center:** ABC is the closest existing metal processing infrastructure in Whatcom County. Its MSR stream represents a material feedstock opportunity for secondary recovery. Potential for co-development: MSR preprocessing hub adjacent to ABC, or an offtake agreement for MSR as circular-economy feedstock.
- **Expansion opportunity:** Zoning is Heavy Impact Industrial — permitted use per WCC 20.68.054(6). Additional industrial sites in Ferndale (near Slater Road corridor / Republic Services) could support an anchor center.

### A5. RE Sources / Disposal of Toxics
- **Disposal of Toxics facility:** Whatcom County; processes hazardous waste, chemicals, paint stewardship (Paint Care program collected 122,840 lbs in 2024). 11,690 residential/commercial customers, 634,996 lbs total in 2024.
- **RE Sources Circular Economy Accelerator:** Awarded $92,059 (2024) for manufacturing waste diversion program — targets Whatcom manufacturing sector for commercial recycling infrastructure. This is an existing funded program already operating in the county — a potential partnership or co-location opportunity for a circular economy hub.
- **Feedstock relevance:** RE Sources is actively building commercial recycling capacity with grant funding. An anchor center aligned with RE Sources' infrastructure goals could leverage the same grant pipeline.

### B2. Methane Feedstock

| Source | Location | Est. Generation | Monetizable? | Complexity |
|--------|----------|-----------------|--------------|------------|
| Cedarville Landfill LFG | Everson | Low-Medium (no current energy recovery) | Yes — ACM0001 | Medium (requires gas study + GCCS upgrade) |
| Agricultural digesters | Whatcom County | Low (dairy/crop; Nooksack Valley has some farmland) | Potentially — AMS-III.BM or Gold Standard | High (requires digester infrastructure) |
| Food/organics waste | Whatcom County | Medium (SSC + independent haulers) | Potentially — Gold Standard decentralized organic methodology (2024) | Medium |
| Wastewater treatment | Whatcom WWTP, Lynden, Everson | Low-Medium | Limited — requires dedicated RNG infrastructure | High |

**Cedarville LFG is the primary near-term methane opportunity.** Under WAC 173-408 (effective June 13, 2024), all MSW landfills that received waste after January 1, 1992 and meet HIC thresholds must install GCCS. Cedarville's HIC should be calculated using the EPA protocol — if it exceeds 450,000 tons waste-in-place for active / 750,000 tons for closed landfill threshold, it falls under mandatory GCCS requirements. A project that upgrades the existing flare system to an ACM0001-eligible energy recovery or high-efficiency destruction device could simultaneously satisfy WAC 173-408 compliance and generate VCUs.

**Carbon pricing context:** Methane avoidance credits under Verra ACM0001 typically price at $8–$20/tCO2e in current markets (Q1–Q2 2026), with CCP-labeled credits commanding premium. LFG-to-electricity projects ≤10 MWe are CCP-eligible under ACM0001 v15–v19.

## Section D — Carbon Methodology

### Verra VCS — ACM0001 (Flaring or Use of Landfill Gas)
- **Status:** Active until November 30, 2026; replaced by VMR0016 (new methodology revision) from December 1, 2026
- **Scope:** Destruction of methane via flaring or energy recovery; displacement of more GHG-intensive service
- **Additionality:** Performance standard or three-prong test; regulatory surplus test required
- **CCP eligibility:** Yes — CCP-labeled if LFG-to-electricity projects ≤10 MWe or non-electric projects; additional criteria per ICVCM decision
- **MRV:** Methane concentration continuous monitoring (15-min intervals); calibrated collection efficiency using EPA LFG model; methane destruction efficiency tested for flares/energy devices
- **Pricing:** $8–$20/tCO2e (CCP-labeled premium); $5–$12/tCO2e (non-labeled)
- **Complexity:** Medium — requires gas flow study, GCCS engineering, baseline modeling, annual verification
- **Commercial attractiveness:** **HIGH** for Cedarville-type closed landfill with existing infrastructure gap

### Verra VCS — VMR0008 / AMS-III.BA. (E-waste Recovery & Recycling)
- **Status:** Active since September 4, 2023
- **Scope:** Collection + recycling of e-waste at dedicated facilities; recovery of ferrous metals, non-ferrous metals, plastics; displacement of virgin material production → energy savings → GHG reductions
- **Additionality:** Upstream displacement discount factor applied (addresses uncertainty in virgin material displacement)
- **CCP eligibility:** Yes — Sectoral Scope 13
- **MRV:** Material balance (tonnes of e-waste processed); metal recovery rates; displacement factors for virgin production
- **Pricing:** $5–$15/tCO2e (varies by metal prices and recovery efficiency)
- **Complexity:** Medium — requires inbound/outbound material tracking, metal assay documentation, third-party verification
- **Commercial attractiveness:** **MEDIUM-HIGH** for urban mining hub with pre-processing infrastructure. High when co-located with metal shredding operations (ABC Recycling MSR path)

### Puro Earth — LFG-to-Energy / Methane Avoidance
- **Status:** Active; Nordic markets leader; expanding to North American projects
- **Scope:** Similar to ACM0001; focus on energy recovery from methane sources
- **Additionality:** Performance standard + financial additionality test
- **CCP eligibility:** Puro's registry operates independently; alignment with ICVCM CCP framework in progress
- **Pricing:** $10–$25/tCO2e (premium for Nordic/CDM legacy credits)
- **Complexity:** Medium-High
- **Commercial attractiveness:** **MEDIUM** — fewer North American projects; more relevant for European/Nordic project developers
