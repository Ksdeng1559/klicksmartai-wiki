## Executive Summary

Three signals converge on Whatcom County / Ferndale as a viable anchor center:

1. **Washington State closed its last active landfill to new waste in 2025.** Cedarville is capped and post-closure — methane currently vented or flared, not monetized. WAC 173-408 (effective June 13, 2024) creates regulatory urgency for improved capture. ACM0001 (Verra) is live through November 30, 2026 before VMR0016 activates.

2. **ABC Recycling is operational at 741 Marine Drive, Ferndale** — permitted metal shredding processing depolluted automobiles and appliances. Its MSR (metal shredder residue) outflow goes to landfill as alternative daily cover — a bridging point to circular-economy infrastructure. The facility is the closest existing metal processing infrastructure in Whatcom County.

3. **Microsoft and Google are actively sourcing carbon removal credits and have explicit e-waste/circular-economy procurement pipelines.** Microsoft's FY24 server reuse/recycle rate hit 90.9%. Google's data center hardware management saves over $1B/year in TCO. Both are undersupplied relative to their net-zero commitments — a buyer gap exists.

**The strategic question is not whether feedstock or buyers exist — they do. The question is whether a county-level anchor center can be sited, permitted, and structured as the aggregation and MRV layer between feedstock generators and carbon/e-waste buyers.**

---

**Scale layer — from Cascadia research:**

The Grand Boulevard strategy (42K acres of underutilized arterials, 1.4M–4.6M unit capacity) and the Whatcom MPC ($50B, 25–30 years, 20K+ residents, 2K-acre industrial park) transform the opportunity:

- At 50–80 tons of C&D waste per unit, Cascadia's housing build-out generates **25–370M tonnes of feedstock** over the build cycle — mainstream infrastructure scale, not a niche play
- The Whatcom MPC is the **capital vehicle**: a P3 developer consortium that could absorb Breakthrough as the circular economy operator inside the MPC's industrial zone component
- Cascadia Rail integration (in the MPC pitch) turns Ferndale into a **multimodal freight corridor** — processed materials north to BC industrial buyers, carbon credits south to Seattle hyperscalers
- Washington exports more to BC than to all other Canadian provinces combined — BC cross-border trade is the latent buyer for secondary metals

**Bottom line:** Ferndale is the anchor. ABC MSR + Cedarville LFG are the two near-term feedstocks. The Cascadia scale layer (Grand Boulevard C&D + MPC capital) expands the opportunity from a county-level niche play to a Cascadia-scale infrastructure operation. Breakthrough's job is to position as the **circular economy infrastructure operator inside the MPC framework**, with carbon credits and secondary metals as the two revenue tracks.

## Section A — Whatcom County Waste Infrastructure

### A1. Cedarville Landfill (CLOSED — Post-Closure)
- **Location:** 3463 Cedarville Rd, Bellingham WA 98226 (~1,700 ft west of Hwy 542 / Cedarville Rd intersection)
- **Owner/Operator:** Whatcom County Health & Community Services (regulatory owner); leased to Sanitary Service Company (SSC) for drop-box/recycling operation
- **Status:** Closed September 1993; active post-closure monitoring ongoing
- **Current system:** Final engineered geomembrane cover, LFG collection + flare system installed at closure; groundwater barrier wall constructed May 2021 (sheet pile cutoff, ~220 ft along western boundary); NPDES permit WA0501490
- **Monitoring cadence:** Groundwater sampling every 5 years (next: 2027 per 5-year cycle from 2022)
- **Methane status:** Glaciomarine drift clay underlay; low-permeability substrate; no current LFG-to-energy monetization
- **WAC 173-408 relevance:** Imposes methane concentration limits (5 ppm instantaneous / 2.5 ppm integrated average) at surface — creates mandatory GCCS upgrade urgency. Existing flare may be upgradeable to energy recovery or higher-efficiency destruction under Verra ACM0001.
- **Feedstock value estimate:** ~0.5–1.5 MW landfill gas generation potential (requires EPA LFG calc tool modeling). Conservative: 1,500–2,500 tCO2e/yr at 75%+ capture efficiency.
- **MPC alignment:** Methane compliance + carbon credit dual-benefit positions Breakthrough as the MPC's environmental infrastructure operator for the county's post-closure landfill obligations.

### A2. Birch Bay Landfill (CLOSED)
- **Location:** 4297 Birch Bay-Lynden Rd, Blaine WA 98230
- **Status:** Closed; drop-box/recycling site open Sundays; no active LFG collection system
- **Assessment:** Smaller footprint; seasonal tourist waste volume makes gas generation less reliable. Not a primary monetization candidate without a dedicated gas study.

### A3. Sanitary Service Company (SSC) Drop-Box / Transfer Station Network
- **Primary facility:** 1001 Roeder Ave, Bellingham (M–F 8am–5pm)
- **Other sites:** Cedarville (Sat), Birch Bay (Sun), Nooksack Valley Disposal (Lynden), Republic Services (Ferndale), Recycling & Disposal Services (Ferndale)
- **Owner/Operator:** SSC — largest franchised hauler in Whatcom County
- **Feedstock adjacency:** Entry point for self-haul waste stream; any co-located circular-economy hub gets first access to incoming recoverable material.
- **Limitation:** No dedicated e-waste stream; Disposal of Toxics handles hazardous waste; e-waste via retailer take-back and periodic events.

### A4. ABC Recycling — 741 Marine Drive, Ferndale
- **Location:** 741 Marine Drive, Ferndale WA (Port-adjacent Heavy Impact Industrial zoned, WCC 20.68.054(6))
- **Owner/Operator:** ABC Recycling LLC (permitted 2023–2024, construction/commissioning phase)
- **Throughput:** Industrial-scale metal shredding; inbound = post-consumer depolluted automobiles + kitchen appliances. Output = ferrous metal (75–80%), non-ferrous metal, MSR (20–25%).
- **MSR disposition:** Transported to local landfills for alternative daily cover — the bridging point to circular-economy infrastructure. Long-term circular play: MSR as feedstock for secondary recovery (glass, rubber, fabric, plastics, metals).
- **Significance for anchor center:** Closest existing metal processing infrastructure in Whatcom County. Potential for co-development: MSR pre-processing hub adjacent to ABC, or offtake agreement for MSR as circular feedstock.
- **Cascadia scale relevance:** Grand Boulevard demolition waste contains massive quantities of ferrous/non-ferrous metal. ABC's shredding infrastructure is directly applicable to C&D metal recovery at scale — no new capital equipment needed.

### A5. RE Sources / Disposal of Toxics
- **Disposal of Toxics facility:** Whatcom County; processes hazardous waste, chemicals, paint stewardship. 11,690 residential/commercial customers, 634,996 lbs total collected in 2024.
- **RE Sources Circular Economy Accelerator:** $92,059 award (2024) for manufacturing waste diversion program targeting Whatcom commercial sector. This is an existing funded program — potential partnership or co-location.
- **MPC alignment:** RE Sources' grant-funded commercial recycling infrastructure aligns with the MPC's zero-waste targets. Breakthrough as MPC operator would interface with RE Sources as the county's existing circular economy NGO partner.

**Key finding — AI server wave:** The primary wave from 2022–2024 AI GPU deployments reaches 3–5 year refresh cycle 2026–2029. This is the largest near-term value opportunity. At current commodity prices (Au ~$2,900/troy oz; Cu ~$9,200/tonne), a ton of e-waste contains ~90g Au, 400g Ag, 200,000g Cu — urban mining concentration is 40–300x richer than virgin ore.

A local pre-processing hub (sorting, depolluting, staging) captures margin before material ships to Aurubis Richmond (Georgia, 180,000 t/a) or Metallium Texas for final processing.

## Section D — Carbon Methodology

### Verra VCS — ACM0001 (Flaring or Use of Landfill Gas)
- **Status:** Active until November 30, 2026; replaced by VMR0016 from December 1, 2026
- **Scope:** Destruction of methane via flaring or energy recovery; displacement of more GHG-intensive service
- **Additionality:** Performance standard or three-prong test; regulatory surplus test required
- **CCP eligibility:** Yes — CCP-labeled if LFG-to-electricity projects ≤10 MWe or non-electric projects
- **MRV:** Methane concentration continuous monitoring (15-min intervals); calibrated collection efficiency using EPA LFG model; methane destruction efficiency tested for flares/energy devices
- **Pricing:** $8–$20/tCO2e (CCP-labeled premium); $5–$12/tCO2e (non-labeled)
- **Complexity:** Medium — requires gas flow study, GCCS engineering, baseline modeling, annual verification
- **Commercial attractiveness:** **HIGH** for Cedarville-type closed landfill with existing infrastructure gap and WAC 173-408 compliance driver

### Verra VCS — VMR0008 / AMS-III.BA. (E-waste Recovery & Recycling)
- **Status:** Active since September 4, 2023
- **Scope:** Collection + recycling of e-waste at dedicated facilities; recovery of ferrous metals, non-ferrous metals, plastics; displacement of virgin material production → energy savings → GHG reductions
- **Additionality:** Upstream displacement discount factor applied
- **CCP eligibility:** Yes — Sectoral Scope 13
- **MRV:** Material balance (tonnes of e-waste processed); metal recovery rates; displacement factors for virgin production
- **Pricing:** $5–$15/tCO2e (varies by metal prices and recovery efficiency)
- **Complexity:** Medium — requires inbound/outbound material tracking, metal assay documentation, third-party verification
- **Commercial attractiveness:** **MEDIUM-HIGH** for urban mining hub with pre-processing infrastructure co-located with ABC Recycling

### Puro Earth — LFG-to-Energy / Methane Avoidance
- **Status:** Active; Nordic markets leader; expanding to North American projects
- **Scope:** Similar to ACM0001; focus on energy recovery from methane sources
- **Additionality:** Performance standard + financial additionality test
- **Pricing:** $10–$25/tCO2e (premium for Nordic/CDM legacy credits)
- **Complexity:** Medium-High
- **Commercial attractiveness:** **MEDIUM** — fewer North American projects; more relevant for European/Nordic project developers

## Synthesis — Three-Way Integration

### The Cascadia Scale Layer Changes the Investment Thesis

The exploratory package identified two feedstock types (methane + e-waste). The Cascadia documents add a third — and largest — category: **C&D waste from the housing build-out itself.**

The Grand Boulevard strategy (42K acres, 1.4M–4.6M units) and Whatcom MPC ($50B, 25–30 years) transform Breakthrough from a niche methane/e-waste play to a **full-spectrum zero-waste infrastructure operator** covering:
- Methane (Cedarville, WAC 173-408 compliance-driven)
- C&D metals (Grand Boulevard demolition + new construction at scale)
- E-waste (AI server wave 2026–2029)
- Organics (food waste from 20K+ new MPC residents)

### The Whatcom MPC Is the Capital Vehicle

$50B, 25–30 years, developer-led P3 — this structure can absorb Breakthrough as the **circular economy infrastructure operator inside the MPC's industrial zone component** (the 2K-acre industrial park). Rather than competing for grant funding, Breakthrough positions as MPC co-development partner: feedstock aggregator + MRV operator for the MPC's zero-waste targets.

### Cascadia Rail Changes Unit Economics

If Cascadia Rail is built through Ferndale (alongside existing BNSF mainline), it becomes a **freight corridor** for processed materials north to BC buyers and south to Seattle hyperscale campuses. Ferndale becomes the multimodal hub — not just a road/port site.
