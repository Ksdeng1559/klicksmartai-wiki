# Faceless YouTube Channel: "History That Sounds Fake"

**Status:** Validated pilot (Phase 1 TrendMine complete, PASS 82/100). Not yet launched — Shorts-first discovery test recommended before committing long-form budget.

## Whitespace (key finding)
No top-100 faceless YouTube channel owns the specific "sounds fake but documented" hook. Closest: **MrBallen** (mystery/unsettling, 6M+, RPM $5-12) and **Weird History** ("never covered in school") — neither claims the "sounds fake" framing. AI-assisted reconstruction is rare in the history niche → underused differentiator.

## Strategic pivot (critical)
Lead with a **Shorts-first audience-discovery test** before long-form production budget. Shorts RPM low (~$0.05-0.10) but are the discover engine; long-form (~$5-12 RPM) is the revenue engine. Don't sink long-form budget on an untested hook.

## Difference engines (5 content gaps)
1. Sources-cited differentiation (show the doc, PDF on Patreon) → trust + academic/brand sponsors
2. Documentary visual style (AI cinematic reconstruction — rare in niche)
3. Weekly "this day in weird history" recency format → search rediscovery + Shorts tail
4. AP World History curriculum mapping → teacher/parent share
5. Built Shorts→long-form bridge (pinned comment, planned recap)

## Production path
AI-assisted: Higgsfield / Kie.ai / HyperFrames / Remotion. Single creator, ~$500-1,500/mo tool budget. Monetization: AdSense primary; sponsorship + affiliate (history books, museums) + newsletter/Patreon secondary.

## Pilot episode
**"The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower (Twice)"** — Victor Lustig's 1925 con. Light tone, shareable, clean sources.
